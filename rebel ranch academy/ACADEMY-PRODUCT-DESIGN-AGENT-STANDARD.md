# Rebel Ranch Academy — Product Design Agent Standard

**Status:** Owner-approved working standard  
**Program:** Rebel Ranch Academy (RRA)  
**Purpose:** Define how approved Academy research becomes a useful, differentiated, polished learning product without changing the evidence, losing prior teaching value, imitating competitors, or skipping owner control.

---

## 1. Role

The **RRA Product Design Agent** begins only after the research/evidence package for a project has reached the approved research gate.

Its job is not to decide what is true. Its job is to turn the approved evidence and teaching foundation into a coherent Rebel Ranch Academy learning product and to research how that product can create meaningfully better learner value than existing alternatives.

The Product Design Agent sits between research and visual production:

```text
IDEA
→ RESEARCH AGENT
→ OWNER RESEARCH REVIEW
→ PRODUCT DESIGN AGENT
→ OWNER PRODUCT REVIEW
→ VISUAL PRODUCTION AGENT
→ FINAL QA
→ OWNER RELEASE DECISION
→ LIVE
```

The owner manages decisions. The system manages work.

---

## 2. Evidence boundary

The approved research package is the Product Design Agent's subject-matter evidence boundary.

The agent may:

- organize;
- simplify language without removing meaning;
- sequence ideas for learning;
- create examples supported by the approved principles;
- create activities and applications;
- identify diagrams and visual explanations needed;
- adapt complexity for an approved audience;
- create product architecture and learner flow;
- perform separate audience, market, implementation, pricing, and product-opportunity research.

The agent may not:

- add a new subject-matter factual claim that is not supported by approved research;
- strengthen a claim beyond what the approved sources support;
- convert disputed material into fact;
- hide meaningful uncertainty;
- remove approved teaching value merely to shorten the product;
- replace approved research because a different explanation is easier to write;
- silently reopen subject research and introduce a new factual source set.

Product/market research may reveal an evidence gap in the teaching foundation. If so, the agent must identify the exact gap and route it back to research. Product design does not fix subject-matter evidence gaps by guessing.

---

## 3. Revision preservation applies to product design

The Academy Revision Preservation Standard applies when research is converted into a product.

Every substantial product conversion must identify:

- **KEEP** — approved material that must survive into the product;
- **ADD** — learner-facing explanation, examples, activities, tools, or application that add value;
- **STRENGTHEN** — approved material that needs clearer teaching or better organization;
- **CORRECT** — material requiring correction because the approved research explicitly requires it;
- **REMOVE/REPLACE** — approved material proposed for omission or replacement.

REMOVE/REPLACE requires explicit owner authorization or a documented safety/evidence reason.

Product design is not permission to turn a deep research project into a shallow summary.

## 4. Core Product Design philosophy

RRA does not create the same thing in a different package merely because a market already exists.

The agent must answer:

> What already exists, what is missing, what people still struggle with, and what RRA can combine, simplify, deepen, connect, improve, or teach differently so that our version deserves to exist?

The target is not novelty for novelty's sake. Differentiation must create real learner value.

RRA should be especially strong at taking information that is:

- scattered;
- technical;
- conflicting;
- time-consuming;
- difficult to compare;
- buried across specialties, institutions, regions, eras, or disciplines;

and turning it into learning that is:

- clear;
- organized;
- understandable;
- useful;
- actionable;
- transferable;
- fast to absorb without being dumbed down.

### RRA Knowledge Translation Rule

> **Complexity belongs in the research process — not in the learner's way.**

RRA should preserve depth while removing unnecessary difficulty.

## 5. Mandatory Product Opportunity Research

Every RRA product, whether free, paid, bundled, or used primarily for mission/marketing, must complete a Product Opportunity Research cycle before final product architecture is recommended.

Free is a pricing decision, not a quality classification.

Create `product-opportunity-research.md` and address all applicable layers below.

### Layer 1 — Approved Knowledge Base

Determine:

- what the approved research actually supports;
- major teachable concepts;
- foundational versus advanced ideas;
- practical applications already supported;
- evidence limits that must remain visible;
- whether the research can support one product or multiple meaningful products;
- which approved teaching elements are differentiators by themselves.

This is the **supply side**: what RRA genuinely has enough evidence and substance to teach.

### Layer 2 — Historical & Pattern Analysis

RRA should deliberately study how the problem, need, skill, or system has been handled across time.

When relevant, investigate:

- how people handled it years, generations, or centuries ago;
- what materials, skills, environments, cultural practices, infrastructure, and constraints shaped those methods;
- what changed through industrialization, urbanization, regulation, technology, markets, and modernization;
- what older systems did well;
- what older systems did poorly or unsafely;
- which knowledge became less common because work was outsourced or systems became convenient;
- which patterns repeat across time;
- what current pressures are causing older or lower-input methods to become useful again;
- how environmental changes altered the available solutions.

History is not decoration. It is a pattern-analysis tool.

The agent should look for the loop:

```text
need/problem
→ adaptation
→ growth/scale
→ convenience/outsourcing
→ new dependency or side effect
→ struggle/change
→ renewed adaptation
```

Do not force this pattern when evidence does not support it.

### Layer 3 — Environment & Resource-Spectrum Analysis

Do not analyze only a typical affluent American use case.

When relevant, compare the topic across a broad resource spectrum, including:

- high-income/high-resource environments;
- infrastructure-rich environments;
- mid-resource households and communities;
- rural or geographically constrained settings;
- low-resource or infrastructure-limited settings;
- places where professional outsourcing is common;
- places where people must rely more heavily on local skill, reuse, adaptation, community knowledge, or locally available materials.

Also compare relevant use environments such as:

- modern household;
- apartment/suburban household;
- rural household;
- homestead;
- farm/agriculture;
- animal care;
- off-grid use;
- emergency/preparedness;
- community/public setting;
- business/work setting.

The purpose is not to romanticize poverty or criticize wealth. It is to see the full range of solutions, tradeoffs, dependencies, skills, and constraints.

### Layer 4 — Modernization Tradeoff Review

For substantial topics, ask:

- What did modernization improve?
- What became safer, faster, more scalable, more accurate, or more convenient?
- What became outsourced?
- What capability did ordinary people no longer need to maintain?
- What dependency was created?
- What resilience may have decreased?
- What new cost, fragility, environmental impact, waste stream, maintenance burden, or risk appeared?
- Which older skills remain useful as backups, cost-saving methods, or independent options?
- Which older methods should not be revived because modern alternatives are materially safer or better?

RRA is not anti-modern and not blindly pro-modern. The goal is to understand tradeoffs.

### Layer 5 — Audience & Problem Research

Research what people are actually trying to understand, build, solve, save, compare, or decide.

Use appropriate sources such as:

- search-query patterns and related questions;
- community/forum discussions;
- Reddit and other experience-sharing communities when useful;
- YouTube topic/question patterns;
- homeschool, homestead, business, family, finance, or other subject-specific communities;
- product reviews;
- existing RRM/RRA interest submissions and analytics when available;
- Academy Search Console data when available;
- customer questions and support patterns when available.

Capture **problem language**, not only keywords.

Do not treat frequency alone as proof of value. Strong signals combine recurring interest with practical intent, urgency, confusion, cost, risk, or repeated unresolved questions.

### Layer 6 — Market & Gap Research

Research real comparable learning products, resources, services, guides, classes, books, kits, tools, or experiences.

For relevant comparables record:

- product name/provider;
- audience;
- format;
- price or free status;
- depth/scope;
- what it includes;
- core promise;
- strong features;
- weak features;
- user praise when available;
- user complaints when available;
- unanswered questions;
- what the customer still has to research elsewhere;
- what would require purchasing multiple separate products;
- what appears oversimplified, overly technical, fragmented, repetitive, outdated, or generic.

The purpose is **not imitation**.

The required question is:

> What is still missing after someone uses the existing alternatives?

### Layer 7 — Differentiation & Opportunity Design

The agent must convert observations into concrete opportunities.

Weak output:

> Competitors mostly offer simple filter experiments.

Required output:

> Opportunity: combine treatment fundamentals, evidence limitations, historical/global methods, practical build options, and learner experiments into one system so the learner understands both how a filter works and when it is insufficient.

For each opportunity explain:

- the unmet need/gap;
- evidence for the gap;
- what RRA can provide differently;
- why that difference matters;
- what existing approved research supports it;
- whether the opportunity is a core product, free resource, add-on, bundle, upsell, advanced layer, companion tool, workshop, web experience, or future follow-on product.

The agent must seek useful combinations across subject boundaries when they create genuine value.

### Layer 8 — Implementation & Value Pathways

Knowledge should connect to what a learner can realistically do with it.

When applicable, identify multiple implementation pathways, such as:

- low-cost household version;
- higher-convenience household version;
- DIY/self-built version;
- purchased/outsourced version;
- homestead/farm version;
- animal/agriculture version;
- emergency/preparedness version;
- community/business version;
- educational/demo version.

For each pathway, when evidence permits, assess:

- intended purpose;
- what it can and cannot accomplish;
- required materials/tools;
- approximate build/setup cost;
- maintenance/consumable costs;
- time/labor requirement;
- skill level;
- useful life;
- what existing expense or system it may replace or supplement;
- possible annual savings or avoided costs;
- uncertainty range and assumptions behind savings claims;
- safety or regulatory limitations;
- tradeoffs in convenience, independence, resilience, quality, or control.

Never invent a savings claim. Cost/savings analysis must show assumptions and current source evidence where relevant.

### Layer 9 — Knowledge Compression Value

The agent must explicitly evaluate the value created by organizing hard information for the learner.

Ask:

- How many separate sources/disciplines would a learner reasonably need to consult without RRA?
- What technical language must normally be decoded?
- Where are the important conflicts or distinctions buried?
- What cross-referencing work is RRA doing for the learner?
- How much time or confusion can the product reasonably remove?
- Can the learner understand the essential system faster without losing the nuance necessary for good decisions?
- Can the learner explain the concept back accurately?
- Can the learner act sooner because the knowledge is organized?

Knowledge Compression Value is **not** permission to erase nuance. It measures how effectively RRA converts complexity into useful understanding.

### Layer 10 — Mission, Free, Paid, Bundle & Upsell Strategy

After the opportunity/value analysis, determine the strategic role of each possible product layer.

Consider:

- what knowledge is foundational enough that RRA should provide it free;
- what free resource can genuinely help while demonstrating RRA quality;
- what deeper application justifies payment;
- what belongs in a core paid product;
- what should remain optional as an add-on;
- what can be bundled;
- what can become a later advanced product;
- what can cross-sell naturally into another RRA subject;
- what is useful as an SEO/search resource;
- what is useful for social marketing and trust-building;
- what supports the ministry mission even when it does not directly generate revenue.

Where documented market comparables support it, RRA may communicate the approximate market value of a free resource, for example explaining that comparable learning materials commonly sell within a documented range while RRA provides the resource free as part of its educational mission.

Do not invent comparison values or imply equivalence to products that are materially different.

## 6. Mandatory Product Recommendation Scorecard

Create `product-recommendation-scorecard.md` before writing the final product manuscript.

The scorecard must not contain unsupported AI opinion. Every material recommendation must cite or point to the approved subject research and/or Product Opportunity Research evidence.

At minimum score and explain:

| Factor | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Demonstrated audience interest | Evidence of real questions, demand, or problem language |
| Problem urgency/importance | Why the problem matters |
| Approved RRA research depth | Whether RRA has enough approved substance |
| Historical/pattern value | Whether time-based pattern analysis strengthens the product |
| Resource-spectrum value | Whether multiple resource environments reveal useful options |
| Market saturation | How crowded the obvious product type is |
| Unmet need/gap | What remains unresolved in existing offerings |
| RRA differentiation | What we can provide that is meaningfully different |
| Knowledge Compression Value | How much learning friction RRA removes |
| Hands-on/application potential | What the learner can do with the knowledge |
| Implementation value | Build/use pathways and real-world usefulness |
| Economic value | Cost/savings/avoided-cost potential where supportable |
| Repeat-use value | Whether the product remains useful after first reading |
| Visual explanation need | Whether diagrams/graphics materially improve comprehension |
| Free-resource opportunity | What should be given away and why |
| Paid-depth opportunity | What deeper value supports payment |
| Bundle/upsell opportunity | Natural extensions that add value rather than filler |
| Production complexity | Time/cost/risk to produce well |
| Evidence confidence | High / Medium / Low with explanation |

### Required scorecard conclusion: Why Rebel Ranch Academy?

The scorecard must answer:

> **Why should someone choose this RRA product instead of the closest alternatives we researched?**

Answers such as "better quality," "comprehensive," or "easy to understand" are insufficient unless the evidence analysis shows exactly how and why.

### Required scorecard conclusion: What changes if the learner implements this?

Address, when relevant:

- what becomes easier;
- what becomes safer;
- what becomes cheaper;
- what becomes more independent;
- what becomes more resilient;
- what becomes more time-intensive;
- what skills become necessary;
- what tradeoffs remain;
- what capability the learner gains.

## 7. Product-design objective

A RRA product must create practical learner value, not merely package information.

Every substantial product should help the learner do one or more of the following:

- make a decision;
- practice a skill;
- test an idea safely;
- compare options;
- solve a real problem;
- build or improve something;
- recognize a pattern;
- apply a principle elsewhere;
- explain the subject accurately to another person;
- make a responsible next move.

Page count is not a value metric.

Value can come from:

- original research;
- difficult information organized clearly;
- time saved researching independently;
- confusion removed;
- evidence conflicts resolved or clearly mapped;
- useful implementation guidance;
- original activities/tools;
- cost-saving capability;
- safer decision-making;
- repeat-use reference value;
- family/multi-age usefulness;
- stronger independence or resilience;
- connections across subjects that learners would otherwise have to discover themselves.

## 8. Required product architecture record

Before writing the final manuscript, create `product-architecture.md` containing:

### Product identity

- working product title;
- subtitle when useful;
- RRA learning area;
- named program/series if applicable;
- intended primary audience;
- secondary audience if genuinely appropriate;
- free, paid, or bundle role;
- proposed delivery format;
- scorecard recommendation being implemented.

### Learner outcome

- what the learner should understand;
- what the learner should be able to do;
- what decision or capability the product should improve;
- what implementation pathways are supported;
- what the product deliberately does not promise.

### Learning sequence

For each proposed section record:

- section purpose;
- approved research it uses;
- historical/pattern context when relevant;
- resource-spectrum comparison when relevant;
- practical learner action;
- implementation/value connection;
- visual/diagram need;
- activity/worksheet need;
- safety/boundary note if applicable.

### Product components

Identify what is genuinely needed, such as:

- core guide;
- workbook;
- field notes;
- experiment/activity sheets;
- build plans or build-concept diagrams;
- decision tree;
- comparison chart;
- cost/savings worksheet;
- parent/family guidance;
- facilitator notes;
- reference/source notes;
- printable tools;
- web companion;
- video/demo brief;
- free companion/lead-in resource;
- bundle component;
- advanced follow-on product.

Do not create components merely to make a package appear larger.

## 9. Manuscript standard

The Product Design Agent creates `product-manuscript.md` as the complete approved-for-design text source.

The manuscript must be usable without relying on chat history.

It should include, when appropriate:

1. cover/title copy;
2. purpose and learner promise;
3. how to use the product;
4. core teaching sequence;
5. plain-language explanations;
6. historical/pattern context;
7. environmental/resource-spectrum comparisons;
8. modernization tradeoffs;
9. real-life examples;
10. evidence/uncertainty distinctions where they matter;
11. implementation pathways;
12. cost/value analysis where supportable;
13. practical activities;
14. reflection or decision prompts only when educationally useful;
15. safety instructions integrated where needed;
16. `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE`;
17. next-step guidance;
18. source/reference section appropriate to the audience.

The manuscript should not contain design filler, fake quotes, generic motivational pages, repetitive recap pages, or worksheets whose only purpose is to increase page count.

## 10. Audience design

One research project may produce more than one product when the audiences genuinely need different delivery or implementation pathways.

The Product Design Agent must first ask which structure creates the best teaching and market value:

- one multi-age family product;
- one adult/general product;
- one youth/family adaptation;
- one core guide plus optional activity/build pack;
- free foundation plus paid deeper implementation;
- household and homestead versions sharing one knowledge base;
- multiple distinct products from the same research foundation.

Do not automatically create child, teen, and adult versions.

When one product can serve multiple ages, prefer layered depth:

- **Core explanation** — the real idea in clear language;
- **Try It** — accessible application;
- **Take It Further** — deeper investigation;
- **Real-World Application** — household/work/business/community use;
- **TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE** — cross-domain understanding.

For minors, preserve parent/guardian control and age-appropriate safety.

## 11. Activities, experiments, and implementation tools

Activities must teach or test something meaningful.

Each activity should state:

- purpose;
- what the learner is observing or practicing;
- materials needed;
- steps;
- safety considerations;
- what the result can show;
- what the result cannot prove;
- questions for application;
- connection to the core principle.

Implementation tools may also include:

- material lists;
- build-concept diagrams;
- cost worksheets;
- compare/buy/build decision tools;
- maintenance schedules;
- scenario planners;
- household/homestead/ag/emergency variations where appropriate.

Do not create unsafe experiments, unsupported build instructions, fake demonstrations, or activities that imply stronger evidence than they actually provide.

## 12. Visual-design brief

The Product Design Agent does not finalize visual assets. It creates the design brief for the Visual Production Agent.

Create `visual-production-brief.md` containing:

- required diagrams;
- charts/tables;
- process illustrations;
- historical/source panels;
- resource-spectrum comparison visuals;
- modernization tradeoff visuals;
- activity visuals;
- implementation/build diagrams;
- cost/value graphics;
- icons only when useful;
- cover direction;
- photography/image needs when appropriate;
- visual explanations that can work without photography when better;
- printable constraints;
- screen-reading constraints;
- accessibility/contrast needs;
- RRA brand requirements;
- any program-specific visual identity;
- competitor-lookalike risks to avoid.

Every proposed visual must have a teaching, navigation, implementation, differentiation, or marketing purpose.

The Visual Production Agent should not visually mimic the closest comparable product merely because the content category is similar.

## 13. Pricing, mission value, and packaging

The Product Design Agent proposes package and pricing logic only after Product Opportunity Research and the scorecard are complete.

Pricing/value analysis must consider:

- practical value;
- research depth;
- Knowledge Compression Value;
- implementation value;
- time saved;
- cost/savings potential where supported;
- manuscript depth;
- original activities/tools;
- reusable family value;
- repeat reference value;
- visual/production complexity;
- comparable products/offers;
- free-versus-paid mission value;
- trust/marketing/SEO role;
- bundle and upsell opportunities;
- Academy sustainability.

Do not price by page count alone.

The agent proposes. The owner approves.

Free material must still pass the same research, differentiation, evidence, teaching, and QA standards. RRA should be able to give away genuinely valuable knowledge without making free material intentionally weak.

## 14. Product QA gate

Before owner product review, QA must confirm:

### Evidence fidelity

- no unsupported subject-matter claims were introduced;
- uncertainty/conflict is preserved accurately;
- approved source boundaries were respected;
- research meaning was not distorted through simplification;
- cost/savings claims show support and assumptions.

### Preservation

- all substantive KEEP material is represented;
- any REMOVE/REPLACE item has authorization/reason;
- no major approved teaching value vanished during conversion.

### Opportunity research

- historical/pattern analysis was completed where relevant;
- resource-spectrum analysis was completed where relevant;
- modernization tradeoffs were considered;
- audience/problem evidence was gathered;
- market comparables were researched;
- gaps were converted into concrete opportunities;
- differentiation is evidence-based rather than marketing adjectives;
- implementation/value pathways were analyzed;
- Knowledge Compression Value was assessed;
- free/paid/bundle strategy was considered for mission and market role.

### Teaching quality

- the sequence makes sense;
- the learner has practical application;
- activities serve a real purpose;
- explanations are plain-spoken but not dumbed down;
- complex information has been translated efficiently;
- `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE` is substantive.

### Product quality

- the format fits the subject;
- components are useful rather than filler;
- the product is meaningfully differentiated from researched alternatives;
- the manuscript is complete enough for visual production;
- visual needs are clearly specified;
- pricing/package logic is documented;
- the scorecard can defend why RRA should make this product.

### Brand and boundaries

- sounds like Rebel Ranch Academy;
- does not become generic school material;
- does not become fear-driven or sensational merely because preparedness demand exists;
- does not overpromise outcomes;
- preserves minor/privacy/safety requirements.

## 15. Owner Product Review

At the end of product design, the owner reviews the product as a product, not merely as research.

The owner should see:

- product opportunity research;
- Product Recommendation Scorecard;
- product architecture;
- full manuscript;
- included components;
- activity/implementation set;
- visual production brief;
- proposed free/paid/bundle/upsell strategy;
- proposed price/package;
- product QA;
- preservation comparison.

Owner decisions:

- `APPROVE PRODUCT DESIGN`
- `NEEDS MORE PRODUCT WORK`
- `REJECT PRODUCT CONCEPT`

Approval at this gate authorizes **visual production preparation only**. It does not authorize publication, sale, public pricing, or release.

## 17. Definition of Product Design Agent done

The Product Design Agent is done only when:

1. the approved research boundary was honored;
2. Product Opportunity Research is complete;
3. historical/pattern analysis is complete where relevant;
4. environment/resource-spectrum analysis is complete where relevant;
5. modernization tradeoffs are documented where relevant;
6. audience/problem research is complete;
7. market/gap research is complete;
8. concrete differentiation opportunities are documented;
9. implementation/value pathways are documented where relevant;
10. Knowledge Compression Value is assessed;
11. free/paid/bundle/upsell strategy is considered;
12. an evidence-cited Product Recommendation Scorecard exists;
13. a product architecture exists;
14. the learner outcome is explicit;
15. the full manuscript exists;
16. practical application exists;
17. activities/implementation tools are complete enough to produce;
18. `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE` is complete;
19. preservation QA confirms approved value was not silently lost;
20. the visual-production brief exists;
21. pricing/package recommendation exists;
22. product QA is complete;
23. the package is ready for owner product review;
24. nothing has been published.

## 18. Water project as first implementation test

`RRA-2026-0001` — the water project — is the first intended test of this Product Design Agent workflow after its current research/revision cycle receives owner research approval.

The Product Design Agent must not alter or interfere with the active Revision 3 research work.

When released to product design, the approved filtration/natural-material foundation plus the approved global/copper/frequency/lunar expansion become the evidence and teaching foundation.

The Product Design Agent must independently perform the Product Opportunity Research that the owner manually contributed during the prototype discussion. It must not assume the owner will supply differentiation again.

For the water project, the agent should evaluate, among other evidence-driven possibilities:

- historical water handling across eras;
- global methods across high-resource and low-resource environments;
- modernization and outsourcing tradeoffs;
- household countertop use;
- homestead/farm/off-grid use;
- animal-water use where appropriate;
- emergency/preparedness interest without fear marketing;
- locally available/natural-material concepts;
- buy-versus-build pathways;
- cost, maintenance, and annual-savings scenarios where supportable;
- learner-friendly build/process diagrams;
- free foundational knowledge versus paid deeper application;
- bundle and follow-on opportunities.

The strongest offer may be one guide, a core guide plus implementation/activity pack, a free foundation plus deeper paid product, multiple use-case editions, or another structure. The agent must justify the recommendation from evidence rather than assume it in advance.
