## Known uncertainty and safety boundaries

- Clear water is not automatically potable water.
- A demonstration filter is not automatically a drinking-water treatment system.
- Treatment selection must match the actual contaminant/problem.
- Traditional use documents practice; it does not by itself prove efficacy or universal safety.
- Copper findings must remain tied to tested conditions and must not become unsupported health claims.
- Moringa can perform particular treatment functions under particular conditions; it is not a universal purifier.
- Acoustic/frequency effects must not be converted into unsupported claims about permanent beneficial water restructuring or human health.
- Lunar-human evidence is mixed and must remain presented as mixed.
- DIY implementation concepts involving human drinking water must clearly distinguish educational demonstration, pre-treatment, filtration, disinfection, contaminant removal, testing and verified potable-water treatment.
- Economic/savings claims require current evidence, explicit assumptions and scenario-specific calculations; do not promise savings or cost recovery.

## Mandatory product questions to solve

The Product Design Agent must independently research and answer, with evidence:

1. **What already exists?** Identify real free and paid comparable offerings, their formats, prices, strengths, weaknesses, reviews/questions and unresolved gaps.
2. **What is missing?** Find what users still have to research elsewhere, what existing products oversimplify, what they fail to connect, and what people continue asking after consuming them.
3. **Why should RRA's version exist?** Produce concrete differentiation rather than repackaging the same product.
4. **How has the problem been handled across time?** Follow water handling across years/centuries, environmental change, industrialization and modernization; identify repeating patterns, gains, losses and retained useful knowledge.
5. **How does the answer change across the resource spectrum?** Compare infrastructure-rich/high-income environments through rural, constrained, low-resource and infrastructure-limited environments, and explain why different solutions emerge.
6. **What did modernization trade?** Examine comfort, safety, scale and convenience alongside outsourcing, dependency, lost capability, cost, resilience and new problems.
7. **What can the learner actually implement?** Investigate relevant household, DIY countertop, purchased/outsourced, homestead, 55-gallon/outdoor, animal/ag, off-grid and emergency/preparedness pathways. Separate potable-human-water requirements from animal/ag and demonstration uses.
8. **What is the economic value?** Where supportable, compare setup cost, consumables, maintenance, labor, useful life, bought-versus-built alternatives and possible annual savings/avoided costs. Show assumptions and uncertainty; never invent a savings claim.
9. **How can the knowledge become visually understandable?** Recommend diagrams/process graphics/build schematics that can explain construction and system logic without depending on photographs.
10. **What is the Knowledge Compression Value?** Estimate what scattered technical reading, terminology, cross-referencing and confusion RRA can responsibly compress into a much faster learning path without losing important nuance.
11. **What should be free, paid, bundled, upgraded or used as a follow-on?** Free material receives the same research and quality rigor as paid material. Free is a pricing decision, not a quality classification.
12. **What changes if the learner implements this?** Explain gains and tradeoffs in cost, time, capability, independence, resilience, convenience, quality, control and safety.
13. **Why Rebel Ranch Academy?** Answer from documented evidence and differentiation, not generic claims such as “comprehensive” or “easy to understand.”

## Product-design philosophy

**Complexity belongs in the research process, not in the learner's way.**

RRA should do the digging, cross-referencing, verification and systems thinking required to convert scattered, technical, conflicting and time-consuming information into learning that is clear, organized, understandable, actionable and transferable—without stripping away the nuance that makes the knowledge accurate.
