## Relationship to Rebel Ranch Ministries

Business Freedom is a Rebel Ranch Ministries program. Public pages use the repository's official shared RRM header and footer without restyling, replacing, or duplicating them. The Business Freedom identity begins inside the page body.

The shared shell currently comes from:

- `assets/css/public-surface.css`

## Brand character

Business Freedom should feel capable, direct, modern, practical, and human. It helps independent owners see a business problem clearly and take a useful next step without making them feel behind, foolish, or overwhelmed.

The visual system should communicate movement from confusion to clarity. It may use strong contrast and energetic accents, but it should not feel like generic corporate software or a loud internet-marketing funnel.

## Palette: Slate Ocean

| Role | Color | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep foundation | `#102635` | Hero, high-contrast sections, closing sections |
| Primary ocean | `#173244` | Main page background |
| Panel | `#21475A` | Interactive controls and contained explanations |
| Raised panel | `#28566B` | Selected or emphasized surfaces |
| Primary text | `#FFFFFF` | Headlines and essential copy |
| Secondary text | `#D7E4EC` | Supporting copy |
| Active cyan | `#19BFE6` | Current selection, digital connection, key phrase |
| Informational sky | `#65C7E8` | Icons, secondary accents, supporting sections |
| Connected seafoam | `#43D6B5` | Positive movement and connected outcomes |
| Missed-connection coral | `#FF5263` | Problems, friction, and warnings |
| Paid-action orange | `#FF6500` | Primary paid call to action only |

### Color boundaries

- Do not use cream or beige as a Business Freedom page background.
- Orange identifies the primary paid action. Do not distribute it across decoration, secondary links, icons, or free-program actions.
- Coral describes friction or a missed connection; it is not a general decorative accent.
- Seafoam describes progress or a connected outcome.
- Cyan communicates an active choice or a digital connection.
- Keep body copy at accessible contrast on dark surfaces.

## Typography and hierarchy

- Use a clean, sturdy sans-serif stack that performs reliably on phones.
- Headlines are compact, high contrast, and written in plain language.
- Use short eyebrow labels to orient the reader, not to add slogans to every block.
- Keep paragraphs brief. When an explanation is necessary, use progressive disclosure, a visual sequence, or a before/after comparison.
- Price and primary action should be visible in the opening screen and repeated in the close.

## Photography

- Lead with authentic people in recognizable customer and owner situations.
- Show capable owners doing real work and customers trying to discover, understand, contact, or choose them.
- Prefer contemporary small-town settings without turning rural life into a costume or stereotype.
- Use natural light and warm human tones against the cool Slate Ocean palette.
- Compose mobile images intentionally; do not rely on a desktop crop when it removes the story.
- Do not use staged handshakes, old technology as shorthand for small business, generic laptop meetings, fake dashboards, platform logos, invented statistics, or invented testimonials.
- Do not show men wearing aprons. Use clothing appropriate to their actual work, such as work shirts, polos, T-shirts, coveralls, uniforms, or ordinary business-casual clothing.

## Interaction

- Interactive elements must earn their space by helping the customer understand the problem or decision.
- Every icon control needs a plain-language label; icons never carry the meaning alone.
- On mobile, keep tap targets generous and reveal one concise explanation at a time.
- Show a clear selected state and support keyboard focus and reduced-motion preferences.
- Avoid carousels, modals, tiny labels, decorative pills, and large walls of interchangeable cards.

## Sales-page sequence

The exact layout changes with the service. The reusable persuasion logic is:

1. Catch attention visually and state the recognizable problem.
2. Connect that problem to a real customer behavior or business consequence.
3. Explain the complexity with the most suitable visual device for that service.
4. Show that the paid help is practical and fitted to the individual business.
5. Place relevant mission or free-program connections in a supporting role with clear boundaries.
6. Close with the paid outcome, starting price, and one unmistakable action.

This is a decision framework, not a page template. A future service may need a different section count, visual rhythm, interaction, or storytelling device.

## Calls to action

- The primary paid call to action uses vivid orange, high-contrast white text, and direct outcome language.
- Secondary links remain visually quieter.
- A free program may support the story but must not compete with the paid service on a paid landing page.
- Pricing must distinguish a starting price, flat price, and recurring price accurately. Never imply a monthly charge when none exists.
