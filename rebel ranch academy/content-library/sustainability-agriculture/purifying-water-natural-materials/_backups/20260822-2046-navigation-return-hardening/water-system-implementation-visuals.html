7. Function Before Form / Use What You Have

Name the job first. Then compare forms that can responsibly perform it.

STOREJug · bucket · drum · tank. Suitability still depends on material, history, cleaning and intended use.
MOVEHose · tubing · pipe. Diameter, material, pressure and sanitation change what works.
CREATE FLOW / PRESSUREGravity · pump. The same function can be achieved differently, but the constraints change.
CONTROLValve · float · timer · sensor. Control reduces waste and labor only when failure modes are understood.
MICROBIAL CONTROLValidated UV · boiling · chlorine-based disinfection · solar disinfection under defined conditions. They are not universal substitutes.
VERIFYTesting · inspection · measurement · maintenance record. A system is not finished until you know how you will verify it.
USE WHAT YOU HAVE before deciding what you need to buy — unless substitution would weaken safety or performance.

8. Scale Changes the Engineering

The function may stay the same while weight, head, support, fittings and maintenance change.

1 GALLON
portable · light · easy to inspect
5 GALLONS
heavier · handle/support matters
55 GALLONS
~459 lb of water · stable support · fittings/overflow
LARGER TANK
site/load · plumbing · access · cleaning · overflow · control
Scaling up is not “use a bigger container.” Recheck weight, support, pressure/head, cleaning, overflow, material suitability and failure consequences.

10. Pond / Surface Water Emergency Caution

Surface water can carry multiple hazard classes. Solving one does not prove you solved the others.

VISIBLE DEBRIS / PARTICLES
MICROORGANISMS
UNKNOWN CHEMICALS / TOXINS
INTENDED USE
TEST / VERIFY
Particle filtration + UV, boiling, chemical disinfection or solar disinfection does NOT automatically resolve unknown chemical/toxin contamination. High-stakes potable use requires a source-appropriate evidence and verification path.

11. Evidence / Claim Ladder

Do not jump rungs. Match the strength of the conclusion to the strength and type of evidence.

12. Water Through Time & Place

Methods changed because conditions changed. Historical use is evidence of practice—not automatic proof that old is better.

SOURCERiver, spring, well, rain, stored supply.
AVAILABLE ENERGYSun, fire, gravity, hand labor, later electricity.
LOCAL MATERIALSCloth, sand, vessels, plant materials, metals, later manufactured media.
KNOWLEDGE + INFRASTRUCTUREWhat people understood, could test, could build and could maintain shaped the method.
Study older methods for function, constraints and ingenuity. Keep what evidence supports; correct what newer evidence shows was incomplete or unsafe.
