## Source-use notes

1. No source is accepted merely because of institutional prestige.
2. Current EPA/CDC material is used primarily to document current safety guidance and treatment distinctions, not as an unquestioned statement of universal truth.
3. WHO is used for performance-evaluation logic and independent technology testing criteria, not because institutional authority alone proves a homemade filter works.
4. Historical USDA material is used to document what was actually taught historically. Old chemical dosages or procedures must not be copied into modern recommendations without current verification.
5. The product should repeatedly distinguish **ordinary charcoal** from **activated carbon**. They are not interchangeable terms.
6. Original peer-reviewed studies are included to test and corroborate broad slow-sand/biosand claims. Their results belong to the specific designs and conditions studied and must not be converted into promises for a learner-built demonstration filter.
7. The evidence supports the project’s central lesson: water treatment is contaminant-specific and system-specific. No single material or institutional statement replaces testing, design, operation, maintenance and verification.
