## Comparable alternatives reviewed
Introductory emotional-intelligence explainer content is widely available for free from sources like Verywell Mind, Psychology Today, and various corporate training blogs, but it is typically shallow (a list of "5 signs of high EQ"), does not disclose the real academic disagreement about the construct, and does not cite a specific tested regulation technique. Paid EQ courses and assessments (e.g., commercial EQ-i or MSCEIT-adjacent training products) generally run well into the "Core resource" to "Guided/advanced" range ($19–$150+), but those are typically full assessment/certification products, not a single introductory lesson — not a fair comparison for this project's scope.

## Why this material is more, less, or similarly valuable
More valuable than typical free EQ content because it: (1) cites real, checkable primary and near-primary sources instead of unsourced claims, (2) is honest about the field's actual disagreement instead of presenting EI as settled science, and (3) grounds the practical activity in a specific studied technique (reappraisal) rather than generic advice. Not comparable in scope to paid multi-module courses or licensed assessment tools — this is a single foundational lesson, not a program.

## Free-versus-paid analysis
This is a **foundational, single-topic, introductory** resource. Per the Free Material Standard, free material should be "useful enough that a learner can gain something real" and should build trust and introduce a deeper subject — exactly this project's role. It is not artificially incomplete (no held-back "buy the rest" hook); a learner who only ever reads this one page gets the complete, honest picture.
