## Research-agent source judgment

The owner asked specifically for recent demonstrations of social influence and for evidence strong enough to show that people can yield to a group even when reality is checkable.

The **Asch experiment remains the closest direct match to the factual-override question** because it used an objectively checkable visual task. The newer studies in this addendum are deliberately not misrepresented as replications of that exact fact-vs-majority setup. They show that peers, friends, parents, expected group behavior and cultural norm interpretation can materially alter preferences, decisions, attitudes and behavioral intentions.

The combined evidence supports teaching social influence as a real decision mechanism while preserving these boundaries:
- conformity is not universal;
- social influence may be constructive or destructive;
- public conformity is not identical to private belief change;
- majority agreement is not evidence of truth or falsity by itself;
- cultural effects are contextual, not permission for stereotyping.

**Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.**
