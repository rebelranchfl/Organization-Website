## Required KEEP material

Product Design must preserve the approved function-first distinctions:

1. **Requirements follow the job, not the container label.**
2. Potable household water, emergency reserves, livestock water, ordinary irrigation and higher-risk produce-contact agricultural water are materially different storage jobs and must not be collapsed into one standard.
3. Potable safe-storage functions include suitable prior use, cleanability, protected closure/opening, sanitary dispensing, cleaning/maintenance and protection against recontamination.
4. Do not repurpose a container that previously held toxic chemicals, petroleum products or pesticides for potable-water storage.
5. Emergency storage capacity, rotation and handling are separate decisions from everyday potable storage.
6. Livestock storage must account for contamination, algae, manure/organic loading, access and cleaning rather than being treated as a human-potable standard by default.
7. Agricultural-water requirements vary by actual use, especially where water contacts edible produce or postharvest surfaces.
8. Storage does not preserve water quality automatically; recontamination and microbial regrowth can occur after source/treatment steps.
9. Historical/global container practices may be taught as evidence-backed function/pattern material without romanticizing every traditional vessel or assuming age proves safety.
10. Testing verifies/refines a decision where conditions require it; it does not block teaching general storage principles.
11. `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE` and Function Before Form remain part of the product logic.
12. Existing research supports tangible outputs such as a **Container Suitability Decision Tree, Storage-by-Function Branch Map, Container Anatomy visual, Capacity Planner, Use What You Have Audit, maintenance system and failure-mode exercise**.

No broad REMOVE/REPLACE action is authorized by this approval.

## Product Opportunity Research questions

Before choosing a product format, Product Design must determine with evidence:

- Which storage decisions people actually get wrong, overcomplicate or repeatedly research?
- Which audiences have the strongest use case: families/emergency storage, homesteads, livestock owners, growers, preparedness learners or a multi-branch tool?
- What comparable storage guides/tools exist and what function/use distinctions do they miss?
- Can one interactive selector responsibly branch by intended use, prior container history, material, capacity, dispensing method, location/exposure and maintenance burden?
- What container anatomy/cutaway visuals would let a learner inspect openings, lids, vents, outlets, cleaning access, support, overflow and contamination pathways?
- What should the learner be able to inspect or calculate with a **Use What You Have Audit** before buying another container?
- Which components/forms are `USE WHAT YOU HAVE`, `REUSE / REPURPOSE`, `SOURCE LOCALLY`, `WORTH BUYING`, `OPTIONAL UPGRADE` or `DO NOT SUBSTITUTE`?
- What repeat-use output would materially reduce research burden and unsafe guessing?
- What should be free, paid, bundled or a follow-on resource?
- Why Rebel Ranch Academy, specifically, rather than a generic emergency-water checklist?
- What changes if the learner implements the product correctly?

## Product Design boundaries

- Do not imply that any food-grade-looking barrel/tote/jug is automatically suitable for every use.
- Do not equate “container” with potable-water suitability.
- Do not make specific material-migration or species/crop thresholds without adequate evidence.
- Do not use fear-based preparedness framing.
- Do not turn this into a shopping catalog; teach function, inspection and suitability first.
- Practical visuals and learner decisions should lead. Text should support the decisions rather than become the product itself.
