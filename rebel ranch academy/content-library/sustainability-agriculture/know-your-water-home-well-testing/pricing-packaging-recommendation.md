# RRA-2026-0002 — Pricing & Packaging Recommendation

**Status:** Product Design recommendation only — not owner-approved or public  
**Date:** 2026-08-23

## Recommended package

### Free mission layer
**What Should I Test — and Why?**

Includes:
- source/use/event/local-risk intake;
- multi-select intended uses;
- routine vs event-triggered distinction;
- first Testing Question Map;
- evidence-route guidance;
- clear boundary between what can be prepared now and what should wait for a result.

This layer should remain genuinely useful because basic well-testing education is already available free through public-health and well-owner resources. RRA should not charge merely to restate that material.

### Paid core
**My Well Test Plan**

Recommended one-time price for owner review: **$29**.

Includes the persistent interactive work that creates repeat-use value:
- living multi-branch household plan;
- Test Route Comparator;
- provider/sample organizer;
- Result Context Records;
- result-to-next-job routing;
- confirmation/retest logic;
- recurring/event calendar and testing history;
- printable/exportable household record;
- optional evidence/technical depth without losing state.

## Paid-value test

**What work does RRA perform that the learner would otherwise have to research, compare, design, organize or decide?**

RRA:
1. converts source + simultaneous intended uses + events + local-risk clues into a structured testing-question map;
2. keeps routine and event-triggered plans together without confusing them;
3. compares evidence routes by function instead of advertising;
4. preserves provider/sample requirements so the learner does not rebuild the process from memory;
5. keeps result value, units, sample point and benchmark/source together;
6. routes confirmed results to the next job instead of immediately selling treatment;
7. maintains history and due actions for future routine/event decisions;
8. creates a portable household record the learner can use with a laboratory, local resource or later treatment-planning process.

The product therefore sells **decision support, planning, organization and repeat-use capability**, not more words to read.

## Why $29 is the current recommendation

Current Product Opportunity Research shows:
- Florida describes routine local testing as commonly around $20–$30 per sample;
- nearby Alachua currently lists microbiological analysis at $25 per bottle;
- commercial mail-to-lab well panels can cost hundreds of dollars depending on scope;
- substantial free well-owner education already exists.

Those prices do not directly determine the educational product's price. They show the economic consequence of poor test selection and the value of making better route decisions before spending.

$29 is recommended because it is:
- low enough to remain accessible relative to the cost of repeated testing/panel purchases;
- high enough to require a real interactive system rather than a static PDF;
- aligned with the product's repeat-use household-record value;
- intentionally below the parent Water system's proposed $39 direction because this product owns a narrower diagnostic/testing job.

## Price-kill condition

The $29 recommendation **fails** if Visual Production delivers mostly:
- text cards;
- a static checklist;
- generic explanations;
- disconnected printables;
- a plan that does not materially change with learner inputs;
- depth navigation that loses state.

In that case the product must be repriced downward or moved to free rather than pretending static content has the same value as the approved architecture.

## Bundle direction

Potential later bundle, subject to separate owner approval:
- My Well Test Plan + parent Water treatment-planning product.

The testing product should hand the parent Water product a defined treatment/source-correction job; the parent product handles broader treatment-system architecture. The products should connect without duplicating one another.

No bundle price is recommended in this cycle because the parent product's final package/release status is not yet approved.

## Ethical commerce boundary

The product may later link to verified testing resources or services when educationally justified, but education and diagnosis come first:

`QUESTION → REQUIRED EVIDENCE FUNCTION → OPTIONS → BUY ONLY WHERE JUSTIFIED`

Do not turn the product into a test-kit catalog or rank vendors without a separately supported comparison.

## Owner decision boundary

Owner Product Review may approve the product design while changing or withholding the proposed price. Product approval authorizes Visual Production only. Public pricing remains a later release decision.
