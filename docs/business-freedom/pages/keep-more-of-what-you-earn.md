## North star

- Audience: sole proprietors and small businesses selling services or goods, especially rural and blue-collar North Florida businesses.
- Problem: they do quality work and revenue enters the business, yet too little remains because the operation has many connected places where money can be lost.
- Core story: `1. Do quality work. 2. Money comes in. 3. Too little stays.`
- Value: reveal how the whole operation moves money, find root causes across interconnected systems and processes, then create a customized path that helps the business retain more of what it earns.
- Promise boundary: demonstrate visibility, controls, streamlining, and customized operational support; do not promise profit, income, savings, or guaranteed results.
- Distinct from Get Your Time Back: Time addresses capacity and administrative burden. This page addresses retained income, operating waste, margin visibility, and disconnected money-moving systems.
- Distinct from Get Paid Faster: this page covers the whole operating system; Get Paid Faster covers the time between completed work and collected payment.
- Primary action: `Find Where My Money Is Going`

## Human recognition definition

- Five-second recognition moment: `You do quality work. Money comes in. Too little of it stays.`
- Familiar visible consequence: the owner knows work is coming in and bills are being paid, but the bank balance never seems to match the effort or sales.
- Human outcome: the owner can understand where earned money is going, keep more of it in the business, and use it for stability, reinvestment, growth, or the life the work is meant to support.
- Audience's natural words: `We stay busy, so where is the money going?` `We made money on that job—didn't we?` `Why is there never as much left as there should be?`
- Why the visitor can see themselves or someone they know: the page begins with the familiar mismatch between working hard, bringing in revenue, and seeing too little remain—not with a technical diagnosis.
- Distinct from Get Your Time Back: this recognition is about the missing financial result, not paperwork, long hours, or lack of family time.

## Content and methodology parameters

- Explain value in plain language first, then introduce relevant credibility: systems engineering, process engineering, Lean, Six Sigma, quality systems, regulatory experience, root-cause analysis, controls, and continuous improvement.
- Lean/Six Sigma language must be explained for a sole-proprietor audience; credentials cannot substitute for showing practical value.
- Show interconnected inputs, handoffs, systems, decisions, and controls. Do not simplify the work into a generic four-step checklist.
- Potential causes include duplicate or underused software, disconnected systems, processing fees, inventory waste or weak controls, unbilled extra work, incomplete invoicing, nonpayment/late payment, missing cost or margin visibility, manual re-entry, and untracked approvals.
- Refer to keeping, retaining, protecting, or strengthening earned money. Do not repeat “leaks” as the page's identity.
- Do not frame the cause as owner incompetence. The operation outgrew what one person can see and control alone.
- This is operational/process improvement, not a financial audit, accounting, tax, CPA, or investment service.
