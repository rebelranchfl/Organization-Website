# RRA-2026-0001 — Product Manuscript Addendum: Responsible Rebellion Rebuild

**Status:** PRODUCT WORKING — ADDITIVE MANUSCRIPT CORRECTION  
**Applies with:** `product-manuscript.md`  
**Evidence boundary:** Owner-approved Revision 3 + owner-approved UV addendum  
**Purpose:** Correct the structural learner-experience failures found in Final Product Review without deleting the approved base manuscript.

## How to use this addendum

The base manuscript remains valid research-to-learning material. This addendum changes how the learner enters, sees, branches through and uses that material.

Where the base manuscript says or implies one intended use, one linear plan, or testing before meaningful system education, this addendum governs.

## Opening learner promise

You do not need to know every contaminant before you can learn how water systems work.

We can show you how people have protected, settled, filtered, disinfected, stored and moved water; what different materials and methods do; how systems changed across time and place; what you can build or adapt; where modern equipment is useful; and where testing changes the decision.

What we cannot know without information from your water is which exact treatment jobs your exact water needs.

That is the difference between **teaching the method** and **claiming we know your water**.

## Step 1 — Tell Us About Your Water

Plain questions first:
- Where does your water come from?
- What does it look/smell/taste like, if anything stands out?
- What treatment already happens before it reaches you, if known?
- Has anything changed recently?

Immediately show a recognizable source visual, not a block of explanation.

## Step 2 — What Do You Need the Water to Do?

**Choose every use that matters.**

Examples:
- drink / cook;
- animals;
- garden / irrigation;
- emergency backup;
- household non-potable use;
- demonstration / learning.

The learner may select two, three, four or more relevant uses. Do not force a single choice.

### What the learner sees next

Show one source feeding a branch diagram.

Example:

`PRIVATE WELL`

`shared source / pump / first-stage protection`

`↙ drinking & cooking   ↓ animals   ↘ garden / irrigation`

Under the diagram explain:
- what can be shared;
- where functions split;
- why the split exists;
- what each branch must accomplish.

The purpose is to make complexity understandable, not to hide it.

## Step 3 — See the Jobs Water Systems Perform

Before asking the learner to choose products, show the jobs visually:
- keep debris/contamination out where possible;
- settle larger material;
- screen/prefilter;
- filter particles;
- adsorb selected compounds;
- treat specific dissolved contaminants when the identified problem calls for it;
- disinfect/inactivate microorganisms when required;
- store;
- move;
- control;
- verify;
- maintain.

Each job opens into three depths:
1. **What this does** — plain language;
2. **See a system** — diagram/build concept;
3. **Show me the science / sources** — optional depth.

## Step 4 — See Actual Systems

The learner should be able to inspect multiple evidence-backed system families even before personal testing is complete.

### Layered 55-gallon filtration build concept

Show a cutaway barrel and label each approved layer/component. For every layer show:
- the function;
- what it can help with;
- substitutions documented in the approved historical/regional research;
- what it does not establish;
- cleaning/maintenance considerations;
- where the water goes next.

The learner is not told that a layered barrel automatically makes unknown water potable. The learner is taught exactly what the stages are doing and what additional treatment/verification may be needed for the selected use.

### 5-gallon gravity build concept

Use the same function-first logic at smaller scale. Show what changed because of scale and what stayed the same.

### Settling / prefiltration / storage setup

Show how storage and settling can be part of the system rather than treating every problem as a cartridge-filter purchase.

### Modern treatment train

Show where carbon, contaminant-specific treatment, validated UV, RO, ion exchange or other approved modern methods fit by job—not as a universal stack everyone needs.

### Historical / regional comparison

For each approved example, answer:
- What problem were people solving?
- What materials did they have?
- What did the method actually do?
- What limitation remains?
- What modern system performs the same or a related job today?

## Step 5 — Use What You Have Before Deciding What to Buy

Every branch sorts options into:
- **Use What You Have**;
- **Reuse / Repurpose**;
- **Source Locally**;
- **Worth Buying**;
- **Optional Upgrade**;
- **Do Not Substitute**.

Buying is justified by function, performance, safety, durability, convenience or verification—not by default.

## Step 6 — What Should You Test?

Testing appears after the learner understands the system space.

Ask:
- What decision are you trying to make?
- What unknown would change that decision?
- Can a low-cost home screen answer that question?
- Is a targeted test more useful?
- Is a certified/laboratory result needed for the decision?

Then show the verified affordable/free/local testing routes from the approved evidence package when applicable.

### Important teaching rule

Do not say: `We cannot tell you anything until you test.`

Say: `Here is what these systems and methods are known to do. Here is what we still do not know about your water. This test would tell us whether your branch needs X, Y or Z.`

## Step 7 — Build My Water System

Now convert the learner's answers into a living plan.

For each selected branch show:
- required jobs;
- shared components;
- branch-specific components;
- build / buy / hybrid options;
- substitutions;
- testing that would change the branch;
- verification;
- maintenance;
- failure / backup;
- next action.

The learner should be able to see the whole system and expand one branch at a time.

## Step 8 — Does Responsible Rebellion Hold Water?

When a learner builds or implements an alternative system, give them a structured experiment:

`UNDERSTAND → BUILD / IMPLEMENT → OBSERVE → TEST → COMPARE → IMPROVE → DECIDE`

Compare the alternative with the modern/default approach where supportable:
- performance;
- cost;
- energy use/dependence;
- consumables;
- maintenance;
- repairability;
- locally available materials;
- capacity;
- reliability;
- waste;
- convenience;
- resilience.

Permanent learner rule:

> **A successful build is not proof. Test the result.**

## Step 9 — Scale It Without Losing the Principle

The learner can switch between:
- countertop;
- 5-gallon;
- 55-gallon;
- larger fixed storage / farm use.

Show how weight, support, head/flow, fittings, cleaning, overflow, portability and maintenance change while the underlying functions remain recognizable.

`See Scale and Function Before Form` must be an in-product expandable panel or working route that returns the learner to the exact prior place. It must never point to an unfinished page.

## Step 10 — My Water Plan

This is the payoff, not a generic recap.

It must display:
- source;
- every selected use;
- whole-system branch map;
- shared infrastructure;
- what each branch needs to do;
- known facts;
- meaningful unknowns;
- testing choices and what they change;
- selected build/system options;
- what each component does;
- substitutions/use-what-you-have choices;
- what is worth buying;
- verification;
- maintenance/retest;
- failure/backup;
- next three actions;
- experiment/comparison results when used.

If the learner changes an earlier answer, this plan updates where technically practical.

## Optional depth behavior

`Tell me more`, `Show me why`, `Show me the science`, `See the system`, `Technical details`, and `Sources` are depth layers—not exits from the product.

They must:
- open without losing learner answers;
- preserve scroll/step context;
- have a clear return path;
- never 404;
- never require the learner to restart the profile.

## Safety language

Use the approved general footer disclaimer for the broad educational boundary.

Inside lessons, add a short warning only when a specific material and reasonably immediate hazard is tied to the action being shown. Do not repeat generic legal/compliance disclaimers as content.

## TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE

The final learner question is not only `Did my water system work?`

It is:

> **Can I use the same method—understand the function, inventory resources, compare forms, test the result and improve it—to solve a different real-life problem?**

That is Responsible Rebellion.
