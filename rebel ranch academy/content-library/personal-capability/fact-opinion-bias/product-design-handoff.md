## Required KEEP material

Product Design must preserve the distinct value of the approved foundation, including:

1. The distinction between factual claims, interpretations/inferences, opinions/value judgments, predictions and mixed claims.
2. The separate question of **claim type** versus **evidence strength**.
3. Bias blind spot, myside bias, motivated reasoning, belief bias, repetition/illusory-truth effects and continued influence after correction.
4. Source tracing, accuracy attention, alternative explanations and `consider the opposite` as practical debiasing tools, without overstating what any one tool can accomplish.
5. The RRA learner method `SORT → SOURCE → STRESS TEST → DECIDE`.
6. The boundary that identifying bias does not automatically make a person's conclusion false.
7. The boundary that an opinion can be evidence-informed and a factual claim can still be unsupported or false.
8. Francis Bacon's historical `Idols` connection as a pattern/history teaching layer, not as a claim that Bacon discovered modern cognitive psychology.
9. Revision 2 evidence on conformity and social influence from peers, friends, parents, norms, groups and culture.
10. The distinction between public conformity and private belief change.
11. Evidence that social information can influence judgments and behavior while preserving the learner's agency and the evidence that accuracy interventions/corrections can improve judgment.
12. Practical family, relationship, business, money, leadership and media/information examples.
13. The exact Academy rules: **Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.** and **Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.**
14. `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE`, including the boundary that factual evidence does not fully settle genuine value questions.

No broad REMOVE/REPLACE action is authorized by this approval.

## Product Opportunity Research questions

Before choosing a product format, Product Design must determine with evidence:

- Which audience problem is strongest: family/homeschool evidence literacy, teen/adult critical thinking, media/information literacy, difficult-conversation judgment, business decision quality, or a layered product family?
- What comparable products/curricula/tools already exist and where do learners still struggle after using them?
- What real-life decisions justify a practical RRA product rather than another reading-heavy critical-thinking guide?
- Which exercises can make conformity, repetition, claim classification, source tracing and evidence-strength comparison visible or measurable without manipulation or shame?
- What personalized or repeat-use output would materially help a learner classify claims, inspect evidence, recognize social pressure and make a decision?
- What age/depth branching is actually useful without fragmenting the product into unrelated versions?
- What should be free, paid, bundled or follow-on work?
- Why Rebel Ranch Academy, specifically, rather than a generic media-literacy worksheet?
- What changes for the learner if they actually use the method?

## Product Design boundaries

- Do not teach reflexive distrust of institutions, media, experts, friends, family or groups.
- Do not imply that independence means ignoring other people.
- Do not label disagreement itself as bias.
- Do not claim that a debiasing exercise eliminates bias.
- Do not make political persuasion the product identity.
- Plain language and real-life application should lead; technical psychology belongs in optional depth where useful.
- Build tangible learner outputs and decisions, not primarily more text to read.
