# Rebel Ranch Local Brand Guide

## Brand hierarchy

**Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc.**  
→ **Rebel Ranch Ministries**  
→ **Rebel Ranch Local**  
→ **Marketplace • Goods • Services**

Rebel Ranch Local is the local-commerce umbrella. Marketplace is one category within the umbrella rather than the word being forced to represent food, goods, and services by itself.

## Core promise

**Everything local. One easy place.**

This is the broad marketing idea. It can be used in campaign copy and supporting layouts without being forced into every logo application.

## What the brand must communicate quickly

Within roughly 1–2 seconds, the viewer should understand:

- local food / farm products;
- handmade/local goods;
- trades and local services;
- this is a local/community system, not a big-box marketplace;
- the experience is meant to be easier for buyers and fairer for small local sellers/providers.

## Master logo rules

1. Use the approved realistic three-section composition as the master visual direction.
2. Keep the outer geometry circular; do not stretch or compress it into an oval.
3. Keep the three visual sections clearly separated.
4. Keep the local map-pin at the top in its approved placement.
5. Do not insert an extra decorative leaf or unrelated icon inside the map-pin unless specifically approved.
6. Marketplace section should visibly communicate local food: raw milk, honey, canned goods, produce, and meat.
7. Goods section should communicate handmade products: candle, tincture, soap, etc.
8. Services section should communicate trades/services with distinct, realistic tools and a visible `TRADES` treatment on the pouch.
9. Use functional labels where they improve instant comprehension: `RAW MILK`, `HONEY`, `CANNED GOODS`, `HAND MADE`, `TINCTURES`, `SOAP`, `TRADES`.
10. Do not remove or change approved elements during derivative production unless the owner requests that specific change.

## Typography direction

- `REBEL RANCH`: strong slab/serif display treatment with **light/minimal texture only**.
- Do not over-distress the primary name.
- `LOCAL`: clean, bold, highly readable.
- Supporting/category text: clean sans serif, high legibility.
- Avoid fake-western novelty typography and overly rustic decorative fonts.

## Color palette

The approved direction is camo/field-inspired without becoming tactical or military branding.

| Role | Name | Hex | Use |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Primary | Ranch Olive | `#2F3D1F` | primary logo type, borders, major brand fields |
| Secondary | Sage | `#6B7F4A` | supporting accents and secondary fields |
| Light | Cream | `#F2E9DA` | light canvas, reversed text, separation |
| Warm accent | Saddle Tan | `#B47A4A` | warm accent tied to leather/wood/product imagery |
| Dark warm | Leather Brown | `#5A3A24` | secondary dark neutral, realistic goods/trades support |
| Dark neutral | Ranch Charcoal | `#1A1A1A` | deep-background use, typography where appropriate |

### Contrast rule

Do not simply drop the standard olive logo onto black. On dark backgrounds, tune the treatment so the brand does not disappear: cream/bright separation, stronger light outlines, and sufficient contrast are required.

## Photography / imagery

Use realistic, grounded images. Avoid generic stock-farm clichés or random western decoration.

Marketplace examples:
- vegetables and produce;
- meat on a quality cutting board;
- raw milk;
- local honey;
- canned goods.

Goods examples:
- candles;
- soap;
- tinctures;
- handmade/maker products.

Services examples:
- real tradespeople;
- repair/service work;
- distinct practical tools;
- service-provider situations.

The imagery should support the actual product category rather than treating Rebel Ranch Ministries as a farm-first brand.

## Organization naming rule

When campaign copy explains who built, supports, or operates the program, use **Rebel Ranch Ministries**. Do not shorten it to `Rebel Ranch` in that context.

The program/product name remains **Rebel Ranch Local**.

## Brand voice

Direct, useful, grounded, local, slightly rebellious but responsible.

Prefer language such as:
- `A fairer place for local people to get seen.`
- `One easy place to find and support local.`
- `Big Business has enough platforms. Local people need one too.`
- `Buy local. Shop local. Build local.`
- `Real People. Real Skills. Real Community.`

Avoid generic corporate/nonprofit language and do not make the brand cute, crafty, tactical, or overly rustic.
