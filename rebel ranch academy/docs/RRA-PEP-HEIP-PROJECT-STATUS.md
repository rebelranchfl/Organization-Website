## 3. CURRENT PEP / HEIP RULES BEING USED

Research must always be checked against the current Step Up for Students PEP Purchasing Guide, PEP Family Handbook, Non-School Provider Handbook, official Summary of Changes, and current EMA/provider instructions before a compliance claim is treated as final.

### Current HEIP eligibility framework established during this project

The current PEP Purchasing Guide states that FTC-PEP funds may be used for tuition and fees for programs, courses, or classes offered to FTC-PEP students when those services are part of the student's sequential instruction.

The HEIP eligibility considerations include that the program:

1. is selected by the parent/guardian as part of the student's parent-directed education;
2. provides courses or activities that enhance or enrich learning opportunities; and
3. is a business organization rather than having tuition/fees payable to an individual, subject to the exceptions stated by Step Up.

### July 2026 change identified and controlling for project planning

The Step Up Summary of Changes expressly reported for PEP/FES-UA HEIP:

- removal of the requirement to have a **published tuition and fee schedule**; and
- removal of the requirement to **be operated in a physical location**.

Older Step Up pages/guides may still display superseded language. Do not silently reintroduce an old requirement solely because an older or unreconciled provider page still contains it. When Step Up sources conflict, document the conflict and check the latest change notice/current controlling material before making a compliance conclusion.

### Important unresolved source inconsistency

As of September 5, 2026, some Step Up web content/search indexing still displays older HEIP language stating that a tuition schedule must be publicly published and that the program may not be online. This conflicts with the July 2026 Summary of Changes identified during this project. Treat this as a source-version inconsistency requiring careful reconciliation, not as permission to guess.

### Fee schedule distinction

Removal of the requirement to **publish** a fee schedule publicly does not mean RRA has no prices or that EMA never needs pricing information. Current PEP reimbursement guidance for a first-time HEIP states that vetting may require a tuition/fee schedule and descriptions of courses/activities. The RRA fee schedule should therefore use exact bona fide retail prices for actual offerings once finalized.

## 4. PAYMENT MODEL — CURRENT WORKING POSITION

### Objective

Parents should not have to front large RRA tuition expenses when EMA Direct Pay is available.

### Established distinction

- **Parent reimbursement:** parent pays and requests reimbursement.
- **EMA Direct Pay:** payment is directed to the approved provider/business through EMA under applicable rules.

Step Up documentation reviewed during this project states that Direct Pay requests for providers occur after the applicable service has been rendered. Exact HEIP marketplace workflow, parent approval steps, and payment timing must be verified against current EMA instructions before operational promises are made.

### Pricing rule — LOCKED

RRA uses **full bona fide retail pricing**.

RRM historically discounts/subsidizes many services. That does not mean the official RRA retail price should be artificially low. Ministry assistance, community discounts, sponsored seats, volunteer benefits, or charitable discounts are separate decisions from the full retail educational price.

Do not create a special inflated “PEP price.” Scholarship-funded students should be charged under the same legitimate pricing policy applicable to comparable RRA services/students.

## 5. RRA EDUCATIONAL METHOD — LOCKED

Read `RRA-EDUCATIONAL-METHOD.md` before developing or restructuring RRA courses.

Core method:

**UNDERSTAND → EXPLORE → COMPARE → APPLY → CREATE**

The educational product is **organized knowledge + understanding + application**.

RRA does not manufacture arbitrary lesson counts or filler curriculum merely to resemble a conventional school. Course development begins with research and organization of real knowledge: old and new knowledge, historical and modern methods, different cultures/environments, simple and advanced tools, failures, tradeoffs, science, technology, and practical application.

Students should understand how and why a system works well enough to evaluate available resources and create/adapt solutions rather than merely reproduce one RRA-specific build.

## 7. SOLAR — FIRST MAJOR COURSE DEVELOPMENT AREA

The solar program is the initial detailed application of the RRA method.

Do not reduce it to “buy this kit and follow these steps.”

Teach the system:

**energy source → generation/capture → movement → storage → conversion/control → load/use**

Students should understand concepts such as energy, electricity, voltage, current, power, consumption, generation, storage, conversion, losses, loads, capacity, protection, safety, system sizing, and troubleshooting.

Research should examine how humans have captured, stored, converted, and used energy across time and around the world, including simple/traditional approaches and current solar/battery technology.

The end goal is the ability to analyze a need and available resources and design/adapt an appropriate solution—including, at advanced levels, systems capable of maintaining useful loads during power outages.

## 8. FEE SCHEDULE — CURRENT PROJECT STAGE

**WE ARE HERE.**

Do not finalize arbitrary prices before defining the actual educational offerings.

Earlier planning ranges and arbitrary four-session structures are **not approved final pricing**.

### Correct sequence

1. Research the body of knowledge for a program area.
2. Organize it using the RRA educational method.
3. Determine what constitutes a genuine complete sellable course/activity/lab/program.
4. Determine delivery method and resources required.
5. Determine real cost and full retail value.
6. Assign an exact bona fide retail price.
7. Add that exact offering and price to the RRA tuition/fee schedule.
8. Confirm the resulting EMA/service-offering structure complies with the then-current PEP/EMA rules.

The fee schedule should not be a generic `$X–$Y` range when submitted as the actual provider schedule. Planning ranges are acceptable internally; final offerings should have exact prices.
