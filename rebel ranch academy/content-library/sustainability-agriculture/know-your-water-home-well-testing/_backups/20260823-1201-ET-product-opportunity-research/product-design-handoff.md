## Required KEEP items
- The diagnostic sequence: `SOURCE + INTENDED USE + LOCAL RISK + RECENT CHANGE/EVENT + HOUSEHOLD VULNERABILITY → TEST PLAN → CERTIFIED/APPROPRIATE TEST → RESULT INTERPRETATION → CONFIRMATION WHEN NEEDED → TREATMENT JOB OR SOURCE/WELL CORRECTION → RETEST/VERIFY`.
- Diagnose before prescribing.
- Private-well responsibility is materially different from public-system monitoring responsibility.
- Routine testing creates a baseline, but no single national panel should be presented as universal.
- Florida's current routine bacteria/nitrate guidance must remain labeled as Florida-specific; EPA's broader baseline must remain distinguishable rather than collapsed into one rule.
- Local geology, land use, well condition, contamination events and household vulnerability can change the appropriate test plan.
- Event-triggered testing must remain distinct from routine testing.
- Sample collection/handling quality is part of test quality; learners must follow the selected lab/analyte instructions rather than a made-up universal sampling procedure.
- Certified drinking-water laboratory testing remains the decision-grade pathway when a health/safety decision depends on the result.
- Results are measurements, not complete diagnoses; interpretation must preserve analyte, units, reporting/detection limits, benchmark, sample location, treatment status and confirmation needs.
- Treatment selection follows the identified contaminant/problem; some problems may require source or well correction rather than a treatment device.
- Nitrate remains a concrete example of why clear water is not equivalent to safe water and why vulnerable household members can change urgency.
- The parent Water Through the Layers project remains unchanged; this spin-off deepens the diagnostic/testing branch and must not duplicate the parent's broader treatment architecture.
- `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE`: measure the condition that determines the decision before buying the solution, with the analogy boundary preserved.

## Governing evidence rules
> Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.

> Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.

> Evidence determines what RRA may teach. Uncertainty determines what RRA may say about the learner's specific situation. Uncertainty does not prohibit education.

Do not overclaim. Do not under-teach.

## Known uncertainty that must remain visible
- Consumer home-test-kit performance, certification, analyte coverage and decision-grade limitations have not yet been comparatively researched or endorsed.
- A nationwide state-by-state contaminant/testing matrix is not part of the approved foundation.
- PFAS, radionuclides, arsenic, pesticides/VOCs and other specialized panels require location/risk-specific evidence before detailed learner-facing recommendation logic is built.
- Exact interpretation thresholds must remain tied to the analyte, intended use, jurisdiction and current evidence.

These are Product Opportunity questions or targeted Research returns if Product Design identifies an exact evidence gap. They are not reasons to reopen the approved general research foundation.

## Safety / professional boundaries
- Do not present an unverified consumer kit as equivalent to a certified laboratory when a drinking-water health/safety decision depends on the number.
- Do not invent universal sampling procedures; use the selected laboratory/analyte instructions.
- Do not recommend a treatment device before identifying the treatment job.
- Do not universalize Florida-specific guidance.
- Do not treat boiling, filtration, disinfection or another treatment function as solving contaminant classes it does not address.
- Use short, specific warnings only for material hazards actually present in an implementation path; do not build repetitive disclaimer clutter.

## Product questions to solve
Product Design must perform the full mandatory Product Opportunity Research and Product Recommendation Scorecard before selecting a format. At minimum:

1. What real learner questions and decision failures exist around private-well/home testing?
2. What comparable test-planning guides, kits, laboratory services, result interpreters and well-water education products already exist, and where are the actual gaps?
3. What parts should be free mission education versus paid depth, bundle components, calculators, diagnostic tools or follow-on resources?
4. Would an interactive `What should I test, and why?` decision tool materially reduce confusion without creating false certainty?
5. What can responsibly be understood before results are available, and what decisions truly should wait for results?
6. What does each test tell the learner, what decision does it change, and what are the home-screening versus laboratory limitations?
7. What low-cost, free or local testing pathways are genuinely available in relevant regions, especially Florida, without manufacturing availability claims?
8. What Functional Decomposition applies to the testing/diagnostic system before recommending kits, labs or products?
9. What implementation/value pathways exist: routine baseline, event-triggered testing, vulnerable-household planning, treatment verification, well/source troubleshooting and maintenance?
10. What does modernization improve (accuracy, analyte range, standardized methods), what dependency does it create (labs, logistics, cost, specialized interpretation), and what practical knowledge remains transferable?
11. What Knowledge Compression Value can RRA provide by turning scattered federal/state/lab guidance into an understandable decision path?
12. Why Rebel Ranch Academy?
13. What changes if the learner implements this?
14. What meaningful Opportunity Intelligence branches should be structured without duplicating the parent Water project?
