## Why Personal Capability is the primary area

The central skill is judgment. Learners must be able to identify what kind of statement they are hearing, separate evidence from interpretation, notice when confidence is being driven by repetition or prior belief, and slow down before converting perception into certainty or action.

That makes `personal-capability` the strongest primary home. The project also transfers naturally into communication, relationships, leadership, business and media use.

## Likely audiences

- Teens and young adults learning how to evaluate claims before repeating them.
- Adults who want better decision-making and stronger critical thinking.
- Parents and homeschool families looking for practical media/evidence literacy.
- Small-business owners and leaders who make decisions from incomplete or emotionally loaded information.

## Academy alignment

This project directly supports the RRA outcome of growing capable, confident and responsible people. It also fits the Think Like a Rebel method:

1. What are you actually being asked to believe?
2. What do you actually know?
3. What is inference, interpretation or preference?
4. What do you not know yet?
5. What evidence would verify or challenge the claim?
6. What is your own prior belief doing to the judgment?
7. What would change your mind?
8. How should the conclusion affect action?

## Conflicts or unresolved direction

No governing-document conflict was found.

A terminology issue must remain visible: basic educational sources often define opinion too narrowly as an unsupported belief. RRA should teach a more useful distinction. An opinion can be informed by strong evidence, and a factual claim can still be false. The learner needs to identify the **type of claim** and the **standard of evidence** rather than treating “fact” and “opinion” as labels for “good” and “bad.”

## Required intellectual standards

**Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.**

**Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.**
