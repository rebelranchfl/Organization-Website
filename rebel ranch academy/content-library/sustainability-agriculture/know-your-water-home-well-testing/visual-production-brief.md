# RRA-2026-0002 — Visual Production Brief

**Status:** DRAFT — not authorized for Visual Production until Owner Product Review approves Product Design  
**Project:** Know Your Water — Home & Well Testing

## Visual job

Make the testing system understandable at a glance before asking the learner to study technical details.

Primary sequence:

`RECOGNIZABLE SCENE → PLAIN QUESTION → SIMPLE ACTION → PERSONAL RESULT → OPTIONAL DEEPER DIAGRAM / EVIDENCE`

## Required learner-facing visuals

1. **Where does your water come from?**  
   Recognizable private-well/pump/home scene beside public/community water scene.

2. **One source, many uses**  
   Whole-system visual with one source feeding multiple selected branches: drinking/cooking, infant/young-child, household, animals, irrigation, emergency, treatment verification. The learner must be able to keep more than one branch active.

3. **Routine vs event-triggered testing**  
   Split visual: calendar/baseline on one side; flood/repair/change/local-event triggers on the other. Both can coexist.

4. **Local-risk map**  
   Simple property/community scene showing septic, agriculture/livestock/fertilizer, fuel/pesticide source, landfill/waste and geology/local-groundwater questions. It must teach “ask whether this pathway matters here,” not claim every risk exists everywhere.

5. **Testing Question Map**  
   Visual chain: question → why it matters → evidence route → decision changed → what waits.

6. **Test Route Comparator**  
   Recognizable scenes/icons for county/state resource, certified local lab, mail-to-lab and limited home screen. Compare functions/tradeoffs; no “best” badge.

7. **Sample Journey**  
   Source → correct provider bottle/instructions → collection point → preservation/transport → laboratory → result. Teach that the sample is part of the test.

8. **Result anatomy**  
   Annotated sample report showing analyte, value, units, reporting/detection limit, sample location, before/after treatment status and benchmark/source. Do not use invented laboratory values as if they were real learner results.

9. **What job comes next?**  
   Result-to-action route: confirm/retest; inspect source/well/system; local support; define treatment job; maintain; monitor; schedule.

10. **Verify the correction**  
    Before → action/correction → after → test → compare. Include the line: `A successful-looking fix is not proof. Test the result.`

11. **My Well Test Plan**  
    Integrated preview showing source, selected uses, open questions, route, sample tasks, results, next actions and due dates in one living record.

12. **TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE**  
    Simple cross-domain comparison showing diagnose-before-buying across water, soil, business and repair without implying identical evidence standards.

## Plain-language labels

Use human task/result language:
- Where does your water come from?
- What do you use it for?
- Why are you testing now?
- What should you ask locally?
- What question are you trying to answer?
- Choose how to get the evidence
- Get the sample ready
- Read the result in context
- What job comes next?
- Verify the correction
- My Well Test Plan

Do not surface internal labels like `diagnostic intake`, `result interpreter`, `branch engine`, or `state contract` as default learner copy.

## Depth controls

Clickable controls only may use full pill/capsule styling. Non-interactive labels/statuses must not use pill styling.

Depth controls:
- Tell me more
- Show me why
- Show me the science
- See the system
- Sources

Every control must return to the learner's exact place and preserve answers.

## Accessibility / readability

- ordinary language first;
- text must wrap; no clipped labels or result values;
- do not encode meaning by color alone;
- diagrams must have text explanation/alt equivalents;
- mobile layout must preserve multi-branch comprehension;
- print/export version must remain readable without interactive-only cues;
- no base64-embedded images in any live implementation; use linked assets under `assets/` when later authorized.

## Evidence/safety visual boundaries

- label Florida-specific guidance as Florida-specific;
- do not depict a consumer strip as equivalent to certified laboratory analysis;
- do not visually imply that clear water equals safe water;
- do not show one treatment product as the answer to every result;
- do not create generic warning boxes throughout the product;
- use a short targeted warning only where a supported immediate hazard is being taught.

## Owner-review expectation

If Product Design is approved later, Visual Production must deliver the complete integrated learner-facing product in sequence, not disconnected graphics. The owner must be able to open at the beginning, answer questions, see My Well Test Plan update, open depth, return without losing state and reach the end without dead links.
