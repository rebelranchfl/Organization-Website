# Rebel Ranch Academy — Final Product Acceptance Standard

**Status:** Owner-approved governing standard  
**Program:** Rebel Ranch Academy (RRA)  
**AI-Agent:** ChatGPT/GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Session:** RR Website — Final Product Acceptance Checklist

## Purpose

Final Product Review judges the **actual customer experience**, not whether files exist, a worker reached 100%, or an automated QA document says PASS.

A product is not ready for owner approval until the complete learner experience proves that the approved research and product architecture became something understandable, usable, valuable, technically functional, and worth the learner's time or money.

Governing rule:

> **Complete is not the same as acceptable. The finished customer experience must prove its value.**

## 1. What Final Product Review must inspect

The owner must be able to experience the product from the beginning as the learner/customer would, including relevant questions, branches, visuals, activities, personalized outputs, optional depth, downloads/printables, navigation, and final result.

Disconnected files, asset galleries, screenshots, manifest lists, descriptions of what the learner would see, or agent summaries do not substitute for the integrated experience.

## 2. Hard acceptance criteria

Before `FINAL_PRODUCT_REVIEW` may receive an `APPROVE` decision, all applicable criteria below must pass for the current product revision.

### A. Complete journey
- The learner can start at the real beginning and reach the intended finish.
- No required step is missing, inaccessible, dead, or represented only by a placeholder.
- The owner is reviewing the same meaningful experience the customer will receive.

### B. Customer value / payoff
- The final product delivers a tangible outcome, capability, decision, plan, build concept, tool, practice, or meaningful understanding appropriate to the product promise.
- The product does more than reorganize research into additional words to read.
- If paid, the owner can identify what work RRA performed that the customer otherwise would have had to research, compare, design, calculate, test, organize, practice, or decide independently.
- The perceived value is credible at the proposed price.

### C. Personalization integrity
- Meaningful learner input materially changes the result when the product promises or implies personalization.
- Multi-select / multi-branch reality is supported when simultaneous needs legitimately coexist.
- Personalized outputs do not collapse back into generic advice.
- Changing an earlier input updates dependent results where technically practical.

### D. Visual teaching
- Visuals perform explanatory work; the product is not primarily text arranged into boxes when the subject can be shown.
- Recognizable scenes/images establish concepts where useful.
- Diagrams, cutaways, comparisons, components, processes, branch maps, before/after examples, or other relevant visuals are present where they materially improve understanding.
- Technical visuals remain available as optional depth when appropriate.

### E. Practical implementation
- Where the evidence supports application, the learner can see what to do with the knowledge.
- Functions, components, sequence, alternatives, substitutions, tradeoffs, limits, maintenance, testing/verification, and next actions are clear where applicable.
- `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE` is present for substantial Academy products.

### F. Navigation and state continuity
- Every internal link/control works.
- No 404 or broken destination exists in the learner journey.
- `Show me why`, `Show me the science`, `See the system`, evidence/technical depth, and similar exploration controls return the learner to the correct place.
- Learner answers/state survive normal navigation and deeper exploration.
- A learner is not forced to restart because they explored optional depth.

### G. Plain-language usability
- The default layer uses the simplest accurate language.
- The learner can understand the essential lesson without opening technical depth.
- Human task/result names are used instead of internal system vocabulary.
- Complexity lives beneath the experience rather than in the learner's way.

### H. Evidence integrity / Responsible Rebellion
- The product teaches the evidence-backed solution space rather than suppressing education because a learner-specific unknown exists.
- Uncertainty about the learner's exact situation is distinguished from uncertainty about general system knowledge.
- Historical, traditional, regional, modern, engineering, scientific, and field evidence is represented appropriately where approved.
- Government/institutional guidance is accurately represented as evidence, not automatic Academy doctrine.
- The product does not overclaim and does not under-teach.

### I. Testing / verification integrity
- Testing is used to verify, refine, compare, or decide when appropriate; it is not treated as permission to educate.
- The learner understands what a test can change and what it cannot prove.
- Responsible experiments include observation, testing/comparison, and interpretation where applicable.
- A successful build is not presented as proof merely because it appears to work.

### J. Safety proportionality
- Broad generic educational disclaimer language is handled through the approved site/product disclaimer location rather than repeated throughout the learning experience.
- In-product warnings appear only where a specific material reasonably immediate hazard warrants one.
- Warning language is short, direct, useful, and tied to the action.
- The product does not introduce unnecessary hazardous pathways when a safer method teaches the same principle.

### K. Technical and delivery quality
- Required links, controls, forms, calculations, media, files, downloads, and printables work.
- Mobile/screen behavior is usable where applicable.
- Printable materials print correctly where applicable.
- No draft, placeholder, internal-only, or test content appears in the customer experience.
- Preview/review version matches the actual release candidate.

### L. Owner "Would I pay for this?" test
For paid products, the owner must be able to answer **yes** to:

> **If I bought this at the proposed price, would I feel that Rebel Ranch Academy did meaningful work for me and delivered what the offer promised?**

If the owner's reaction is primarily “I got a bunch of words,” the product fails Final Product Acceptance regardless of research quality or file completeness.

## 3. Outcome

A passed checklist authorizes the owner to choose **Approve Final Product** at the Final Product Review gate.

It does **not** authorize publication.

Final Product Approval moves the accepted product toward the separate Release Workflow:

`FINAL PRODUCT APPROVAL → RELEASE PREP → OWNER RELEASE DECISION → PUBLISH → VERIFY LIVE → LIVE`

## 4. Failure routing

If one or more criteria fail, the product must be routed to the stage that owns the defect:

- evidence gap → Research;
- architecture/value/personalization/branching problem → Product Design;
- visual/integration/navigation/delivery problem that does not require architecture change → Visual Production;
- mixed structural problems → Product Design first, then Visual Production after owner Product Review.

Do not mark a criterion passed because another stage can fix it later.

## 5. Water lesson carried forward

`RRA-2026-0001` demonstrated that a technically complete package can still fail as a customer product when:

- real-life multi-use needs are forced into single-select paths;
- deeper-learning links 404;
- navigation loses answers/state;
- personalization yields mostly generic text;
- visuals do not do enough teaching;
- the learner reaches the end without a substantive usable payoff.

These are now system-level Final Product Acceptance failures for every applicable Academy product.
