# RRA-2026-0002 — Integrated Learner Flow

## Goal

The learner should move through one coherent experience, not a bag of disconnected worksheets.

## Flow

1. **Start with the water source**  
   Learner identifies private well, public/community supply, or other/unknown.

2. **Select every real use**  
   Drinking/cooking, infant/young-child use, household use, animals, irrigation, emergency backup, treatment verification and other remembered uses may coexist.

3. **Select why testing matters now**  
   Routine baseline and any event/change branches remain separate but visible together.

4. **Identify local-risk questions**  
   The learner sees supported risk categories and creates questions to verify locally rather than receiving unsupported universal analyte prescriptions.

5. **Build the Testing Question Map**  
   Each active branch records the question, why it matters, what evidence can answer it, what decision changes and what should wait.

6. **Compare evidence routes**  
   Local/county, certified local laboratory, mail-to-lab and limited home screening are compared by function and consequence.

7. **Prepare the sample**  
   Provider-specific instructions are stored in the plan. No invented universal sample procedure.

8. **Record the result in context**  
   Analyte, value, units, reporting limit, sample location, treatment status, benchmark/source and affected use remain together.

9. **Choose the next job**  
   Confirm/retest, inspect/correct source or well, contact local resource, define treatment job, maintain, monitor or schedule next check.

10. **Verify**  
    Before/after or follow-up testing confirms whether the correction actually changed the measured condition.

11. **Maintain My Well Test Plan**  
    History, due dates, events, results and unresolved questions remain reusable.

12. **Transfer the Principle**  
    Diagnose the condition that determines the decision before buying the solution.

## Free-to-paid boundary

### Free: What Should I Test — and Why?
Completes steps 1–5 and gives a useful first Testing Question Map plus next evidence-route guidance.

### Paid: My Well Test Plan
Adds persistent multi-branch state, route comparison, sample organizer, result-context records, next-action routing, retest/verification history, recurring calendar and printable/exportable household record.

The free layer must solve a real problem. The paid layer earns its value by doing more planning, comparison, organization and repeat-use work—not by hiding foundational truth.

## Depth navigation

Plain language is the default. Optional controls may open:
- Tell me more;
- Show me why;
- Show me the science;
- See the system;
- Sources.

Every depth route must:
- keep current answers;
- keep all selected uses visible;
- return to the exact originating step;
- never reset the personalized plan;
- never lead to a dead/404 route.

## Change propagation examples

- Add `flood` after building a routine plan → create a new event-triggered branch while preserving routine history.
- Add `infant/young-child use` → surface the approved nitrate urgency example and appropriate decision-grade evidence need without inventing a universal expanded panel.
- Change provider/lab → refresh sample-instruction reference fields without deleting prior completed result records.
- Add `animals` or `irrigation` → preserve those branches and flag that use-specific thresholds require relevant evidence/local guidance where not present in the approved foundation.

## Learner comprehension test

At every step the learner should be able to answer:
1. What am I looking at?
2. Why does it matter?
3. What do I do now?
4. What did my answer change?
5. What is my next step?
6. Where can I go deeper if I want to?
