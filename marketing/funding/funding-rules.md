# Rebel Ranch Ministries / FFN Funding & Grant Rules

## Purpose

This file governs how the funding agent researches grants, assesses
eligibility, and drafts grant applications and donation-letter language on
behalf of Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc. (FFN) / Rebel Ranch
Ministries (RRM).

This is the most legally sensitive file in the marketing/ folder. Getting
tax-exempt status language wrong can create real liability. Follow this
file exactly. When in doubt, flag the question for Brooke instead of
guessing.

## Resolved 2026-08-16 — was an open conflict

`docs/non-negotiables.md` previously stated flatly "FFN — the 501(c)(3)
nonprofit" with no further detail. Brooke confirmed directly on
2026-08-16 that this is fact: FFN does not hold an IRS 501(c)(3)
determination letter — it is tax-exempt as a church under automatic law

this and to match the language already established consistently across
the live site's legal disclaimer (16 pages): "operates as a tax-exempt
church under Section 501(c)(3) of the Internal Revenue Code, organized
under Section 508(c)(1)(a)." The live site disclaimer itself was not
changed — it was already worded correctly.

## Actual status (operating rule until corrected)

- Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc. is a **church**, and churches are
  **automatically tax-exempt under IRC Section 508(c)(1)(A)** — no
  application or IRS approval is legally required for that exemption to
  be real and valid.
- FFN does **not** currently hold an IRS 501(c)(3) **determination
  letter**. A determination letter is optional formal documentation some
  churches choose to file for (Form 1023) — it is not what makes a church
  tax-exempt, but it is often what grantors and donors ask to see as
  proof.
- Filing for a determination letter is a real, weighed decision (cost,
  processing time) that Brooke has explicitly deferred. Do not draft, plan
  toward, or imply that filing is scheduled or in progress unless Brooke
  says otherwise.

## Approved language

Locked 2026-08-17, after Brooke flagged that spelling out "does not
currently hold a determination letter" in public-facing copy reads as
a hedge/red flag to a lay donor, even though nothing legally requires
disclosing it. She's right: the law doesn't ask us to volunteer this,
so don't. State the real, confident, accurate fact — automatic
tax-exempt status — and stop there in anything public-facing.

Use, in all PUBLIC-facing copy (website, donation letters, outreach
messages):

- "Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc. is a church and is automatically
  tax-exempt under federal law (IRC Section 508(c)(1)(A))."
- "Donations may be tax-deductible as allowed by law."
- "We can provide a donation acknowledgment letter for your records."

Do NOT include in public-facing copy: any mention of not holding a
determination letter, not being "501(c)(3) certified," or similar
hedging. It isn't required, it's true either way, and it reads as
uncertainty when there isn't any.

Use ONLY when a grant application, donor verification form, or funder
specifically and directly asks whether a determination letter is held
(that's a different situation — answering a direct question truthfully
is not the same as volunteering a caveat nobody asked for):

- "We do not currently hold a formal IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter.
  We are evaluating whether to pursue one."

This internal file (and `non-negotiables.md`) still need the full,
precise picture for eligibility triage — that doesn't change. What
changed is what gets said out loud to the public.

Do not use:

- "We are a 501(c)(3)." — false as stated; do not say this anywhere,
  including casually in outreach or social copy.
- "We are 501(c)(3) certified" or "501(c)(3) approved" in any form.
- Any implication that a determination letter is pending, filed, or
  in-process unless Brooke has actually authorized starting that filing.
- Any specific promise about how much of a donation is deductible for a
  given donor's specific tax situation.

## Business donor tax-benefit pitch — confirmed accurate 2026-08-17

It is legitimate to lead with tax deductibility as a reason for a
business to donate — confirmed against the IRS's own Publication 1828
(Tax Guide for Churches and Religious Organizations): a church that
meets 501(c)(3) requirements is automatically treated as tax-exempt, and
donors may claim a charitable deduction even without the church holding
a determination letter.

Use precisely, per these rules:

- Say "tax-deductible," not "tax exempt," when describing the donation —
  "tax exempt" describes FFN's own status, not what the donor gets.
- Do not claim or imply a guaranteed dollar-for-dollar business write-off.
  Most target businesses (feed stores, restaurants, small dealerships)
  are pass-through entities (LLC/S-corp/sole proprietor) — for these, the
  deduction flows to the *owner's personal return* and only helps if they
  itemize. Do not explain this in outreach messages; simply avoid
  overpromising and let the donor's own accountant sort out specifics.
- One honest risk exists and should not be hidden if asked: without a
  determination letter, a donor who is audited would need to
  independently show FFN qualifies. This is rare and the church still
  legitimately qualifies — but do not deny this exists if a business or
  their accountant raises it.
- For in-kind donations (supplies, equipment) specifically: donations
  over $500 require the donor to file IRS Form 8283 on their end; over
  $5,000 requires a qualified appraisal. RRM providing a proper donation
  acknowledgment letter is what makes any of this usable for the donor —
  always offer one.
- Approved short pitch line for outreach: "Your donation may be
  tax-deductible as allowed by law, and we're glad to provide a donation
  letter for your records."

## Donation letters

FFN can provide donors a written donation acknowledgment/receipt letter
regardless of determination-letter status — this is standard practice for
churches operating under automatic 508(c)(1)(A) exemption and is not
dependent on holding a determination letter. The agent may draft a
standard donation-letter template for Brooke's approval, but must not
send one to an actual donor without separate authorization, and must not
state or imply IRS-verified 501(c)(3) status in that letter.

## Grant eligibility triage — mandatory for every grant found

Every entry in `grant-research.md` must be tagged with one of:

- **Does not require a determination letter** — church/508(c)(1)(A)
  status, a fiscal sponsor, or general nonprofit/community-org language is
  sufficient per the grantor's own published eligibility rules.
- **Requires a determination letter** — the grantor's published rules
  explicitly require proof of 501(c)(3) status. Do not draft or submit an
  application to this grant. Keep the entry for future reference (in case
  a determination letter or fiscal sponsorship arrangement changes this).
- **Unclear — needs direct verification** — published rules do not say
  either way. Contact the grantor directly to confirm before drafting.
  Do not assume eligibility.

The agent must not draft a full grant application for any grant tagged
"Requires a determination letter" unless Brooke has separately authorized
pursuing fiscal sponsorship or a 1023 filing and that path is confirmed
resolved.

## Fiscal sponsorship — flagged, not decided

Fiscal sponsorship (a recognized 501(c)(3) organization accepting
grants/donations on FFN's behalf and re-granting the funds) is a real,
faster alternative to filing Form 1023 directly, and could unlock
grants currently tagged "Requires a determination letter." This is a
strategic decision for Brooke, not something the agent should pursue or
recommend a specific sponsor for without her direction. Recorded here so
it isn't lost, not as an active work item.

## Program breadth rule

FFN/RRM's program mix is broad — ministry, practical education
(Academy, Creation Station), agriculture/sustainability (Animal
Husbandry), and small-business/community support (Business Freedom,
Marketplace). Grant research should span all of these categories, not
narrow to one. A grant that fits any real current or near-term program is
worth recording.

## Brand and claims rules (inherited from `marketing/social-media/brand-rules.md`)

- Do not overpromise tax treatment.
- Do not invent nonprofit/legal/tax claims not covered above.
- Do not claim a program is fully available if it is still being built
  (Rebel Ranch Academy is in development — do not describe it as
  operating/enrolling in a grant narrative).
- Keep 3P Business Optimization entirely separate from any FFN/RRM grant
  or donation material — 3P is a for-profit business and must never
  appear as if it receives or is eligible for FFN/RRM grant funds, per
  `docs/non-negotiables.md`.

## Agent-first workflow

The agent does all automatable funding research and drafting. Brooke's
role is limited to approving, rejecting, or correcting drafts, and making
the legal/strategic decisions that are hers alone (whether to pursue a
determination letter, whether to use fiscal sponsorship, which grants to
actually submit).

The agent must:

- research and add real grant opportunities to `grant-research.md`, each
  tagged with the eligibility triage above
- create and revise grant application drafts in `grant-queue.md`
- check `funding-rules.md` and `grant-research.md` before drafting any
  application or donation-letter language
- check `funding-archive.md` before reusing narrative language, to avoid
  contradicting a previous submission
- submit or send funding materials only after the specific content is
  `Approved` and submission has been separately authorized
- mark the original entry `Submitted` after it goes out, and copy the
  final submitted material into `funding-archive.md`

`grant-queue.md` is the only working queue. `funding-archive.md` is the
only source of truth for submission history. Do not create a duplicate
tracker.

## Approval checklist

Before any grant application or funding material is approved:

- Does this avoid ever saying "we are a 501(c)(3)"?
- Is the tax-exempt/determination-letter language exactly as approved
  above?
- Has the eligibility triage tag been checked, and does it allow drafting
  this application?
- Is 3P kept completely separate from this material?
- Does this avoid claiming Rebel Ranch Academy is fully available?
- Does the program described actually exist and match its real status?

If the answer is no to any of those, it goes back to draft.
