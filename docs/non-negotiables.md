# Rebel Ranch Ministries / FFN / 3P — Mission, Vision & Non-Negotiables

This is the source-of-truth reference for who we are, what we stand for, and
the rules that don't bend — across Faith, Family & Nature Church (FFN),
Rebel Ranch Ministries (RRM), and 3P Help Me (3P). Hand this to anyone
(a new hire, a contractor, an AI assistant, a new chat session) before they
write or build anything on our behalf.

For visual identity (colors, logo, typography), see [`brand-guide.md`](./brand-guide.md) —
that document is not duplicated here.

## Mission

Rebel Ranch Ministries exists to help families and individuals reclaim
their freedom — financially, personally, and everywhere in between — and
to bring back what's been slipping away: the American Dream, the family
unit, and real community. We do it through practical education, real-life
skills, and emotional stability, because a family that's educated,
self-sufficient, and emotionally sound is a family that can stand on its
own — and still show up for the people around it. Independence and unity
aren't opposites here. You need both to actually change anything.

## Vision

We envision a Rebel Ranch that helps parents earn more, understand how
these systems work, and build the skills to improve their personal
situation — because opportunity for a family starts with the parents and
flows down to the children. Kids don't get a vote in how healthy or
stable their home is until they're grown; that's on us. So everything
starts there — building parents and family relationships strong enough
to carry the next generation further than the last one got. Our door
stays open to anyone, but our compass points at the family first.

## Entity Relationship Map

- **FFN (Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc.)** — the parent organization.
  FFN operates as a tax-exempt church under Section 501(c)(3) of the
  Internal Revenue Code, organized under Section 508(c)(1)(a) — the same
  language used consistently across the live site's legal disclaimer.
  FFN does **not** currently hold an IRS 501(c)(3) determination letter;
  that exemption is automatic by law and does not require one. This
  matters for grant eligibility specifically — see
  `marketing/funding/funding-rules.md` — because many grantors ask for a
  determination letter as proof even though it isn't legally required.
  Confirmed directly by the owner 2026-08-16.
- **RRM (Rebel Ranch Ministries)** — a *ministry program under FFN*, not a
  separate legal entity. Everything RRM does is FFN's work.
- **3P (3P Help Me)** — a separate for-profit business, owned and run
  independently. Aligned with FFN/RRM in mission, but not owned by FFN and
  not itself a program of FFN.

FFN feeds RRM as a program. RRM and 3P operate as aligned partners — not
as parent/subsidiary, not as vendor/client in the traditional sense.

## Org-Wide Non-Negotiables

These apply across all three, no exceptions:

1. **RRM is a program of FFN.** Never referred to or presented as a
   standalone entity. All standalone legal/organizational references use
   the full "Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc." or clarify RRM's status
   as a ministry program.
2. **"Align," not "partner."** Operational language across the org uses
   "align with" rather than "partner with" — this reflects the
   hub-and-partnership model (RRM as connective trunk, others as
   branches), not a legal partnership.
3. **Non-denominational, resource-only — never worship-recruiting.** RRM
   is an online hub for information and resources. We connect people to
   what they need. We do not promote church worship, and we do not try to
   get people to come to us to worship. That is not RRM's job.
4. **Brand voice:** plain-spoken, rural, faith-rooted, opportunity-framed.
   Never implies fault. Always frames struggle as discovery — a starting
   point, not a judgment.
5. **Marketing is a first-order design priority, not an afterthought.**
   Every program, page, product, and decision gets built with visibility,
   conversion, and audience-reach in mind from the start — not bolted on
   after the fact. This is a known organizational weak spot (a tendency
   to underprice, over-give, and under-market), so it has to be actively
   corrected for, every time, by design.

## FFN/RRM ↔ 3P Non-Negotiables

The specific rules governing how the nonprofit side and the for-profit
side interact:

1. **3P provides services and expertise to RRM/FFN at no charge.** This
   is one-directional — 3P gives its time/expertise; it does not bill
   RRM/FFN for it.
2. **3P never receives funds from RRM or FFN.** Full stop. Money that RRM
   earns (through its own programs, marketplace, academy, etc.) goes back
   into the program and the church — never to 3P.
3. **Every 3P service rendered to RRM/FFN gets logged as an in-kind
   donation**, with the service described and a fair-market value noted.
   This isn't optional paperwork — it's what keeps the "free services"
   arrangement defensible to a CPA or auditor instead of just being
   something we know is true internally.
4. **Purpose of the relationship:** 3P exists in part to capture the
   Gainesville-area market — an audience with more disposable
   income — and funnel that revenue and opportunity toward funding RRM's
   programs and expanding access in smaller, rural towns that can't
   otherwise support that level of investment.

## 3P-Specific Non-Negotiables

Rules for anyone (including RRM) writing or building on 3P's behalf:

1. **3P is a for-profit business**, legally and financially distinct from
   FFN's nonprofit status. Never blur this in copy, filings, or
   marketing — the separation is what protects FFN's tax-exempt status.
2. **Public-facing persona: "Brooke Ritchie."** Credentials-led, not
   title-led. Lead with the Certified Six Sigma Master Black Belt
   credential (and relevant operations background) rather than a
   "Founder" framing.
3. **Marketing-first, same as the org-wide rule** — 3P is the
   for-profit engine intended to fund the mission, so its own visibility
   and conversion performance directly determines how much RRM/FFN can
   do. Underselling 3P isn't just a business problem, it's a mission
   problem.
