## Updated mandatory visual standard

Visual Production must rebuild or replace weak imagery. Do not merely cosmetically adjust the existing diagrams.

### 1. Tangible system communication

Where the subject is a physical system, prefer recognizable physical-system imagery/cutaways over flat boxes and rectangles. The learner should be able to identify the vessel/components/materials, understand where water enters, see the path/flow, understand what each component does, and understand where water exits or is stored.

The visual must help a learner say: **I understand what this is, what each part does, and how the system works.**

### 2. Finished-product visual quality

Visuals must look like intentional educational product artwork, not internal engineering sketches, wireframes, placeholder diagrams, generic AI cards, or QA artifacts.

Use visual hierarchy, recognizable materials/components, depth/scale where useful, clean callouts, directional flow, concise labels, and supporting explanation outside the illustration when necessary.

### 3. Text-fit requirement — zero tolerance

No text may:

- overlap another label;
- cross a border unintentionally;
- collide with a leader line;
- be clipped;
- overflow its container;
- be hidden behind another element;
- become unreadable at normal desktop or mobile viewing size.

If copy does not fit, enlarge/recompose the visual, shorten the on-image label without weakening meaning, move explanation outside the illustration, use leader lines, or divide the concept into multiple panels. Do not force approved educational meaning into an undersized box.

### 4. Real learner value

The paid/deep learner experience must provide substantially more than words arranged inside boxes. Required visual teaching includes, where applicable:

- realistic or clearly recognizable filtration cutaways;
- source-to-use flow;
- 5-gallon and 55-gallon system concepts;
- settling/prefiltration/storage relationships;
- treatment-function comparisons;
- historical/regional system comparisons;
- substitutions and Function Before Form;
- testing/verification decision logic;
- UV treatment train and boundaries;
- multi-use branching for drinking/cooking, animals, irrigation, and emergency use;
- My Water Plan / implementation system.

### 5. Reference-quality principle

Visual Production may use strong public educational imagery as quality/reference inspiration, but final RRA work must be original and evidence-correct. The target is the immediate comprehension of a strong physical cutaway: recognizable object, visible materials/components, obvious flow, attached labels, and useful build/system understanding.

Any broken route, clipped/overlapping instructional text, missing required visual, or materially unfinished diagram is an automatic QA FAIL and blocks owner review.
