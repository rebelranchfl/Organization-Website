## Corporate / Retail Community Grants

### Walmart Spark Good Local Grants

- What: Local cash grants, $250-$5,000, from individual Walmart U.S.
  stores, Sam's Clubs, and Distribution Centers to organizations serving
  that store's community.
- Eligibility note: explicitly open to "nonprofit organizations, schools,
  government entities, and faith-based organizations" serving the same
  community as the store — faith-based orgs are named directly, which is
  a strong signal for a church without a determination letter, but
  verification is still required (application requires a Spark Good
  account and third-party verification through Deed).
- 2026 cycle: begins February 1, 2026, three application cycles across
  the year.
- Link: https://www.walmart.org/how-we-give/open-applications
- Eligibility triage: **CONFIRMED BLOCKED — 2026-08-17.** Deed support
  replied directly: they only onboard organizations classified under
  501(c)(3), 501(c)(4), 501(c)(6), or 501(c)(19) — no path for a church
  operating under 508(c)(1)(a) without a filed classification, regardless
  of actual legal tax-exempt status. This is a Deed platform limitation,
  not a Walmart-specific rule, and it will likely block **any** other
  funder that also uses Deed for verification — worth checking whether a
  prospective funder uses Deed before investing research time.
- Progress: 2026-08-16 Deed's org search had no match for FFN and no
  church category existed. Brooke emailed nonprofits@joindeed.com
  2026-08-17 to request manual verification as a 508(c)(1)(a) church.
  Deed replied same day 2026-08-17 confirming the above — not currently
  possible without one of the four listed classifications. Not pursuing
  further with Deed unless FFN's status changes (fiscal sponsorship or a
  determination letter).
- Fit: any program — this is general community-serving, not
  program-specific.
- Researched: 2026-08-16

  not an FFA chapter, so this specific pot likely does not apply directly
  unless RRM formally affiliates with or sponsors student FFA involvement.
- 2026 window: opens March 1, closes May 15; awards announced August 2.
- Link: https://www.ffa.org/grants-for-growing/
- Eligibility triage: **Requires a determination letter — likely not
  applicable as-is** (FFA-chapter-specific, not a general nonprofit
  grant). Kept for reference in case an FFA-affiliation angle develops.
- Researched: 2026-08-16

### Publix Charities

- What: Grants and sponsorships focused on youth, education, hunger
  relief, and homelessness, serving FL/GA/AL/TN/SC/NC/VA.
- Eligibility note: confirmed 2026-08-17 across multiple independent
  sources — eligibility is consistently described as "nonprofit
  organizations such as schools, government bodies, or 501(c)(3)s," with
  no mention anywhere of a fiscal-sponsor alternative (unlike Cedar Tree,
  which explicitly names one). Same pattern that turned out to be a hard
  requirement for Cedar Tree — leaning toward blocked, but not a
  word-for-word confirmed rule the way Cedar Tree's was.
- Deadlines: hunger relief requests March 1-April 30 (already closed for
  2026); housing/shelter requests July 1-August 31 (likely closed or
  closing for 2026 depending on exact date); all other program/
  sponsorship requests accepted year-round — this is the only currently-
  open window.
- Link: https://publixcharities.org/request-support/
- Eligibility triage: **Likely requires a determination letter — not
  word-for-word confirmed like Cedar Tree, but same pattern.** Worth a
  direct call/email to confirm rather than assuming either way before
  spending drafting effort on it.
- Fit: youth/education angle (Creation Station, Academy).
- Researched: 2026-08-16

## Government / State Grants

### Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services (FDACS) — grant portal

- What: FDACS runs multiple grant programs across divisions — Florida
  Urban and Community Forestry Grants, Florida Farm to School grants,
  Florida Specialty Crop Block Grant Program, and others, each with its
  own eligibility rules.
- Portal: https://www.fdacs.gov/Business-Services/Grant-Opportunities and
  grants.fdacs.gov for active listings.
- Eligibility triage: **Unclear — needs direct verification per
  individual program.** State/government ag grants often accept a wider
  range of applicant types than private foundations, including
  government-adjacent and community organizations, but this varies by
  specific grant and must be checked program-by-program.
- Fit: Animal Husbandry, Academy sustainability curriculum.
- Researched: 2026-08-16

### Rooted in Justice Grants — Cedar Tree Foundation (corrected 2026-08-17; "CTF" = Cedar Tree Foundation, not an FDACS program)

- What: Grants for community-based organizations running youth-led urban
  greening / environmental and food justice programming. One-year grants
  of $20,000-$25,000, renewable for a second year (total $40,000-$50,000
  over two years) — sources vary slightly on exact structure, confirm
  with the foundation directly if pursued.
- Geographic eligibility: explicitly includes Florida (also AL, DC, GA,
  LA, MD, MS, NC, SC, VA).
- Next deadline confirmed: **Friday, October 2, 2026.**
- Eligibility triage: **Requires a determination letter — confirmed
  2026-08-17.** Published requirement: "Organizations must have 501(c)3

- Fit: strong — Academy (real-life skills, youth 12-20 range) and Animal
  Husbandry/sustainability overlap directly with this program's focus.
  The fit is real; the blocker is eligibility, not program match.
- Researched: 2026-08-16, corrected 2026-08-17

### USDA SARE (Sustainable Agriculture Research and Education)

- What: Farmer grants, $7,500-$30,000, for on-farm research and
  sustainable agriculture projects. Florida has received over $11M across
  236 projects since 1988.
- Eligibility note: SARE farmer grants are typically aimed at individual
  farmers/producers rather than nonprofit organizations broadly — Rebel
  Ranch's Animal Husbandry/ag work may qualify as a farm operation, which
  is a different eligibility path than a standard nonprofit grant and may
  not turn on 501(c)(3) status at all.
- Link: https://www.usda.gov/farming-and-ranching/financial-resources-farmers-and-ranchers/grants-and-loans
- Eligibility triage: **Unclear — needs direct verification** (different
  eligibility model than nonprofit grants; worth checking the regional
  Southern SARE office directly).
- Fit: Animal Husbandry / Rescue-Ag program.
- Researched: 2026-08-16

### Alachua County Small Grant Initiative (SGI)

- What: Up to $5,000 for grassroots nonprofits and emerging small
  businesses with 25 or fewer full-time employees and under $1M net
  worth, for one-time capacity-building or infrastructure projects.
- Eligibility note: must serve Alachua County residents living at or
  below 150% of the federal poverty level. Grant period Oct 1, 2026-Sept
  30, 2027. One application per organization. Applied for via the
  OpenGov portal.
- Eligibility triage: **Unclear — needs direct verification** on whether
  501(c)(3) status specifically is required, but the "grassroots
  nonprofit" framing suggests it may not gatekeep as tightly as private
  foundations do.
- Fit: general — but note RRM's home base is Gilchrist County, not
  Alachua; would need to show real service delivery to Alachua County
  residents to qualify. Ties to the existing Gainesville-market rationale
  in `docs/non-negotiables.md` (3P's target market) — worth checking
  whether RRM's own reach into Alachua County is documented enough yet.
- Researched: 2026-08-16

## Program Affiliation Path (not a grant — unlocks a different funding lane entirely)

### 4-H club affiliation — recommended, real next step

- Why: Walmart/Tractor Supply youth funding (Paper Clover, local store
  sponsorships, some FFA-adjacent programs) flows to institutionally
  affiliated youth — 4-H clubs and school FFA chapters — not to
  independent programs asking to be individually verified as a
  nonprofit. Confirmed 2026-08-17 after Brooke noticed local kids getting
  Walmart/Tractor Supply support that RRM hasn't. FFA requires an
  in-school agriculture class program (not realistic for RRM — the only
  non-school FFA option, an "Alumni chapter," supports a school program
  rather than getting kids their own funding). **4-H is the realistic
  path.**
- Requirements to start a club (Florida/UF-IFAS): 2 background-screened
  and fingerprinted volunteer leaders (Level 1 or Level 2 screening
  depending on hours/contact with youth, ~$77 fee good for 5 years),
  5 youth from at least 2 different families, minimum 6 hours of
  instruction. Leaders complete a volunteer application, youth protection
  training, an orientation, and 5 shadowing hours before starting.
  Animal Husbandry/Academy likely already clears the "5 kids, 2
  families" bar.
- Local contact: **UF/IFAS Extension Gilchrist County**, 125 E. Wade
  Street, Trenton, FL 32693 — phone (352) 463-3174, email
  gilchrist@ifas.ufl.edu, Director Jessica Altum-Cooper
  (jaltum@ufl.edu). Same town as Rebel Ranch — no transportation issue.
- Status: Not yet contacted. This unlocks a funding lane (4-H-affiliated
  sponsorship/grants) that is separate from and doesn't depend on FFN's
  501(c)(3)/determination-letter situation at all.
- Researched: 2026-08-17

## Strategic Path (not a grant — a mechanism)

### Fiscal sponsorship

- What: A recognized 501(c)(3) organization accepts grants/donations on
  FFN's behalf and re-grants the funds, unlocking grants that require
  determination-letter proof without waiting on FFN's own 1023 filing.
  Typical cost: the sponsor takes an administrative fee, commonly 1-15%
  of funds raised through them — not free, worth weighing against just
  filing Form 1023 directly.
- Not pursued or recommended yet — flagged in `funding-rules.md` as a
  strategic decision for Brooke. Not an active work item, but Brooke
  asked 2026-08-17 whether local options even exist, so a first look was
  done (research only, no outreach sent):
  - **No confirmed fiscal sponsor located in Gilchrist/Alachua/North
    Central Florida.** Checked the Community Foundation of North Central
    Florida (CFNCF) directly — their site describes "agency funds" (an
    endowment tool for orgs that already have their own 501(c)(3)), not
    fiscal sponsorship for orgs that lack one. Worth a direct call to
    CFNCF anyway (352-367-0060 / office@cfncf.org) — even if they don't
    offer it, a community foundation is likely to know who in the region
    does, if anyone.
  - The **Center for Nonprofit Excellence**, 300 E University Ave Ste
    100, Gainesville (same address as the Greater Gainesville Chamber of
    Commerce) is a nonprofit capacity-building resource — not confirmed
    as a fiscal sponsor itself, but another reasonable place to ask.
  - The **National Fiscal Sponsor Directory** (fiscalsponsordirectory.org)
    lists real Florida fiscal sponsors, but none found in this pass are
    close: Orange Park, Miami, Pensacola, Winter Garden, West Palm
    Beach — all a significant drive away, which matters given the
    transportation constraint in `partner-outreach/partner-rules.md`.
    A sponsor doesn't strictly need to be local (this can often be done
    remotely/by mail), but a nearby one would be easier to build a real
    relationship with.
  - Bottom line: fiscal sponsorship is not a quick local fix based on
    this pass — nothing found within easy reach. Worth weighing against
    just filing Form 1023 directly when Brooke is ready to revisit this.
- Researched: 2026-08-16, expanded 2026-08-17
