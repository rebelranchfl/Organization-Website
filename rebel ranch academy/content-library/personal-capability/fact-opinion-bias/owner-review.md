## One-screen owner snapshot

### What this research established

The project is stronger than a simple “fact vs opinion” worksheet. The evidence supports teaching people to first identify what kind of claim they are hearing, then evaluate how strong the evidence is, then check where their own bias may be affecting the judgment.

The research now distinguishes:

- factual claims;
- interpretations/inferences;
- opinions/value judgments;
- predictions;
- mixed claims.

It also explains why people can mistake familiarity, prior belief or preferred conclusions for stronger evidence than they actually have.

### Key evidence confidence

**High confidence:**

- systematic cognitive bias can affect judgment;
- people often see bias more easily in others than themselves;
- prior beliefs can influence evidence evaluation;
- repetition can increase perceived truth;
- accuracy-focused attention and “consider the opposite” can improve some judgments.

**Important boundary:**

Bias does **not** automatically make a conclusion false. An opinion is not automatically worthless. A factual claim can still be false or unsupported. RRA should teach people to separate claim type from evidence strength.

### What was added beyond the owner idea

- a five-type claim model;
- source-tracing and fake-corroboration logic;
