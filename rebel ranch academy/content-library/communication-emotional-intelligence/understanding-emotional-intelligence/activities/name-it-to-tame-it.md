# Activity — Name It to Tame It

**Purpose:** Practice the two moves the research actually supports — naming a feeling precisely, and reappraising instead of suppressing it — using a real moment from your own life instead of a hypothetical.

**Based on:** Gross (1998) reappraisal-vs-suppression findings; RULER's recognize → understand → label → express → regulate structure (see `sources.md` #9, #10).

**Materials:** Nothing but this sheet and a few minutes of honest thinking. No special tools, app, or purchase required.

**Safety note:** This is a reflection activity, not therapy. If the moment you're thinking of involves ongoing distress, conflict, or safety concerns beyond a normal hard feeling, please talk to a trusted adult or a qualified professional instead of relying on this worksheet.

## Steps

1. **Recognize.** Think of one real moment in the last week or two where you felt something strong — frustration, embarrassment, anxiety, anger, disappointment. Write one sentence describing what happened.

2. **Label precisely.** Don't settle for "mad" or "bad." Was it actually frustration, or was it hurt? Was it anger, or was it fear wearing anger's clothes? Pick the most accurate word you can.

3. **Understand.** Did the feeling change or build during the moment? (Example: nervous → frustrated → resigned.) Write the sequence if there was one.

4. **Identify which of the four abilities was weakest in that moment** (Perceive / Use / Understand / Manage — see the main lesson). Naming the weak spot is more useful than a vague "I should control my emotions better."

5. **Reappraise it, in writing, right now.** Write one alternative way of seeing the situation that would have lowered the intensity of the feeling — not a fake-positive spin, a genuinely plausible different read on what happened. (Example: "They cut me off in the meeting" → "They were probably worried we'd run out of time, not trying to dismiss me.")

6. **Predict.** If the research is right, what should be different next time you reappraise instead of suppress? Write your prediction down.

7. **Test it for real.** The next time a strong feeling shows up, try the reappraisal move in the moment instead of after the fact. Come back and note: did the felt intensity actually drop? Do you remember the moment more clearly than usual, or less?

## What this activity can show
That deliberately reframing a situation in the moment changes how a specific feeling plays out for you, personally, once.

## What this activity cannot prove
One personal experiment does not prove the general research finding — it demonstrates the technique, it doesn't replace the meta-analytic evidence already cited in the main lesson. Don't treat one good (or one bad) result as final proof either way.

## Transfer question
Pick one of the REBEL RANCH PRINCIPLE areas from the main lesson (family, work, money, communication) and write one sentence about where this same recognize-before-you-react move would help you outside of the exact moment you just analyzed.
