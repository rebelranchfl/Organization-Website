## 1. Design the product around the learner's actual situation

The owner identified a major opportunity for an interactive water decision system rather than one generic guide.

The Product Design Agent must evaluate an interactive scenario tool that can narrow recommendations based on inputs such as:

- water source: private well, municipal/city, pond, rainwater, stored water, creek/other;
- intended use: drinking, cooking, household, animals, irrigation, emergency, educational demonstration;
- known test information;
- unknown test information;
- observed issues;
- rural/suburban/urban/farm/homestead/off-grid context;
- desired setup: countertop, portable, whole-home, 5-gallon, 55-gallon/outdoor, automated animal watering, emergency backup;
- available materials/resources;
- budget;
- skill level;
- electricity/pump/gravity availability;
- desired independence, convenience, portability, automation, or maintenance level.

The output should not begin with a branded product recommendation. It should narrow toward:

```text
user situation
→ known facts
→ important unknowns
→ tests/measurements needed
→ treatment jobs/functions required
→ available resources
→ viable substitutions/forms
→ system architecture
→ safety/verification requirements
→ implementation options
→ cost/labor/maintenance tradeoffs
→ upgrade paths
→ next learning/resource path
```

## 2. Testing is a prerequisite opportunity and a learning/product funnel

The owner explicitly identified the problem:

> If a learner does not know what is in the water, they do not yet know what needs to be removed, reduced, disinfected, or otherwise treated.

The Product Design Agent must research and map:

- practical at-home screening options;
- low-cost test kits and what they can/cannot responsibly indicate;
- when laboratory or professional testing is the more appropriate path;
- test-result interpretation needs;
- how a testing resource can feed into treatment/purification learning;
- how a purification learner can be routed backward to testing when the necessary water information is unknown.

Potential RRA opportunity paths to evaluate include:

- free `Know Your Water` foundation;
- interactive `What Should I Test?` selector;
- test-kit comparison/selection guidance;
- test-results interpreter;
- private-well testing guide;
- testing-to-treatment decision path;
- maintenance/retesting schedule resource;
- ethical commerce/affiliate opportunities for testing products or services when justified by evidence and owner-approved release strategy.

No specific commercial product may be recommended merely because it is inexpensive or widely sold. The research must evaluate accuracy, scope, limitations, suitability, and the consequence of being wrong.

## 4. Owner scenario — rural private well + farm + pond backup

Use the following as a concrete scenario for product-design testing, not as a universal prescription.

### Context

- rural/private well;
- not municipal/city water;
- no current treatment plant/municipal treatment context;
- current water contents/contaminants are not known from recent testing;
- household wants a countertop drinking/cooking solution;
- farm needs animal-water solutions;
- property has sand, vegetation, rocks, 55-gallon drums/storage containers, and ordinary reusable containers;
- pond can be siphoned as an emergency source if the well/pump fails;
- owner values low-cost, locally available, reusable resources and practical independence.

### Product questions

The agent must distinguish at least:

1. **Normal household well-water path** — what must be known/tested before treatment architecture is selected?
2. **Countertop household path** — what functions are needed, what can be locally sourced/reused, and what components are worth buying because performance/safety justifies them?
3. **Animal-water path** — what treatment/storage/distribution requirements differ from human potable-water requirements?
4. **Automated chicken-watering example** — how can storage + rain/well collection + appropriate treatment + gravity/lines/float or similar control create a low-attention watering system?
5. **Pond emergency path** — treat this as a different source/risk problem from private-well water; do not reuse the well-water prescription without diagnosis.
6. **Storage/container path** — teach the function `contain/store` before defaulting to new purchased containers; evaluate suitability of reused containers for the intended use.
7. **Scale translation** — show how the same functional architecture can translate between a jug, 5-gallon bucket, 55-gallon drum, larger tank, or other suitable container while capacity/safety requirements change.

## 5. Consumerism inversion

The water product should deliberately teach the opposite of product-first consumer thinking.

The design question is:

> **What function needs to happen, and what do you already have that can responsibly perform it?**

For each build/system component, evaluate:

- USE WHAT YOU HAVE;
- REUSE / REPURPOSE;
- SOURCE LOCALLY;
- WORTH BUYING;
- OPTIONAL UPGRADE;
- DO NOT SUBSTITUTE.

The product must explain why a purchase is necessary when recommending one.

The learner should understand that a 55-gallon drum, 5-gallon bucket, purpose-built tank, or suitable reused jug may all serve the abstract function `contain/store`, while material suitability, contamination risk, capacity, durability, pressure, temperature, and intended use determine whether a specific container is appropriate.

## 7. Think Like a Rebel — water application

The water product is the first major test of the Academy framework:

> **Understand what you are actually trying to do. Break it down. Question assumptions. Understand the function. Use what you have. Find another way. Test it. Learn from it. Apply that thinking somewhere else.**

The Product Design Agent must show how the learner's thinking changes beyond water.

Examples of transferable abstractions include:

- container = hold/store;
- hose/pipe/channel = move;
- gravity/pump = create movement/pressure;
- valve/float/control = regulate/automate;
- screen/filter medium = separate according to a particular mechanism;
- test/measurement = reduce uncertainty;
- maintenance = preserve performance;
- redundancy = improve resilience.

The final product should help the learner see functions and systems rather than only named objects.

## 10. Safety/evidence boundary remains controlling

This addendum increases product-design depth but does not authorize unsupported drinking-water claims.

For human potable-water applications:

- unknown contamination remains unknown until appropriately assessed;
- a visually clear result is not proof of potable water;
- a DIY demonstration is not automatically a validated treatment system;
- treatment jobs must match the problem;
- verification/testing requirements must remain explicit;
- any new subject claim not already supported by the approved Revision 3 evidence must be routed through a targeted research gap before being taught as fact.

This project should teach resourcefulness **and** responsible verification.
