Show me the science: clear water is not the same as safe water
Cloudiness is often described technically as turbidity. It can matter for treatment performance, including UV, but a clear appearance does not prove that germs or dissolved contaminants are absent. Testing and verification have to match the claim you want to make.

5. Build My Water Treatment Plan

Treatment is a set of jobs. A product is only one possible way to perform a job. This plan is built from your source, use, and evidence.

The order matters

Source + use → what we know → what is still unknown → required jobs → safe forms → verify → maintain.

What matters most in your setup?
Technical details: where UV fits
UV is a germ-control job (microbiological disinfection/inactivation), not general purification. For drinking water, the approved boundary is to use a validated/certified safety-critical reactor appropriate to the identified job. Incoming water may need pretreatment/clarity control. Check the exact NSF/ANSI 55 class, model/configuration, certified flow, point-of-use versus point-of-entry need, power/alarm behavior, maintenance instructions, and downstream recontamination risk. UV does not remove sediment, chemicals, dissolved metals, or salts.
See UV, Class A/B, POU/POE, and treatment-train visuals
What happens if this stops working?
A real plan includes failure. Think about empty storage, pump or power loss, blocked lines, dirty filters, failed controls, alarms, contamination events, and what backup keeps the intended use covered. For high-stakes drinking-water treatment, verification has to match the claim.
