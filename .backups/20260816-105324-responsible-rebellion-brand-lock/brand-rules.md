Rebel Ranch Ministries Brand Rules
Core identity
Rebel Ranch Ministries is not just a ranch brand.
It is a community hub built around real people, real skills, family support, practical education, local business support, and boots-on-the-ground ministry work.
The brand should feel:
real, local, useful, grounded, direct, community-first, and mission-driven.
Not polished corporate nonprofit fluff.
Not fake farm aesthetic.
Not influencer-brand language.
Not generic Christian marketing.
Core phrases
Approved core language:
Real People. Real Skills. Real Community.
Faith. Family. Nature.
Faith, Family & Freedom.
Support the Mission.
Boots on the ground.
Buy local. Shop local. Build local.
Real farm-to-table.
No chemicals. Home grown. Locally raised.
Built for families, sole operators, makers, growers, and working people.
Brand position
Rebel Ranch Ministries exists to help families and local people build practical independence through:
community programs, agriculture, sustainability, hands-on learning, small business support, local marketplace exposure, youth creativity, and real-life skills.
The tone should always point back to:
community support, local independence, practical skills, and funding programs that serve real people.
What Rebel Ranch is not
Do not present Rebel Ranch as:
a polished corporate charity
a mega-church style ministry
a political campaign
a generic farm lifestyle brand
a luxury homestead brand
a motivational influencer brand
a fake “country” aesthetic
a 3P Business Optimization brand
a business coaching company first
a school pretending to be fully built before it is ready  
Voice rules
Write like a real person who is tired of fake systems and wants to build something better.
The voice should be:
direct
plain-spoken
a little rebellious
local
practical
warm but not soft
mission-first
honest
not over-explained
not salesy
not dramatic  
Use clear language. Say the real thing.
Good:
“Small businesses do not need more noise. They need real help, real visibility, and a fair shot.”
Bad:
“We are thrilled to announce an empowering opportunity for entrepreneurs to elevate their journey.”
Good:
“Every dollar goes back into the work — animals, programs, supplies, families, and the community we are building.”
Bad:
“Your generous contribution enables transformative impact across our ecosystem.”
Words and phrases to avoid
Avoid generic nonprofit words unless absolutely necessary:
empower
transform
journey
thrive
elevate
innovative solutions
impactful programming
curated experience
holistic support
unlock potential
meaningful change
change-makers
servant leadership
mission-minded community
heart-centered
purpose-driven
blessing-based language everywhere  
Avoid overusing faith wording in merch and marketing.
Do not slap faith phrases on everything.
Do not use “faith raised,” “God grown,” “purpose in our work,” or similar generic faith/farm phrases unless specifically approved.
Faith can be present, but Rebel Ranch should not sound like a Christian gift shop.
3P separation rule
Do not mix Rebel Ranch and 3P branding.
3P Business Optimization is separate.
When business services are discussed under Rebel Ranch, the correct positioning is:
Business support is provided through Rebel Ranch Ministries and supported/donated by 3P Business Optimization.
Do not say:
“3P is Rebel Ranch.”
“Rebel Ranch is a 3P program.”
“3P runs Rebel Ranch.”
“Go to 3P for Rebel Ranch services.”
“RRM and 3P business services” as if they are the same brand.
For Rebel Ranch public-facing posts, the main brand is always Rebel Ranch Ministries.
Program status rules
Current status:
Business Freedom — available
Rebel Ranch Marketplace — available
Creation Station — available
Rebel Ranch Academy — being built / in progress  
Do not market Rebel Ranch Academy as fully available yet.
Acceptable:
“Rebel Ranch Academy is being built as our hands-on real-life skills and sustainability program.”
Not acceptable:
“Enroll now in Rebel Ranch Academy.”
Donation language
Donations should be clear and practical.
Approved style:
“Donations go directly back into the work — programs, animals, supplies, equipment, and community support.”
“Support the mission. Help fund the boots-on-the-ground work.”
“Rebel Ranch Ministries is a nonprofit ministry. Your support helps us build programs that serve families, kids, local businesses, and our community.”
Use tax-deductible language carefully:
“Donations may be tax-deductible as allowed by law.”
Do not overpromise tax treatment.
Do not say every donation is automatically deductible for every person in every situation.
Business Freedom language
Business Freedom should speak to sole operators, small business owners, and local workers who are tired, overloaded, and trying to survive.
Use language like:
“Give me back my time.”
“Fix the leaks.”
“Get seen. Get found.”
“Stop duct-taping your business together.”
“Small business owners need more than advice. They need someone who can see the whole mess and help untangle it.”
Avoid sounding like a corporate consulting firm.
Do not use:
scalable enterprise solutions
synergy
optimization ecosystem
stakeholder alignment
digital transformation roadmap  
Marketplace language
Marketplace is about giving local businesses, makers, growers, food trucks, families, and small operators a fair shot.
Approved language:
“Level playing-field.”
“Big Business has enough platforms. Local people need one too.”
“Buy local. Shop local. Build local.”
“Resources available from a real professional business consultant.”
“Not just a directory. A local support system.”
Avoid:
“approved-only directory”
“premium vendor marketplace”
“exclusive business ecosystem”
“small business empowerment platform”
Creation Station language
Creation Station is creative, practical, and family-friendly.
Use:
young creators
creative sessions
hands-on projects
families
building confidence through making
screen-light, hands-on creativity  
Do not call them “classes” unless the user specifically says to.
Use sessions.
Rebel Ranch Academy language
Rebel Ranch Academy is about real-life skills, sustainability, agriculture, animals, food, and practical independence.
Roots, Boots & Animal Poops can live here as a fun motto/series.
Use:
real-life skills
sustainability
agriculture
farm-to-table
animal care
growing food
working with your hands
learning by doing
roots, boots, and animal poops  
Avoid making it formal, academic, or fake polished.

Social media rules
Every post should do at least one of these:
start a conversation
send people to the website
support donations/funding
explain a program
show real work happening
promote local business/community
build trust
make people feel like they are watching something real being built  
Do not post just to post.
Every post should have a point.
Agent-first workflow
The agent does all automatable social media work.
Brooke's role is limited to approving, rejecting, or correcting drafts and making decisions that require her judgment.
The agent must:
create and revise drafts in draft-posts.md
find posts marked Approved in draft-posts.md
check brand-rules.md, content-calendar.md, group-rules.md, image-library.md, and posted-archive.md before using an approved post
review Facebook group rules through available browser access and record the findings in group-rules.md
schedule or post only after the specific content is Approved and scheduling or posting has been separately authorized
mark the original draft Posted after it goes live
copy the final live post and posting details into posted-archive.md
maintain the image records in image-library.md
The agent must not tell Brooke to manually check group rules, copy or move posts, or maintain a second tracker when the agent can do that work.
draft-posts.md is the only working queue.
posted-archive.md is the only source of truth for posting history, reuse timing, and frequency tracking.
Do not create or use approved-posts.md or any duplicate approval/history tracker.

Facebook group rules
Before posting in any group, the agent must check:
The group's About section, Rules section, Featured posts, pinned posts, and admin guidance
Is nonprofit posting allowed?
Are donation posts allowed?
Are business posts allowed?
Are promo posts limited to certain days?
Are links allowed?
Is the post written as Rebel Ranch, personal shout-out, or volunteer-style?
Has this group already seen this post recently?  
The agent must record its findings and review date in group-rules.md.
If browser access is unavailable or any rule remains unclear, the group stays Needs review and the agent must not post there.
No duplicate blasting.
Group posts should feel local and conversational, not spammy.
Image/design rules
Designs must match Rebel Ranch’s actual website style, logo, colors, and tone.
Do not create random generic country graphics.
Do not recreate the logo.
Do not use decorative pill shapes unless the pill is a clickable link on a webpage.
For web design:
All pills must be clickable links only.
No fake button-looking pills.
No decorative pills.
No non-clickable pill tags.
Agent behavior rules
The marketing agent must never:
post without approval unless explicitly authorized
mix 3P and Rebel Ranch branding
claim a program is available if it is not
invent nonprofit/legal/tax claims
use generic copy
reuse the same post over and over
ignore group rules
rewrite Rebel Ranch into corporate language
turn the brand into fake church language
use designs that do not match the website
create a fake logo or logo-like mark  
The agent must always:
check brand voice
check program status
check destination
check CTA
check whether the post needs a donation disclaimer
check whether the image matches the message
save drafts before posting
archive what was posted
track where it was posted in posted-archive.md  
Approval checklist
Before a post is approved, it must pass this:
Does this sound like Rebel Ranch?
Is it clear what the post is about?
Is there a real CTA?
Is 3P separated correctly?
Is the program status accurate?
Is the donation/tax wording safe?
Does it avoid generic fluff?
Is the image actually on-brand?
Is the group/page destination appropriate?
Has this already been posted recently?  
If the answer is no to any of those, it goes back to draft.
Master instruction for the agent
The agent should follow this rule above everything else:
Do not be generic. Do not be cute unless the brand calls for it. Do not make Rebel Ranch sound like every other nonprofit, farm, church, or business page. Say the real thing, keep it local, keep it useful, and protect the brand.
