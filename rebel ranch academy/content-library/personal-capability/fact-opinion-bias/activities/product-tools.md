# RRA-2026-0003 — Activities & Product Tools

**AI-Agent:** ChatGPT/GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Session:** RRA Product Design Agent

## 1. What Am I Actually Looking At? — free tool

Inputs: one statement + all applicable claim layers + evidence support state.  
Output: a simple two-part result: `What kind of claim?` and `How well supported?`

Success: learner understands that factual type is not the same as proven true.

## 2. Trace the Echo

Learner receives 5–8 posts/headlines/messages that appear to corroborate one claim. Each item includes enough information to trace its origin. Learner connects each item backward until independent roots are visible.

Output:
- number of appearances;
- number of actual independent evidence roots;
- wording-strength changes;
- what new evidence, if any, each repetition added.

## 3. Bias Mirror

Learner chooses a low-stakes disagreement and must complete both sides:

- pressures/assumptions that could affect the other side;
- pressures/assumptions that could affect my side.

No completion state until both sides contain a response.

## 4. What Would Change My Mind?

Learner records:
- evidence that strengthens;
- evidence that weakens;
- evidence that reverses;
- evidence not yet sought.

Output becomes part of My Decision Record.

## 5. The Unanimous Room

Use objective, harmless judgments such as line length, quantity estimates with clear answer, or visual matching.

Sequence:
1. answer privately;
2. record confidence;
3. show unanimous incorrect group answer;
4. answer again;
5. record public answer/private belief distinction;
6. repeat with one dissenter;
7. compare.

Do not shame, rank intelligence, or use emotionally loaded identity topics.

## 6. One Dissenter Changes the Room

Companion activity showing how unanimity changes pressure. Output compares confidence/conformity with and without one dissenting voice.

## 7. Family mixed-claim scenario

Example: “You never listen to me.”

Learner separates:
- observable events;
- interpretation of listening/motive;
- expectation/value;
- emotional experience.

Output: `What can be verified? What requires a conversation rather than proof?`

## 8. Purchase / money scenario

Example: “Everybody in this group says this product saves hundreds, so it must be the best deal.”

Learner traces source/origin, separates testimonials from measurements, identifies missing comparison assumptions and records cost of error.

## 9. Business decision scenario

Example: “Sales dropped because prices are too high, so we need to discount.”

Learner separates metric, customer-belief hypothesis, causal claim and recommendation. Tool requires evidence for each layer before the recommendation can be marked `supported`.

## 10. My Decision Record

Repeat-use output fields:
- exact claim/decision;
- claim layers;
- evidence items;
- source roots and copies;
- assumptions;
- contrary/alternative evidence;
- social-pressure pathways;
- what would change my mind;
- confidence state;
- cost of error;
- next action;
- unresolved questions;
- last updated.

The record is editable. A changed earlier answer should update dependent sections rather than force a new worksheet from scratch.

## 11. Transfer challenge

Learner chooses a different domain and completes a shorter second Decision Record.

Prompt:

> Where else can you separate what happened from what you think it means, inspect the evidence, notice pressure, and make confidence earn its way upward?

## Safety/ethics boundaries

- Use low-risk scenarios for social-pressure experiments.
- Do not impersonate real people or trick participants on sensitive identity/health/political topics.
- Do not turn the tool into a public score of who is “biased.”
- Do not imply that RRA's structured process automatically proves truth.
- For high-stakes specialized decisions, route the learner toward appropriate claim-specific verification rather than generic disclaimers.
