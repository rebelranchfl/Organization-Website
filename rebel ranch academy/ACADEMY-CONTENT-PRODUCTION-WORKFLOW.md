# Rebel Ranch Academy — Content Production & Automation Workflow

**Status:** Owner-approved working standard  
**Program:** Rebel Ranch Academy (RRA)  
**Parent program:** Rebel Ranch Ministries (RRM)  
**Parent organization:** Faith, Family & Nature Church, Inc.  
**Purpose:** Give every human and AI agent one complete workflow for turning an owner idea into researched, source-backed, practical Rebel Ranch Academy learning material that is priced, reviewed, approved, stored, and eventually published without losing the owner's voice or control.

---

## 1. Core operating principle

The owner manages decisions. The system manages work.

The RRA content system should allow the owner to enter a simple idea such as:

> Purifying water with natural materials

The system and assigned AI agent are then responsible for moving that idea through research, content development, material creation, pricing, quality review, owner review, revision, and approved release.

The owner should not have to manually direct every production step.

No material is released simply because an AI agent completed it. Owner approval is the required release gate.

GitHub/repository records are the permanent source of truth. AI memory, chat history, temporary workspaces, and agent-specific state are not the permanent record.

---

## 2. What Rebel Ranch Academy content is

RRA creates practical education for children, teens, adults, families, homesteaders, future and current business owners, and community members.

RRA content is not generic AI copy, disposable worksheets, textbook filler, a collection of internet summaries, or a conventional school curriculum with Rebel Ranch branding added afterward.

RRA material should help people become more capable, confident, responsible, accountable, independent, resilient, thoughtful, and strong.

A completed learning product should help a learner do at least one of the following:

- make a better decision;
- practice a useful skill;
- build, repair, grow, organize, or improve something;
- communicate more clearly;
- understand money, business, systems, people, land, food, or resources;
- become more self-reliant;
- solve a real problem;
- recognize patterns that transfer into other parts of life;
- take accountable action.

The Academy does not stop at "know this." The learner must be able to **do, decide, practice, build, test, compare, apply, or change something** with what was learned.

## 4. The Rebel Ranch Academy voice

The owner is the voice of Rebel Ranch Ministries and Rebel Ranch Academy.

AI agents do not invent a new RRA personality.

The purpose of an AI agent is to research, organize, strengthen, illustrate, test, and produce material in the established RRA voice.

### RRA educational character

RRA should sound and feel:

- plain-spoken;
- intelligent without sounding academic for the sake of sounding academic;
- practical;
- curious;
- polished;
- credible;
- grounded;
- strong;
- responsibly rebellious;
- historically aware;
- evidence-driven;
- willing to question conventional explanations;
- willing to question alternative explanations too;
- respectful without being timid;
- accessible without talking down to people;
- direct about what is known, unknown, disputed, useful, unsafe, or misunderstood.

### RRA should not sound like

- a generic school textbook;
- a corporate training department;
- a government brochure;
- an AI-written listicle;
- a survivalist fear campaign;
- a political rant;
- a children's worksheet company;
- an institutional authority demanding belief;
- an influencer repeating unsupported claims;
- empty motivational language.

### Writing rule

Explain complicated things in normal language without stripping away the important details.

Use examples people recognize from real life.

When a technical term matters, explain it immediately.

Do not use authority, credentials, or complicated language to make weak evidence sound stronger.

Do not write down to the audience. Young learners may need simpler language, but they still deserve real ideas.

## 5. Two non-negotiable intellectual standards

### Authority does not replace evidence.

> **Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.**

A source is not automatically correct because it comes from a government agency, university, museum, large corporation, professional association, news organization, nonprofit, or well-known expert.

Likewise, a claim is not automatically correct because it challenges one of those institutions.

RRA teaches learners to examine evidence rather than simply transfer trust from one authority to another.

### Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.

> **Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.**

Knowledge becomes more valuable when a learner can recognize the underlying principle and use it somewhere else.

A lesson about soil can reveal principles that also apply to business systems. A lesson about emotional regulation can improve family communication, leadership, negotiation, customer relationships, and decision-making. Resource allocation applies to time, money, food, energy, land, labor, and operations.

RRA should deliberately teach these connections instead of presenting subjects as unrelated boxes.

## 6. Required RRA teaching formula

When appropriate to the subject, RRA materials should move through the following questions:

1. **How was this done before modern convenience or the current system?**
2. **Why did it work?**
3. **What knowledge, skill, context, or responsibility was lost or became less common?**
4. **What changed?**
5. **What does current evidence tell us?**
6. **What did older methods get right?**
7. **What did older methods get wrong, or what risks were not understood?**
8. **What should we keep, change, improve, or stop doing?**
9. **How can someone use this knowledge today?**
10. **What can the learner now do, decide, practice, build, test, compare, or change?**

Not every subject needs an artificial historical section. The formula is a thinking framework, not a requirement to force history into a topic where it adds no value.

## 7. Required section: TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE

Every substantial RRA learning project must include a section titled:

# TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE

This section identifies the deeper principle behind the subject and shows where else it applies.

The agent must consider, when relevant:

- Where else does this principle appear?
- How does it apply personally?
- How does it apply within a family?
- How does it apply to communication or emotional intelligence?
- How does it apply to work or business?
- How does it apply to money or resource management?
- How does it apply to leadership or community?
- How does it apply to systems, land, food, tools, or physical resources?
- What other RRA learning area connects naturally to it?
- Where does the comparison stop being valid?

The final question is mandatory when analogy could be misleading. RRA uses connections to deepen understanding, not to pretend two different systems are identical.

## 8. Research standard

Research is mandatory for substantive RRA materials.

The agent must not begin with a conclusion and search only for material that supports it.

The research process should identify:

- what the owner asked to explore;
- what is historically documented;
- what is directly observable or mechanically demonstrable;
- what current evidence supports;
- what laws, rules, standards, manuals, or source documents actually say;
- where sources agree;
- where sources conflict;
- what remains uncertain;
- what common claims are poorly supported;
- what commonly dismissed claims have credible supporting evidence;
- what practices may have value but also carry risk;
- what has changed over time and why.

### Go to the source

Whenever practical, use the closest available source rather than an interpretation of the source.

Examples:

- For tax requirements, use the Internal Revenue Code, Treasury Regulations, IRS instructions, forms, publications, manuals, circulars, revenue procedures, notices, rulings, and other directly relevant primary materials.
- For credit and consumer finance, use the governing statutes, regulations, CFR provisions, regulator manuals, official interpretations, enforcement materials, court decisions when relevant, and actual contract terms.
- For business rules, use statutes, regulations, licenses, governing forms, official instructions, contracts, standards, or direct system documentation.
- For historical topics, seek period manuals, newspapers, diaries, photographs, patents, catalogs, trade publications, agricultural bulletins, archived records, and other primary materials where available.
- For scientific or technical topics, seek original studies, test data, standards, technical manuals, replicated findings, and directly relevant evidence.
- For products, tools, systems, or procedures, use original manufacturer documentation or technical specifications when the claim concerns how that item actually works.

Secondary sources are useful for context, interpretation, discovery, and comparison, but they should not replace an accessible primary source merely because the secondary source is easier to read.

### Corroboration

Important claims should be checked against more than one independent source or line of evidence when practical.

A high-impact claim should not rely on one institution, one study, one article, one expert, one historical record, or one viral claim when other evidence can reasonably be found.

Corroboration should be especially strong for claims involving:

- health or physical safety;
- drinking water;
- food preservation;
- animals;
- chemicals;
- electricity, gas, fire, tools, structures, or mechanical hazards;
- taxes;
- credit;
- money;
- legal obligations;
- children's safety;
- claims likely to materially affect someone's finances or decisions.

## 9. Source evaluation — no prestige hierarchy

RRA does not use a blanket rule that government, university, museum, media, corporate, nonprofit, or professional sources automatically outrank all others.

Evaluate each source based on:

1. **Proximity** — How close is this source to the original evidence, law, event, data, system, or record?
2. **Transparency** — Can we see how the claim was reached?
3. **Corroboration** — Does independent evidence support it?
4. **Relevance** — Does the source actually answer the claim being made?
5. **Date and context** — Is it current enough, or historically appropriate?
6. **Method** — If research is involved, how was the conclusion produced?
7. **Conflicts and incentives** — Does the source have interests that should be understood?
8. **Specificity** — Is the source speaking directly to the issue, or being stretched beyond what it actually says?
9. **Reproducibility or verifiability** — Can another person inspect, test, calculate, locate, or verify the claim?
10. **Limitations** — What does the source not prove?

Institutional misconduct, conflicts of interest, changing guidance, historical error, or proven deception may be relevant context and may affect how heavily a source is relied upon. Those concerns should be documented with evidence rather than assumed.

RRA does not replace blind trust in institutions with blind distrust of institutions.

## 10. Mandatory source record for owner review

Every researched RRA project must contain a source record that the owner can review directly.

At minimum, `sources.md` must include a table with:

| Field | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Claim / topic | What fact, rule, history, method, or concept is being supported |
| Source title | Exact source or document name |
| Source type | Law, regulation, manual, primary record, study, technical documentation, secondary analysis, etc. |
| Direct link | Clickable URL to the source when publicly available |
| Date | Publication/effective/event date when relevant |
| What it supports | Exact reason the source is being used |
| Source proximity | Primary/direct, near-primary, or secondary |
| Corroboration | Other source(s) that support or challenge it |
| Conflict / limitation | Known disagreement, limitation, bias, uncertainty, or scope issue |
| Used in final material? | Yes / No / Background only |
| Owner source decision | Approved / Do not use / Needs replacement / Pending |

The owner must be able to click the links and inspect the evidence.

If a source is inaccessible, paywalled, removed, or not publicly linkable, record enough bibliographic information to identify it and explain why it was used.

The agent must never hide a meaningful source conflict simply because it complicates the final story.

## 11. Lost knowledge standard

"Lost knowledge" is not permission to present folklore, nostalgia, conspiracy, or an old practice as fact simply because it is old, unusual, suppressed, forgotten, or outside modern convention.

When exploring lost or less-common knowledge, separate:

- **Documented historical practice** — evidence shows people actually did this.
- **Documented historical belief** — evidence shows people believed this, which does not prove it was true.
- **Mechanically or scientifically supported practice** — evidence explains why it works.
- **Partially supported practice** — some evidence exists but important questions remain.
- **Unsupported or disputed claim** — evidence is weak, conflicting, or absent.
- **Unsafe historical practice** — historically real but not appropriate to recommend today.

RRA can teach all of these categories when educationally useful. The learner should understand the difference.

## 12. Product-development workflow

The standard workflow is:

**IDEA → CONTEXT REVIEW → RESEARCH → CONCEPT → CONTENT → MATERIALS → PRICING → QA → OWNER REVIEW → REVISION OR APPROVAL → RELEASE → LIVE**

### Stage 1 — Idea intake

The owner may submit as little as a title or idea.

Example:

> Purifying water with natural materials

Optional owner input may include:

- a question;
- a personal observation;
- an audience;
- a historical angle;
- a claim to investigate;
- a desired type of material;
- a connection to another RRA area;
- a tone note;
- a source the owner wants reviewed;
- a source or category the owner does not want used.

The agent must not require the owner to write the research brief the agent is supposed to develop.

### Stage 2 — Context review

Complete Section 3 before research.

### Stage 3 — Research

Create the research record and source table before writing polished teaching copy.

Research should be broad enough to reveal conflicts and missing context, not merely confirm the first plausible answer.

### Stage 4 — Concept proposal

Before expensive production work, the agent defines:

- working title;
- learning area;
- intended audience;
- learning outcome;
- central question;
- major principles;
- historical/lost-knowledge angle if relevant;
- modern-use angle;
- TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE connections;
- proposed format;
- proposed activities/interactivity;
- visual or illustration needs;
- safety or professional-boundary issues;
- proposed free/paid role in the Academy library.

### Stage 5 — Content development

Build the master educational content from the approved evidence base.

The material should include realistic examples and practical use, not just explanation.

### Stage 6 — Material production

Create the approved delivery assets, which may include:

- illustrated field guides;
- practical guides;
- intelligence manuals;
- family learning guides;
- workbooks;
- field journals;
- challenge packs;
- decision trees;
- interactive activities;
- printable experiments;
- comparison sheets;
- checklists;
- diagrams and cutaways;
- timelines;
- historical-source panels;
- videos;
- audio;
- on-site interactive lessons;
- deeper collections or bundles.

Do not call every product an ebook.

### Stage 7 — Pricing proposal

Create a pricing recommendation and explain why.

### Stage 8 — Quality assurance

The agent must check evidence, links, calculations, tone, readability, transferability, safety, practical usefulness, brand alignment, and completeness before requesting owner review.

### Stage 9 — Owner review

Status becomes `READY FOR OWNER REVIEW`.

The owner chooses:

- `APPROVED`
- `NEEDS MORE WORK`
- `REJECTED`

The owner may leave comments with any decision.

### Stage 10 — Revision

If `NEEDS MORE WORK`, the agent reopens the project using the owner's comments as binding revision input.

The previous review and comments remain in the record. Do not erase review history.

When revision is complete, return to `READY FOR OWNER REVIEW`.

### Stage 11 — Release

Only an `APPROVED` owner decision permits the release workflow to begin.

Approval of educational content does not automatically authorize unrelated website edits, new systems, purchases, third-party accounts, or other scope expansion.

### Stage 12 — Live record

When the item is actually live, record:

- live URL or delivery location;
- approved price;
- launch date;
- version;
- final files;
- approval record;
- source set used;
- later corrections or updates.

## 15. Project storage standard

Materials are stored as a complete project, not as one giant file and not scattered across unrelated folders.

Recommended structure:

```text
rebel ranch academy/
└── content-library/
    └── <learning-area>/
        └── <project-slug>/
            ├── project-record.*
            ├── concept.md
            ├── context-review.md
            ├── research.md
            ├── sources.md
            ├── content-outline.md
            ├── master-content.md
            ├── pricing.md
            ├── qa-review.md
            ├── owner-review.md
            ├── revision-history.md
            ├── illustrations/
            ├── activities/
            ├── working-files/
            └── final/
```

The exact machine-readable format for `project-record.*` is an implementation decision for the dashboard project. Do not invent competing formats once that format is approved.

### Why the full project stays together

A future agent should be able to determine:

- where a factual claim came from;
- why a source was trusted or rejected;
- why a product was structured a certain way;
- why it was priced as it was;
- what the owner asked to change;
- which revision fixed the problem;
- what was finally approved;
- what is currently live.

## 16. Delivery strategy

RRA should not become a random digital-download shop.

The Academy website is the learning home. Downloads, interactive materials, videos, activities, classes, workshops, and future guided experiences should feel like parts of one Academy system.

Different subjects may require different delivery.

Possible delivery types include:

- free public sample activities;
- printable PDF resources;
- screen-first illustrated guides;
- interactive web activities;
- field experiments;
- family learning activities;
- downloadable workbooks;
- recorded demonstrations;
- short practical courses;
- guided workshops;
- deeper learning collections;
- live or in-person experiences when separately approved.

The format should be chosen because it teaches the subject well, not because one format is easiest for the agent to generate.

## 17. Pricing framework

Pricing is proposed by the agent and approved by the owner.

The agent must not automatically price a product based only on page count or choose a familiar digital-download price without analysis.

### Current working ladder

This is a starting framework, not automatic pricing:

| Level | Working range | Typical role |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Free | $0 | Genuine sample, introduction, public activity, trust-building resource |
| Focused resource | $7–$15 | Mini-guide, focused activity pack, narrow practical resource |
| Core resource | $19–$39 | Substantial illustrated guide, workbook, field guide, applied resource |
| Deep collection | $49–$89 | Multiple coordinated resources, deeper progression, activities, media, tools |
| Guided/advanced | $99–$199+ | Workshops, guided programs, substantial advanced learning experiences |

### Pricing analysis must consider

- depth and quality of original research;
- practical value to the learner;
- amount and quality of original teaching material;
- illustrations and custom visual explanation;
- interactive components;
- activities or experiments;
- video/audio/demonstration components;
- audience breadth;
- family/multi-age usefulness;
- durability and repeat usefulness;
- competing and comparable offerings;
- production effort/cost;
- whether the product is meant to generate revenue directly or lead into deeper Academy learning;
- whether RRA gains more mission and marketing value by making it free;
- whether pricing is appropriate to RRM's audience and sustainability goals.

### Pricing record

`pricing.md` should contain:

- proposed price;
- proposed product level;
- what is included;
- comparable alternatives reviewed, with links where available;
- why this material is more, less, or similarly valuable;
- free-versus-paid analysis;
- bundle opportunities;
- family-use considerations;
- agent recommendation;
- owner-approved final price.

RRA should not default to underpricing simply because it is ministry-based or mission-driven. Marketing and sustainable revenue are first-order design considerations under RRM's governing rules.

## 18. Free material standard

Free does not mean low quality.

A free RRA resource should be useful enough that a learner can gain something real from it.

Free material may be used to:

- demonstrate how RRA teaches;
- build trust;
- introduce a deeper subject;
- answer a common question;
- allow someone to practice a small skill;
- bring people into the Academy hub;
- create interest in a larger learning collection.

A free product should not intentionally withhold the one fact necessary to make the material useful simply to force a sale.

Paid depth should come from greater scope, application, interactivity, guidance, research, tools, progression, convenience, and value—not artificial incompleteness.

## 19. Multi-age and family design

When appropriate, one strong learning product may serve multiple ages without creating three unrelated versions.

Possible structure:

- **Core explanation** — the real concept in plain language;
- **Try It** — accessible hands-on application;
- **Take It Further** — deeper analysis or older-learner challenge;
- **Real-World Application** — adult, household, work, business, or community use;
- **TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE** — connections across other areas of life.

Age changes should adjust complexity, independence, safety, examples, and parent/guardian involvement—not dilute the truth of the lesson.

Any material involving minors must preserve parent or guardian control and appropriate privacy and safety boundaries.

## 20. Quality-assurance gate

Before requesting owner approval, the agent must verify:

### Context

- Required governing documents were reviewed.
- Current RRM and RRA websites were reviewed.
- The correct learning area and program boundaries were preserved.

### Evidence

- Significant factual claims are sourced.
- Primary/direct sources were used where practical.
- Important claims were corroborated where practical.
- Conflicting evidence was documented.
- Links work at review time.
- Sources actually support the claims attributed to them.
- Historical belief is not mislabeled as proven fact.
- Institutional authority is not being substituted for evidence.

### Teaching

- The content explains why, not only what.
- Real examples are included.
- Practical application exists.
- The learner must do, decide, practice, build, test, compare, or change something.
- TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE is included where required.
- Analogies identify their limits when necessary.

### Voice

- The material sounds like RRA.
- The language is plain-spoken and intelligent.
- It is not generic AI prose.
- It is not institutional, preachy, childish, fear-driven, or artificially rebellious.

### Product

- The chosen delivery format fits the subject.
- Visual/illustration needs are addressed.
- The material is useful on its own.
- Pricing has a written rationale.
- The final package is stored in the project folder.

### Safety and boundaries

- Professional, legal, tax, financial, medical, veterinary, food-safety, physical-safety, and similar boundaries are handled accurately for the subject.
- A safety boundary does not become a wall of unnecessary disclaimers.
- High-risk instructions are not presented casually or beyond what is appropriate to teach safely.
- Minor privacy and parent/guardian controls are preserved.

## 21. Owner review record

`owner-review.md` must preserve each owner decision.

Recommended structure:

```text
Review #: 1
Date:
Decision: NEEDS MORE WORK
Owner comments:
- Too survivalist.
- Add more documented household history.
- Show how this was used before municipal water systems.
- Strengthen the evidence for what natural filtration can and cannot remove.

Agent response:
- Research reopened.
- Sources added/replaced.
- Sections revised.
- Returned for Review #2.
```

Do not overwrite earlier comments simply because later revisions were approved.

Review history is part of the product's institutional memory.

## 23. Future review and maintenance

Educational material is not automatically permanent once published.

Projects involving changing law, tax rules, credit rules, regulations, safety standards, financial systems, technology, scientific findings, or other time-sensitive material should include a review trigger or review date.

When a material change is identified:

1. mark the affected product for review;
2. identify the specific claim or section affected;
3. update research and sources;
4. revise the material;
5. return meaningful changes to owner review before replacing the live product when owner approval is required.

Historical material may also require correction when better primary evidence is found.

RRA should be willing to correct itself. Credibility is strengthened by transparent correction, not by pretending an earlier version could never have been wrong.

## 25. What agents must never do

Do not:

- start research after reviewing only one source-of-truth document;
- assume an old Academy page is current without checking the live Academy and current governing files;
- create generic filler because the owner supplied only a short idea;
- invent a new RRA voice;
- substitute institutional prestige for evidence;
- substitute distrust for evidence;
- cherry-pick sources to reach a desired conclusion;
- hide meaningful disagreement between credible sources;
- call a historical belief a fact simply because it was widely practiced;
- call every learning product an ebook;
- force every subject into the same delivery format;
- price material without written reasoning;
- publish because the agent believes the material is ready;
- erase owner review comments or revision history;
- merge RRA with Creation Station, Marketplace, Business Freedom, or 3P Help Me;
- reduce Rebel Ranch Academy to agriculture;
- reduce Rebel Ranch Ministries to ranch imagery or ranch-only concepts;
- promise credentials, outcomes, income, legal results, tax results, health results, or other unapproved guarantees;
- treat a dashboard status as owner approval unless the approved workflow explicitly defines that status as the owner's recorded decision.

## 26. Definition of done

A content-development cycle is ready for owner review only when:

1. full context was reviewed;
2. the project record is complete;
3. research is documented;
4. the owner can inspect source links;
5. material claims are supported or clearly labeled as uncertain/disputed;
6. the content follows RRA's teaching formula;
7. the project teaches transferable principles;
8. TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE is complete where required;
9. practical application exists;
10. the material sounds like RRA;
11. the proposed delivery format is justified;
12. proposed pricing is documented;
13. QA is complete;
14. all work is stored together in the project folder;
15. the dashboard status is `READY FOR REVIEW`.

A project is ready for release only when the owner's recorded decision is `APPROVED` and all other repository publishing safeguards have been satisfied.

## 27. Direction summary

Rebel Ranch Academy content should help people understand how the world around them actually works and what they can do with that knowledge.

We research deeply. We go to the source. We compare. We question. We verify. We preserve useful knowledge without romanticizing bad information. We use modern evidence without confusing institutional status with truth. We show people the underlying principle so knowledge can move from one part of life into another.

**Authority does not replace evidence. Proximity to the source, transparency, corroboration, and relevance matter more than institutional prestige.**

**Teach transferable principles, not isolated facts.**

The owner remains the voice and final decision-maker for Rebel Ranch Ministries and Rebel Ranch Academy.

The system handles the work. The owner controls what becomes Rebel Ranch Academy material.
