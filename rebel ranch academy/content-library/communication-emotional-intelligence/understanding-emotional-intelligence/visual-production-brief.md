## Assignment 1 — Four Abilities Diagram

- **Project ID:** RRA-2026-0011
- **Authorized workflow stage:** Visual Production (not yet started)
- **Image ID:** `eq-four-abilities-diagram`
- **Educational purpose:** make the four-branch model concrete and memorable as a sequence/set, not just a bulleted list of words.
- **Learner-facing concept:** Perceive → Use → Understand → Manage, as four connected abilities.
- **Approved factual content the visual must preserve** (source: `sources.md` #1, #2, #3):
  1. Perceive — accurately notice emotion (face, voice, body, self).
  2. Use — let the right mood support the right kind of thinking.
  3. Understand — know how emotions blend/change over time.
  4. Manage — sit with a feeling, then choose the response.
- **Evidence/source reference:** `sources.md` rows #1 (Salovey & Mayer 1990), #2 (Mayer/Salovey/Caruso four-branch model), #3 (APA Dictionary corroboration).
- **Approved manuscript section supported:** `master-content.md`, "What it actually is."
- **Required real-world objects/components:** none required — this is a conceptual/structural diagram, not a scene. A simple four-part flow or wheel is appropriate; a realistic photographic scene is not required here (contrast with Assignment 2).
- **Required sequence/orientation/relationship:** the four abilities should read as related/interacting, not four disconnected boxes — the research (`master-content.md`) presents them as abilities that build on and interact with each other, not a strict one-way pipeline. Do not imply a rigid one-directional order that the source material doesn't support.
- **Prohibited shortcuts/misleading representations:** do not add a fifth ability, do not rename any of the four abilities, do not imply the abilities are personality traits ("some people just have this") rather than practicable skills.
- **Safety boundaries:** none applicable (no hazard content).
- **Required dimensions/aspect/use context:** must be legible at both mobile-width (~400px) and desktop; must not rely on tiny text.
- **Accessibility/legibility needs:** each of the four labels must remain readable without relying on color alone to distinguish them (add shape/position distinction too).
- **Exact repository destination:** `illustrations/eq-four-abilities-diagram.png` (or `.svg`) within this project folder.
- **Labeling/text plan:** exactly the four words/short phrases above — Perceive, Use, Understand, Manage — plus a one-line description each, matching `master-content.md` wording exactly (no paraphrase that could drift from the approved text).
- **Inspection checklist:** all four abilities present and correctly labeled and spelled; no invented fifth element; no implied rigid one-way sequence unsupported by the research; legible at mobile width; no color-only distinction between the four parts.
- **Integration checklist:** embedded in `master-content.md` (or its rendered product form) directly under "What it actually is"; alt text written summarizing the four abilities in plain text for screen readers.

## Assignment 2 — Reappraisal vs. Suppression Comparison

- **Project ID:** RRA-2026-0011
- **Authorized workflow stage:** Visual Production (not yet started)
- **Image ID:** `eq-reappraisal-vs-suppression-comparison`
- **Educational purpose:** make the studied outcome difference between the two regulation strategies visually comparable at a glance, since the whole practical value of the lesson rests on this contrast.
- **Learner-facing concept:** two side-by-side columns/paths — "Reappraisal" and "Suppression" — each showing the same three outcome dimensions.
- **Approved factual content the visual must preserve** (source: `sources.md` #9, Gross 1998 — this is a factual/data visual, not decorative):
  - Reappraisal: lowers felt emotion; does not damage memory of the event; does not raise stress in people around you.
  - Suppression: does NOT lower felt emotion; DOES damage memory of the event; DOES raise stress in you and in people around you.
- **Evidence/source reference:** `sources.md` row #9 (Gross 1998, "The emerging field of emotion regulation").
- **Approved manuscript section supported:** `master-content.md`, "The one technique that's actually been tested."
- **Required real-world objects/components:** a simple, recognizable two-person or one-person social scene is appropriate to ground the comparison in something relatable (e.g., a tense conversation), but the three outcome comparisons themselves must be shown as explicit, labeled facts (icons/checkmarks/x-marks or short text), not left to be inferred from the scene alone.
- **Required sequence/orientation/relationship:** side-by-side (not sequential/before-after) — these are two alternative choices in the same moment, not a timeline.
- **Prohibited shortcuts/misleading representations:** do not invent a numeric statistic (e.g., a fake percentage) that isn't in the source; do not show suppression as "working but with a downside" — the source shows it does NOT reduce the felt emotion at all, which must be represented accurately, not softened.
- **Safety boundaries:** none applicable.
- **Required dimensions/aspect/use context:** legible at mobile width; the three-outcome comparison must not become illegibly small when scaled down.
- **Accessibility/legibility needs:** use both icon/symbol AND text label for each outcome (not color alone) so the reappraisal/suppression distinction survives grayscale or colorblind viewing.
- **Exact repository destination:** `illustrations/eq-reappraisal-vs-suppression-comparison.png` (or `.svg`) within this project folder.
- **Labeling/text plan:** column headers exactly "Reappraisal" and "Suppression"; three outcome rows exactly matching the approved content above (felt emotion, memory, stress on others) — no additional invented outcome dimensions.
- **Inspection checklist:** both columns present and correctly labeled; all three outcome comparisons match the approved facts exactly (especially: suppression must NOT be shown as reducing felt emotion); no invented statistics; legible at mobile width; not color-only.
- **Integration checklist:** embedded in `master-content.md` directly under "The one technique that's actually been tested"; alt text describing the three-outcome comparison in plain text.
- **Deployed-QA checklist:** loads correctly on both desktop and mobile widths in the actual rendered page; no broken image path; alt text present and accurate.

- **Approved factual content the visual must preserve** (source: `sources.md` #7, #8, #1):

  2. **1983** — Howard Gardner splits it into interpersonal and intrapersonal intelligence.

- **Required dimensions/aspect/use context:** legible at mobile width (~400px) and desktop.
