# Activity — Dirty Is Visible. Danger Isn't.

**Status:** Working draft for owner review. Not approved for public release.

## Goal
Build a safe demonstration filter, observe what changes, and practice separating **evidence** from **assumption**.

## Safety rule
Use only simulated dirty water made from safe household materials. **Do not drink the output.** This experiment is not a potable-water test or emergency drinking-water procedure.

## Materials
- clear bottle or transparent filter column
- collection container
- washed gravel
- washed sand
- cloth/filter fabric
- optional commercially sold activated carbon
- clean water
- potting soil
- small leaves or plant pieces
- optional food coloring
- measuring cup
- timer
- notebook or printed observation page

## Build your first design
Draw the order of the layers before assembling them.

Ask:
- Which material should the water meet first?
- Which layer is most likely to catch large debris?
- Which layer is most likely to slow the flow?
- What role is the gravel actually serving?

## Make simulated dirty water
Use clean water plus a small amount of potting soil and plant pieces. A tiny amount of food coloring can be added if you want to observe whether a visible dissolved color behaves differently from suspended dirt.

## The question that matters
**What did you actually prove?**

Circle only what your experiment truly demonstrated:
- The water became clearer.
- Some visible particles were removed.
- Flow changed based on the media.
- The water is free of bacteria.
- The water is free of viruses.
- The water is free of lead or arsenic.
- The water is safe to drink.

The last four claims require evidence you did not collect in this demonstration.

## Change one variable
Run the experiment again, changing only one thing:
- change sand depth;
- reverse two media layers;
- pre-settle the muddy water;
- remove the gravel;
- compare with and without activated carbon;
- change the flow rate.

Record what changed.

## TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE
Pick one non-water problem in your life, family, work or business.

1. What is the visible symptom?
2. What might the real problem be?
3. What evidence would help distinguish the two?
4. What solution are people usually trying?
5. Does that solution actually match the problem?
6. What would you test before committing more time, money or effort?

**Core lesson:** Diagnose before prescribing. Match the solution to the actual problem.
