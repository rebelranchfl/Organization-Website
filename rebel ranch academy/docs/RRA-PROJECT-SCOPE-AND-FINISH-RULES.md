# Rebel Ranch Academy Project Scope & Finish Rules

**Status:** Core RRA project-management rule  
**Applies to:** All RRA course, program, website, tool, lab, content, and curriculum projects.

## Why this exists

RRA projects must reach a usable, publishable, functioning state. Research and development can easily expand without limit, especially when AI agents continue generating new ideas, modules, features, lessons, or supporting materials.

The Academy therefore uses a fixed-scope rule to prevent endless creation without launch.

## Core Rule

**Define the outcome. Fix the scope. Finish the version. Launch it. Improve only after completion.**

New ideas discovered during development go into a **Future Expansion** list unless they are necessary to achieve the already-defined outcome.

They do not automatically expand the current version.

## Definition of Finished

A project is not finished because documents or code were created. It is finished only when the agreed version has reached its defined end state and has been verified end-to-end.

For a course, this generally means:

- the educational outcome is clearly defined;
- the required knowledge is researched and organized;
- the student-facing material exists;
- required activities/labs/worksheets exist;
- the offering is priced and described;
- the course is loaded/published where it is meant to live;
- the actual student path works;
- payment/registration works where applicable;
- the final result has been tested from the user's perspective.

For websites/tools/apps, the same principle applies: no “finished” or “fixed” status without end-to-end verification of the actual user path.

## Version Rule

RRA launches in versions.

A version is allowed to be useful without being exhaustive.

Example:

### Solar Technology & Independent Power v1

The v1 objective is for a student to understand:

**SOURCE → GENERATION → CONTROL → STORAGE → CONVERSION → LOAD**

and be able to answer:

> “I need to keep X running for Y hours. What kind of power system do I need?”

That does **not** require teaching every solar technology, every battery chemistry, every historical energy system, whole-house electrical design, utility-scale solar, every world culture's energy methods, or advanced electrical engineering before launch.

Those topics may become future modules, references, advanced courses, or later versions.

## Solar v1 Fixed Scope

Solar v1 is capped at six areas:

1. **Where power comes from** — solar energy to panel to electricity.
2. **Electricity basics** — volts, amps, watts, watt-hours, AC/DC, only to the depth needed for practical understanding.
3. **Storage** — what batteries do, capacity, charging, usable energy, and safety.
4. **Control and conversion** — charge controllers and inverters: what they do and why.
5. **Loads** — identify/measure consumption and calculate runtime.
6. **Build the solution** — given a need and available equipment, design a small independent-power system.

Historical, global, traditional, and modern knowledge should be woven into these six areas only where it improves understanding. It should not become uncontrolled expansion of v1.

## Expected Solar v1 Size

Target approximately **6–10 hours of actual student learning** depending on age and depth.

Expected deliverables:

- one complete Solar Technology & Independent Power v1 course;
- one concise student reference guide;
- a small set of calculators/worksheets;
- several meaningful demonstrations or activities;
- one final design challenge;
- one sellable/publishable RRA offering.

Do not turn this into a semester-long course unless RRA intentionally changes the scope later.

## Future Expansion List

Ideas that are useful but not required for the current version should be documented under Future Expansion, not inserted into the live scope.

Examples for Solar may include:

- advanced battery chemistry;
- refrigeration-specific power design;
- pumps and water systems;
- wind integration;
- whole-house resilience;
- larger off-grid systems;
- advanced inverter systems;
- historical energy technologies;
- advanced electronics;
- career/trade pathways.

## AI / Agent Rule

Any AI agent working on RRA must actively resist unnecessary expansion.

Do not:

- add lessons merely because more content could be created;
- invent arbitrary modules or sessions without educational need;
- expand scope because research uncovers interesting adjacent topics;
- create additional documents unless they serve a defined deliverable;
- delay launch while pursuing completeness beyond the agreed version.

Do:

- keep a visible current-version scope;
- move nonessential ideas to Future Expansion;
- identify the minimum complete set of deliverables;
- work toward completion and verification;
- stop when the agreed version is complete.

## Full Verification Rule

No project may be labeled **finished, fixed, complete, live, or resolved** without end-to-end proof that the exact intended result works.

For course launch this means, at minimum, verifying:

- content exists where intended;
- links and access work;
- registration/enrollment flow works if used;
- payment flow works if used;
- student materials open correctly;
- activities/resources are accessible;
- final user experience matches the intended version.

Creation is not completion. Publishing is not verification. A successful commit is not proof that the user-facing result works.
