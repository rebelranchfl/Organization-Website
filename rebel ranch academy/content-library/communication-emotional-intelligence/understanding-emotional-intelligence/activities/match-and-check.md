# Activity — Match & Check

**Purpose:** Check that the four abilities and the reappraisal-vs-suppression finding actually landed, using recognition (matching, multiple-choice) instead of open-ended writing. Replaces free-text paragraph answers as the lesson's main "Try It" activity, per owner review 2026-09-14: paragraph-writing activities were rejected as impractical for this audience ("nobody is looking to write in paragraph form").

**Based on:** Salovey, Mayer & Caruso's four-branch model (`sources.md` #1, #2); Gross (1998) reappraisal-vs-suppression findings (`sources.md` #9).

**Format:** Rendered interactively in `visual-production/eq-lesson-preview.html` — this file is the durable authoring record of the activity's content and correct answers, not a second place learners fill it out.

## Part A — Matching: ability → what it looks like in real life

| Ability | Matches to |
|---|---|
| Perceive | Noticing a coworker's jaw tighten right before they say "I'm fine." |
| Use | Feeling a little nervous energy before a presentation and using it to sound alert instead of flat. |
| Understand | Realizing your frustration at a slow driver is really about being late, not about the driver. |
| Manage | Waiting ten seconds before responding to a text that upset you, instead of firing back immediately. |

## Part B — Checkboxes: "Which of these are examples of reappraisal (not suppression)? Check all that apply."

1. ✅ "Someone criticizes your work in front of others. You think: 'They're stressed about the deadline too, this probably isn't really about me,' and the sting fades a little." — **Reappraisal.**
2. ⬜ "Someone criticizes your work in front of others. You feel furious but keep a completely flat, calm face the rest of the meeting." — **Suppression** (feeling unchanged, reaction hidden) — distractor, do not check.
3. ✅ "Your flight gets cancelled. You think: 'This is annoying, but it also means I get an extra night to prepare for tomorrow's meeting,' and your frustration eases a little." — **Reappraisal.**
4. ⬜ "Your flight gets cancelled. You're seething, but you smile and tell the gate agent 'no worries, it happens.'" — **Suppression** — distractor, do not check.
5. ⬜ "You reread a friend's short text three times, decide it definitely means they're mad at you, and spend the day irritated." — **Neither.** This isn't reappraisal — reappraisal changes the read to lower the felt emotion; this locks in the worst-case read and never revisits it. Included so the exercise can't be passed by checking "the nice-sounding ones" — distractor, do not check.
6. ✅ "A teammate takes credit for your idea in a meeting. Afterward, you think: 'They probably didn't even realize it was mine — I'll just mention it privately after,' and the anger drops enough to speak calmly later." — **Reappraisal.**

Correct set: **1, 3, 6.**
