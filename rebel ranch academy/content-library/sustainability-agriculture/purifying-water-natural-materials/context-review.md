## RRA fit
The project should not become a fear-based survival manual. It should teach how water treatment actually works, how people historically approached water safety, what sand/gravel/charcoal can and cannot do, why filtration and disinfection are different, how modern evidence changes older practices, and how the same principles transfer to other systems.

## Important safety boundary
A homemade natural-material filter may improve clarity and may reduce some contaminants or microorganisms depending on its design, but a casual bottle filled with gravel, sand, and charcoal is not proof that water is safe to drink. The finished learning product must clearly separate:
- clarification / sediment removal;
- filtration;
- adsorption;
- biological treatment;
- disinfection;
- chemical contamination;
- validated potable-water treatment.

Hands-on learner activities should use simulated dirty water and must not instruct learners to drink the output of an experimental filter.
