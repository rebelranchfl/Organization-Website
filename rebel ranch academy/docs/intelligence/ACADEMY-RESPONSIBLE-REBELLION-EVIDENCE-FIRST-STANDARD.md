## Purpose

Rebel Ranch Academy exists to turn deep, verified research into practical capability. The Academy must not under-teach legitimate evidence-backed knowledge because a learner has not yet tested every variable in their personal situation, because an institution uses a different default method, or because a topic has a safety/compliance dimension.

The governing rule is:

> **Evidence determines what RRA may teach. Uncertainty determines what RRA may say about the learner's specific situation. Uncertainty does not prohibit education.**

And:

> **Do not overclaim. Do not under-teach.**

## 1. Responsible Rebellion

Responsible Rebellion means:

> **Question assumptions. Understand the function. Study the evidence. Build or compare alternatives responsibly. Test the result. Compare it with the modern/default system. Let evidence decide.**

RRA does not reject modern systems because they are modern and does not accept them merely because they are institutional or conventional. Older, traditional, regional, modern, institutional, commercial, scientific, engineering and field systems may all be studied when relevant evidence exists.

Authority does not replace evidence. Institutional guidance is evidence to evaluate, not unquestionable Academy doctrine.

## 2. Education is not permission-seeking

RRA may teach, show, compare, diagram, explain and demonstrate evidence-backed systems and methods when the research supports them.

A missing learner-specific test does not prevent RRA from teaching:

- how a process works;
- what each component or layer does;
- historical and current uses;
- regional/global variations;
- known strengths and limitations;
- substitutions and resource alternatives;
- modern versus lower-input approaches;
- testing and verification methods;
- build concepts and implementation pathways.

The learner's missing information changes how specifically RRA personalizes the recommendation. It does not erase general knowledge.

## 3. Method knowledge versus learner-specific knowledge

Every substantial practical product should distinguish these two questions:

### What do we know about the method?

This comes from verified research, documented use, engineering/scientific evidence, historical records, field practice and corroborated sources.

### What do we know about this learner's exact situation?

This comes from the learner's source, intended uses, measurements, tests, observations, constraints and choices.

RRA may confidently teach the first while plainly identifying unknowns in the second.

Do not turn an unknown learner variable into a blanket refusal to teach the method.

## 4. Tangible output requirement

A practical Academy product must create useful outputs, not merely organize words into cards.

Where the evidence supports practical implementation, Product Design and Visual Production must consider and normally provide:

- actual system diagrams;
- cutaway/component/layer visuals;
- build concepts;
- historical/modern/regional comparison visuals;
- what-each-part-does explanations;
- alternatives/substitutions;
- low-cost / use-what-you-have / purchased options;
- intended-use branches;
- known limitations;
- experiments or verification activities;
- learner-specific system plans;
- clear next actions.

A paid product whose primary output is generalized text after the learner enters meaningful data fails Product Value QA unless the format genuinely requires text-only delivery.

## 5. Multiple real-world uses must remain multiple

When one source supports materially different jobs, the product must not force the learner to choose one merely because a single-select interface is easier to build.

If a learner needs water for drinking/cooking, animals, irrigation, emergency backup or another materially different use, the system should allow multiple selections when those uses can coexist.

The product should then show:

1. what all selected uses share;
2. where the system branches;
3. why each branch differs;
4. which components can be shared;
5. which functions or verification needs differ;
6. how the learner can inspect each branch without losing the whole-system view.

Complexity belongs underneath the learner experience. The interface should preserve continuity and comprehension even when the underlying plan has multiple branches.

## 6. Water treatment / purification teaching rule

For Water products, RRA should teach the treatment-function space rather than acting as if no useful system discussion can occur without a complete laboratory profile.

Relevant functions may include:

- screening/debris removal;
- settling;
- particle/sediment filtration;
- adsorption;
- microbial reduction/disinfection;
- dissolved-contaminant treatment;
- storage;
- distribution;
- monitoring/testing;
- backup/redundancy.

For each system or stage, teach:

- what problem/job it addresses;
- how it works;
- evidence/historical basis;
- materials/components;
- substitutions used in different times/places when supported;
- where it performs well;
- what it does not address;
- how to test or verify performance when appropriate.

Do not describe one filter or treatment stage as removing everything unless the evidence actually supports that statement.

## 7. Historical, traditional, regional and modern comparison

Historical/traditional methods are legitimate educational material when sourced. Modern methods are legitimate educational material when sourced.

RRA should ask:

- What problem were people solving?
- What materials did they have?
- Why did the method work?
- What did it fail to do?
- What changed with modernization?
- What capability was gained?
- What dependency was created?
- What remains useful today?
- What should not be copied because evidence shows a material problem?

Do not dismiss a system because it is old, traditional or used outside the United States. Do not endorse it merely for those reasons either. Explain it from evidence and function.

## 8. Testing is verification, not permission to educate

Testing belongs naturally in the learning process.

If the learner has not tested, RRA can still teach systems, likely functions, comparisons, source-dependent considerations and low-cost next steps.

Where testing materially changes the decision, explain:

- what the test tells the learner;
- what decision the result changes;
- low-cost/free/local testing pathways when genuinely available;
- home-test versus laboratory limitations;
- what can still be done before the result is known;
- what should wait for the result.

Do not create an endless loop where every useful idea is blocked by testing.

## 9. Responsible experiment pattern

When appropriate, Academy products should turn alternatives into experiments rather than unsupported conclusions.

Use:

`UNDERSTAND → BUILD / IMPLEMENT → OBSERVE → TEST → COMPARE → IMPROVE → DECIDE → TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE`

A recurring learner challenge may use:

> **Does Responsible Rebellion Hold Water?**

Compare, where relevant:

- result before / after;
- quality/performance;
- cost;
- energy dependence;
- consumable dependence;
- maintenance;
- repairability;
- locally available materials;
- capacity;
- reliability;
- waste;
- convenience;
- resilience;
- what worked / what did not.

Permanent rule:

> **A successful build is not proof. Test the result.**

## 10. Safety and disclaimer rule

RRA does not repeat generic legal/compliance disclaimers throughout a product merely because the subject is practical.

The site/product's approved general footer disclaimer handles the broad educational boundary.

Inside learning content, add a short safety warning only when it addresses a **specific, material and reasonably immediate hazard in the action being taught**. The warning should tell the learner what the hazard is and what to do about it. Do not use warning boxes as decoration or as a substitute for teaching.

Product Design should avoid unnecessarily hazardous implementation pathways when a safer educational path can teach the same principle.

Do not let generic legal anxiety suppress evidence-backed education.

## 11. Government, institutional and commercial sources

Government agencies, standards bodies, universities, manufacturers, researchers, historical archives, field practitioners and community/traditional sources may all contribute evidence.

For material institutional positions, state them accurately as that source's standard, conclusion, threshold or recommendation where useful.

Do not silently convert one institution's position into universal Academy doctrine.

Where credible evidence differs, teach the disagreement, evidence quality, assumptions and practical implications rather than hiding the conflict.

## 12. Visual-first implementation

For practical systems, the learner should normally **see the system before being asked to study the system**.

Examples for Water include:

- a cutaway 55-gallon layered filter;
- a 5-gallon gravity filter;
- a settling/storage setup;
- a source-to-use treatment train;
- a well/pump/storage/branch diagram;
- drinking vs animal vs irrigation branch comparison;
- historical material substitutions by region/time;
- old method vs modern method comparison;
- before/test/after experiment flow.

Each important visual should make clear:

- what the learner is looking at;
- what each part does;
- where alternatives exist;
- what the system is meant to accomplish;
- what it does not accomplish;
- what the learner does next.

## 13. Navigation and continuity are product quality

Interactive depth must remain inside a coherent learner journey.

Mandatory:

- no 404 links in the owner-review product;
- `Show me why`, `Show me the science`, `See the system`, and similar depth controls must return to the learner's exact place;
- learner answers must persist through normal navigation/deeper-learning exploration;
- changing earlier answers must update dependent branches/results where practical;
- a deeper explanation must not reset the personalized plan;
- multiple selected uses must remain visible throughout the journey.

Broken navigation, lost learner state, or dead deeper-learning links are Final Product QA failures.

## 14. Paid-value test

Before recommending a paid price, the agent must answer:

> **What work did RRA actually do for the learner that they would otherwise have to research, compare, design, calculate, test, organize or decide themselves?**

The paid product should produce clear capability or output such as:

- an understandable system;
- a usable build/implementation concept;
- a personalized multi-branch plan;
- verified comparison logic;
- reduced research burden;
- materials/functions/substitutions map;
- test/verification plan;
- decision-ready alternatives;
- repeat-use reference value.

If the answer is primarily `we gave the learner more words to read`, the paid-value case is not established.

## 15. Required Product and Final Product QA

Before Product Review and again before Final Product Review, verify:

- Is the evidence-backed solution space actually taught?
- Did uncertainty about the learner's exact situation incorrectly suppress general education?
- Are historical/regional/modern systems compared where valuable?
- Are practical outputs tangible and useful?
- Are multiple simultaneous use cases supported when realistic?
- Can the learner see why branches differ?
- Do visuals teach systems/components instead of decorating text?
- Are substitutions and use-what-you-have options visible where supported?
- Is testing used to verify and refine rather than block education?
- Are specific limitations clear without repetitive disclaimer clutter?
- Are targeted safety warnings limited to genuine immediate hazards?
- Does every deeper-learning link work?
- Does learner state survive deeper navigation?
- Does the personalized output materially reflect learner input?
- Does the product create enough tangible value for any proposed paid price?
- Does the learner get to build/test/compare/learn where the subject supports it?
- Is `TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE` present?

Failure of a material item means the product is not ready for Final Product Review.
