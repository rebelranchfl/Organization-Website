# RRA-2026-0003 — Product Architecture

**Working product family:** Fact, Opinion & Bias  
**Core paid product:** **Think It Through**  
**Free entry tool:** **What Am I Actually Looking At?**  
**Learning area:** Personal Strength & Independence / Personal Capability, with cross-Academy transfer  
**Primary audiences:** teens, adults, parents/homeschool families  
**Secondary audience:** sole owners/leaders  
**Proposed paid price:** $24 one-time — recommendation only, not public/approved  
**AI-Agent:** ChatGPT/GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Session:** RRA Product Design Agent

## Product identity

This is not a media-bias worksheet pack. It is a practical judgment system that helps a learner turn a messy real-life claim or disagreement into an examinable evidence-and-pressure map and a living Decision Record.

## Learner outcome

By the end, the learner should be able to:

- separate factual claims, interpretations, opinions/value judgments, predictions and recommendations;
- understand that claim type and evidence strength are different questions;
- trace repeated claims to source and distinguish independent corroboration from echoes;
- identify assumptions and alternative explanations;
- inspect their own bias pressure as seriously as somebody else's;
- notice social influence through information, belonging, norms and value weighting;
- separate public conformity from private belief where relevant;
- calibrate confidence to evidence;
- choose a next action proportional to uncertainty and cost of error;
- update the decision when evidence changes;
- transfer the method to relationships, money, business, learning, leadership and media.

The product does **not** promise to automatically determine truth, eliminate bias, diagnose a person as biased/manipulative, or settle genuine value disagreements by evidence alone.

## Product structure

### Part 1 — See the difference

**Purpose:** establish the key distinction quickly.  
**Visual:** one ordinary mixed statement broken into colored/connected layers without decorative non-clickable pills.  
**Action:** learner identifies what happened, what it might mean, what somebody values, and what is predicted/recommended.  
**Output:** first claim-layer map.

### Part 2 — Two labels, not one

**Purpose:** separate claim type from evidence strength.  
**Action:** learner gives every factual/inference layer a type plus a support state.  
**Visual:** 2-axis map: `WHAT KIND OF CLAIM?` vs `HOW WELL SUPPORTED?`  
**Boundary:** “factual claim” does not mean “proven true”; “opinion” does not mean worthless.

### Part 3 — SORT

**Purpose:** formalize the first RRA move.  
**Action:** split mixed claims into multiple simultaneous layers.  
**Output:** editable claim stack/map.

### Part 4 — SOURCE

**Purpose:** trace evidence.  
**Action:** add source items and identify proximity/independence.  
**Visual:** source-root map that collapses copied/repeated items into a shared origin.  
**Key lesson:** count independent evidence, not echoes.

### Part 5 — STRESS TEST

**Purpose:** make the mind do something different.  
**Action:** record what strengthens, weakens or reverses the conclusion; consider an alternative explanation; ask whether the same evidence standard would be accepted if it pointed the other way.  
**Output:** challenge map, not a score of personal virtue.

### Part 6 — The room around the decision

**Purpose:** integrate Revision 2 social-influence evidence.  
**Action:** select all applicable pressure pathways: information, belonging, norms, value weighting; compare private and public answer where relevant.  
**Visual:** learner in center with evidence path and social-pressure path kept visually distinct.  
**Boundary:** group agreement does not make a claim false; influence can help or hurt judgment.

### Part 7 — DECIDE

**Purpose:** calibrate confidence and action.  
**Action:** choose current confidence state, cost of error and next action.  
**Output:** living **My Decision Record**.

### Part 8 — Practice it

Includes:

- Trace the Echo;
- Bias Mirror;
- What Would Change My Mind?;
- The Unanimous Room;
- One Dissenter Changes the Room;
- family mixed-claim scenario;
- social-media repeat-claim scenario;
- purchase/money scenario;
- business metric/hypothesis/recommendation scenario.

### Part 9 — TRANSFER THE PRINCIPLE

Learner chooses another domain and runs the method again. Explicit boundary: facts can clarify consequences but do not fully settle values/priorities.

## Free entry tool architecture — What Am I Actually Looking At?

5–8 minute interactive experience:

1. choose a prebuilt statement or enter a low-risk claim;
2. split it into claim layers;
3. label each layer;
4. label support separately;
5. receive a simple plain-language result;
6. optionally continue into Think It Through for source tracing, stress test, social pressure and full Decision Record.

Free is a quality product, not a teaser with missing foundational truth.

## Paid core tangible outputs

The paid product must visibly produce:

- claim-layer map;
- evidence/source map;
- repeated-source/echo map;
- assumptions and alternative-explanation record;
- social-pressure map;
- confidence state per meaningful claim layer where applicable;
- next-action plan;
- living My Decision Record;
- printable/exportable summary;
- scenario practice with feedback;
- repeat-use blank/new decision workflow.

If the learner mostly receives generalized prose after entering answers, the product fails.

## Navigation and state

- `Why?`, `Show me the research`, `See an example`, `Technical terms`, and `Sources` open inline or return to the exact prior position.
- Learner answers persist across depth routes.
- Multiple claim layers, sources and pressure pathways remain multi-select.
- Editing the original claim or layer updates dependent record sections where practical.
- No dead links or route resets.

## Language depth

### Plain default

- “What happened?”
- “What do you think it means?”
- “Where did this claim come from?”
- “Is this another independent source or the same story repeated?”
- “What would make you change your mind?”
- “Is the group affecting what you say, what you believe, or both?”

### Optional technical depth

- inference;
- corroboration;
- myside bias;
- motivated reasoning;
- illusory truth effect;
- conformity;
- descriptive/injunctive norms;
- public conformity/private belief change.

## Safety / professional boundary

No generic disclaimer clutter. Use short targeted guidance when a learner chooses a high-stakes domain: this tool helps organize reasoning; specialized legal, medical, financial or safety-critical decisions may require qualified evidence/professional input appropriate to the claim.

## Paid-value answer

RRA is doing the learner's organization work: decomposition, evidence mapping, source independence, alternative testing, social-pressure separation, confidence calibration and decision recording. That is the value—not more reading.
