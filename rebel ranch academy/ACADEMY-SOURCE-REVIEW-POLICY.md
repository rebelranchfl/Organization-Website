# Rebel Ranch Academy — Source Review Policy

**Status:** Owner-approved working standard  
**Program:** Rebel Ranch Academy (RRA)  
**Purpose:** Define who is responsible for source vetting and how owner source controls work without turning every Academy project into a mandatory line-by-line source approval task.

---

## 1. Core rule

The Research Agent is responsible for source vetting.

The owner is not required to manually approve every source before a project may move forward.

A source appearing in `sources.md` means the agent has already evaluated it for relevance, proximity, transparency, corroboration, limitations, date/context, and fit for the claim being made.

The owner source controls exist as an override and review mechanism, not as a mandatory checklist.

---

## 2. Default source status

Unless the owner changes it, a source may remain owner-status `Pending` while the project moves forward, provided the Research Agent has completed its own source audit and research QA does not identify that source as unresolved or blocking.

`Pending` means:

- the owner has not personally overridden the agent's source decision;
- it does not mean the agent failed to review the source;
- it does not block research approval, product design, visual production, or release by itself.

---

## 3. Owner controls

The owner may use source controls at any time to mark a source:

- `Approved` — owner explicitly accepts the source;
- `Do Not Use` — remove this source from future product use and reopen any dependent claim if needed;
- `Needs Replacement` — agent must find a suitable replacement or revise/remove the dependent claim;
- `Pending` — no owner override; agent-vetted source remains usable unless another rule blocks it.

The owner may also leave a comment identifying a concern about the source, claim, framing, conflict, date, methodology, or relevance.

---

## 4. What the agent must do before a source can be used

The agent must document, as applicable:

- the claim/topic the source supports;
- source title/type/link/date;
- why it is relevant;
- whether it is primary/direct, near-primary, or secondary;
- corroborating or conflicting evidence;
- material limitations or uncertainty;
- whether it is used in final material or background only.

The agent must not use owner `Pending` as permission to skip this work.

---

## 5. Blocking source situations

A source issue becomes a real blocker only when one or more of these are true:

- the agent cannot verify an important claim with sufficient evidence;
- the source materially conflicts with another strong source and the conflict affects the teaching conclusion;
- the source is inaccessible/unverifiable and no adequate replacement or corroboration exists;
- the source is materially outdated for a time-sensitive claim;
- the owner marks it `Do Not Use` or `Needs Replacement` and the dependent claim cannot yet be supported another way;
- a safety, legal, financial, health, regulatory, or other high-impact claim lacks adequate corroboration.

When a source issue is blocking, the agent must identify the exact claim affected and what is required to resolve it.

---

## 6. Research approval behavior

Owner research approval means the owner approves the research foundation as a body of work.

It does not mean the owner personally verified every citation.

The owner may approve the research foundation while many individual source rows still show `Pending`.

That approval relies on:

- the agent's completed source audit;
- the agent's research QA;
- visible conflicts/limitations;
- the owner's ability to override any source if desired.

---

## 7. Product-design handoff behavior

Product Design may begin when:

- research is complete;
- research QA has no blocking source/evidence issue;
- owner research approval is recorded;
- required handoff authorization exists;
- no active research revision is in progress.

Individual owner source approvals are not required.

The product handoff should record the source set as:

`Agent-vetted; owner overrides applied where present.`

If the owner has marked any source `Do Not Use` or `Needs Replacement`, the Product Design Agent must honor those decisions.

---

## 8. Dashboard behavior

The dashboard should continue to show source controls because they are valuable owner controls.

However, the interface should not imply that all sources must be manually approved before the project can proceed.

Recommended owner-facing explanation:

> Sources have already been reviewed by the research agent. These controls are optional. Use them only if you want to approve, exclude, or replace a specific source.

A source count such as `24 Pending` must not be presented as `24 items waiting on you` unless a genuine owner decision is required.

---

## 9. Superseding rule

This policy supersedes any older Academy workflow language that can reasonably be read to require the owner to manually approve every source before research approval, product design, or release.

The permanent standard is:

> **Agent source vetting is mandatory. Owner source-by-source review is optional. Owner overrides are binding.**
