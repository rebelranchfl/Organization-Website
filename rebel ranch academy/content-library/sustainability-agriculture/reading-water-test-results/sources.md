## Source audit conclusion
The source set is adequate for an initial Research gate because the central claims are supported by current direct EPA/Florida public-health/regulatory sources plus direct analytical-method guidance. The project does not rely on a consumer testing company, retailer, blog, affiliate page or one laboratory's marketing interpretation.

## Material evidence conflicts / limits preserved
1. **Public-system standards versus private-well responsibility:** MCLs and MCLGs are valuable reference benchmarks, but the federal enforceable standard applies to public water systems. Private wells are not federally regulated under the SDWA. RRA must not blur that distinction.
2. **MCL versus MCLG:** they answer different questions. MCLG is a health goal; MCL is the enforceable public-system limit/treatment standard and may incorporate feasibility/technology/cost.
3. **Primary versus secondary standards:** an exceedance of a secondary guideline can matter for taste, odor, staining, corrosion or equipment without carrying the same health-regulatory meaning as a primary-standard exceedance.
4. **Non-detect:** a result below a lab's detection/reporting threshold is not evidence of absolute zero. The learner must inspect the lab's qualifier/limit.
5. **Bacteria:** presence/absence results are not interpreted like chemical concentration values. Total coliform is primarily an indicator; E. coli is more directly associated with fecal contamination. Public-system compliance procedures must not be copied as private-well legal requirements.
6. **Units:** comparing a result to a benchmark without converting units can create 1,000-fold errors between mg/L and µg/L.
7. **Exact thresholds:** current values must be checked at time of learner-facing publication for any contaminant used as a worked example.
