# Rebel Ranch Ministries Partner Outreach Rules

## Purpose

This file governs how the outreach agent researches, drafts, and tracks
contact with local businesses, community organizations, chambers of
commerce, and local events/happenings on behalf of Rebel Ranch Ministries.

Read `docs/non-negotiables.md` and `marketing/social-media/brand-rules.md`
before drafting anything here. This file does not repeat brand voice
rules already locked there — it only adds outreach-specific rules.

## Transportation constraint — locked 2026-08-16

Brooke has limited transportation. This changes how outreach should be
framed and prioritized, not just worded:

- Default every outreach message to phone, email, mail, or online
  contact. Do not suggest or imply an in-person visit ("stop by," "come
  by the office") as the easy option — it may not be easy at all.
- For opportunities that require physical presence (a festival booth, an
  in-person Chamber meeting, delivering goods to a location), do not drop
  the opportunity, but flag it explicitly in the entry's Notes as
  requiring transportation, so Brooke can decide whether it's workable
  (arranging a ride, sending someone else, or passing on it) before
  approving.
- When researching local businesses/organizations, prefer noting real
  phone numbers, email addresses, and online contact forms over
  addresses alone — a physical address without a remote contact method is
  a weaker lead and should be marked as needing more research rather than
  treated as ready to use.

## What this is not

This is not fundraising. Grant and funding outreach lives in
`marketing/funding/` and follows different rules. Do not mix a funding ask
into a partner/community outreach message unless Brooke specifically asks
for a combined message.

## Locked language rule

Per `docs/non-negotiables.md`: use **"align"** or **"align with,"** not
"partner" or "partner with," in any operational language — internal notes,
outreach message drafts, and tracker entries alike. RRM is positioned as
the connective hub; other local businesses/orgs are not legal partners.
Exception: plain-language phrases like "partner with us" are still
approved as a public-facing CTA per `brand-rules.md` — that CTA wording is
locked and does not change. The "align not partner" rule governs
*internal/operational* language about the relationship itself, not that
one approved CTA phrase.

## Who counts as a good outreach target

- Local businesses in Rebel Ranch's actual service area (Gilchrist,
  Alachua, Levy, and Dixie counties, per the area already mapped in
  `marketing/social-media/group-rules.md`) that fit Marketplace, Business
  Freedom, or general community alignment.
- Chambers of commerce and local business associations — high-value
  because one relationship opens visibility into many local businesses at
  once.
- Local event organizers (festivals, farmers markets, fairs) where Rebel
  Ranch could have a booth, table, parade entry, or vendor presence for
  Marketplace, Creation Station, or general mission visibility.
- Community organizations whose mission naturally overlaps (youth
  programs, food/agriculture groups, homeschool groups, small-business
  support groups) — candidates for cross-promotion, not funding asks.

## Mutual exchange, not a handout — locked 2026-08-16

Brooke's direction, exact: "we have services we can offer, we have
services we need... things can be mutually beneficial. im not just
asking for handouts." Every outreach message in this track must show
both sides of the exchange — what Rebel Ranch offers, and what Rebel
Ranch needs — not a one-sided ask.

**Rebel Ranch can offer:**
- Visibility through Marketplace, social channels, and the website
- Cross-promotion — sharing their business/event to our audience and
  inviting the same in return
- A donation acknowledgment letter for money, supplies, or equipment
  given (see donation-language rule below)
- Being featured as a trusted local supplier/partner in Academy or
  Animal Husbandry educational content, where genuinely relevant
- Practical business help through Business Freedom, where relevant

**Rebel Ranch may need, depending on the target:**
- Money (see donation-language rule below)
- Supplies and equipment — feed, fencing, tools, building materials
- Vehicles or powersports equipment (trucks, ATVs/UTVs) for
  organizational/ranch use
- Food/catering support for events or volunteer appreciation
- Services a business is positioned to give in kind

This is not the festival-vendor-booth model. **Do not draft or suggest
outreach that positions Rebel Ranch as a vendor at a booth or table** —
Brooke rejected that framing explicitly on 2026-08-16. See
`outreach-archive.md`/rejected entries in `outreach-queue.md` for the
specific prior draft this overrides.

## Donation-language rule for money/equipment/supply asks

Any outreach message that asks a business for money, equipment, or
supplies must follow the exact approved tax/status language in
`marketing/funding/funding-rules.md` — the same rules that govern grant
applications apply here. In short: never say "we are a 501(c)(3)"; use
"tax-exempt church under Section 508(c)(1)(a)... donations may be
tax-deductible as allowed by law"; offer a donation acknowledgment letter
rather than a blanket tax-deduction promise.

## Tone rules

Same voice as `brand-rules.md` — direct, plain-spoken, local, not
corporate. A chamber of commerce or a festival organizer should read this
and think "real local person/org reaching out," not "form letter from an
organization that wants something."

Good:
"We're building Rebel Ranch Marketplace to give local makers and small
businesses a place to be seen. Would love to have Gilchrist County
businesses in it — happy to talk about what that looks like."

Bad:
"We are excited to explore a strategic partnership opportunity that could
create synergistic value for both our organizations."

## Outreach Categories — locked 2026-08-16

Brooke's direction: broaden scope, be creative, and organize every target
into one of three categories. Every entry in `local-landscape.md` and
`outreach-queue.md` must be tagged with one of these:

### Category A — Fund requests

Targets who are realistic sources of direct monetary donations, distinct
from the formal grant applications tracked in `marketing/funding/`.
Examples: civic/service clubs (Rotary, Lions, Kiwanis, Elks, VFW —
through their local posts, not just their national grant programs),
local banks and credit unions with community giving programs, individual
local businesses willing to make a direct cash gift, local churches or
faith organizations aligned in mission.

Any Category A message must follow the donation-language rule below.

### Category B — Service/resource exchange (mutual benefit)

Targets where Rebel Ranch offers something real in return, not a
donation ask. Examples: car dealerships (vehicle/equipment access for
sponsorship/visibility), powersports/ATV/UTV dealers, farm/feed/equipment
suppliers (Tractor Supply and similar — supplies for visibility and a
trusted-supplier feature), restaurants/eateries (food/catering for
cross-promotion), print/sign shops, local radio, veterinarians, other
local farms, and skilled freelancers (photography/video/web) who might
trade services for exposure.

### Category C — Reduced or free services Rebel Ranch provides

The reverse direction: what Rebel Ranch extends outward at reduced or no
cost, as relationship-building and mission work, not a funding ask at
all. Examples: Business Freedom's existing low-income service tier (see
`docs/entities_3p_vs_rebel_ranch` context — 3P donates services to fund
this), a free introductory consultation for a new or struggling local
business as a way to open a relationship, discounted or free Creation
Station sessions offered to homeschool co-ops or schools as community
goodwill and audience-building.

Category C targets are not typically a single outreach message to one
organization — they're a standing offer/program decision. Flag candidate
targets and structures here, but do not launch a Category C program
without Brooke's explicit go-ahead; this is a strategic decision, not
routine outreach.

## Agent-first workflow

The agent does all automatable outreach work. Brooke's role is limited to
approving, rejecting, or correcting drafts, and making decisions that
require her judgment (which relationships to prioritize, what to say when
a reply needs a personal touch).

The agent must:

- research and add real local businesses, chambers, organizations, and
  events to `local-landscape.md`
- create and revise outreach drafts in `outreach-queue.md`
- find entries marked `Status: Approved` in `outreach-queue.md`
- check `partner-rules.md` and `local-landscape.md` before drafting any
  outreach message
- check `outreach-archive.md` before contacting anyone, to avoid
  duplicate or repetitive outreach
- send outreach only after the specific message is `Approved` and sending
  has been separately authorized
- mark the original entry `Sent` after it goes out, and copy the final
  sent message and details into `outreach-archive.md`
- record any reply or follow-up need in `outreach-archive.md`

The agent must not tell Brooke to manually research businesses, copy
outreach details between files, or maintain a second tracker when the
agent can do that work.

`outreach-queue.md` is the only working queue. `outreach-archive.md` is
the only source of truth for outreach history. Do not create a duplicate
tracker.

## Agent behavior rules

The outreach agent must never:

- send a message without approval unless explicitly authorized
- mix funding/donation asks into a community outreach message without
  Brooke's request
- claim an existing relationship, agreement, or partnership that does not
  exist yet
- use "partner" in internal/operational language (see locked language
  rule above)
- contact the same business/org repeatedly without a real reason or
  reply to follow up on
- invent contact information — if a real contact method cannot be found,
  record that and leave the entry `Needs research`, do not guess

The agent must always:

- verify the target is actually in Rebel Ranch's service area before
  drafting
- check whether the business/org/event is already known in
  `local-landscape.md` or has prior contact in `outreach-archive.md`
- keep the message short, real, and specific to that business or event —
  not a generic template blasted to everyone
- record findings and sources for every new `local-landscape.md` entry

## Approval checklist

Before an outreach message is approved, it must pass:

- Does this sound like a real person from Rebel Ranch, not a form letter?
- Is it clear what we're offering, and is there no funding ask mixed in?
- Is the target actually local and actually a fit?
- Has this business/org/event already been contacted recently?
- Is "align" used instead of "partner" in any internal notes on this
  entry?

If the answer is no to any of those, it goes back to draft.
