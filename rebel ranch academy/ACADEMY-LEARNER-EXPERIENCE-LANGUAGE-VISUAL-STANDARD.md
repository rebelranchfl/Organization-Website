# Rebel Ranch Academy — Learner Experience, Language & Visual Standard

**Status:** Owner-approved governing standard  
**Date:** 2026-08-22  
**AI-Agent:** ChatGPT/GPT-5.6 Sol  
**Session:** RR Website — learner language, visuals, and signal tracking

## Purpose

Rebel Ranch Academy does the difficult research so the learner does not have to fight through difficult research language just to use what RRA learned.

The learner experience must be designed **for the learner's understanding, not for the system's vocabulary**.

Governing rule:

> **Use the simplest accurate word first. Technical language is optional depth, not the entry requirement.**

If two words communicate substantially the same meaning, use the word more people already understand. A learner should not need a dictionary to understand an Academy instruction when a simpler accurate word exists.

Examples:

- `agree` before `concur`;
- `germs` before `microorganisms`;
- `cloudiness` before `turbidity`;
- `what happens if this stops working?` before `failure consequence`;
- `my water test results` before `results interpreter`;
- `my water treatment plan` before `treatment job builder`.

Technical vocabulary is not banned. It is introduced when it adds value and is paired with plain language first.

## 1. Language Depth Hierarchy

Every substantial learner-facing concept should support four levels when useful.

### LEVEL 1 — PLAIN

The default entry layer. A broad audience should understand it immediately.

- shortest accurate wording;
- common words;
- direct question or answer;
- no unnecessary jargon;
- no academic/system labels when ordinary speech works.

Example:

> **What kind of water do you use now?**

### LEVEL 2 — EVERYDAY DETAIL

For the learner who wants a little more explanation without entering technical language.

Example:

> Where your water comes from changes what you should check first and what kind of backup plan will work for you.

### LEVEL 3 — TECHNICAL

Optional deeper understanding. Introduce the technical term beside the already-understood idea.

Examples:

- germs **(microorganisms)**;
- cloudiness **(turbidity)**;
- where treatment happens **(point of use / POU)**.

The technical label must not replace the plain explanation.

### LEVEL 4 — EVIDENCE / PROFESSIONAL DEPTH

Optional source, standard, engineering, calculation, evidence, operating limit, or professional detail.

Examples include:

- source citations;
- NSF/ANSI standards;
- EPA/CDC guidance;
- calculation method;
- engineering/system diagram;
- test range or performance specification;
- claim/evidence boundary.

This layer preserves RRA's research depth without putting that depth in the learner's way.

## 2. Learner Chooses Depth

RRA does not decide that every learner needs every layer.

The default experience starts at Plain. The learner may deliberately choose deeper layers through controls such as:

- `Tell me more`;
- `Why?`;
- `Show me the science`;
- `See the math`;
- `Technical details`;
- `Sources`.

A user who never opens deeper material should still receive a useful, accurate learning experience.

A user who wants advanced depth should be able to reach the evidence and technical system without leaving the product to reconstruct the research themselves.

## 3. Visual Learning Hierarchy

`Visual` means **anything that helps a learner see the idea**, not only charts, diagrams, flow maps, or system schematics.

RRA should normally teach in this order:

`RECOGNIZABLE IMAGE / SCENE → PLAIN MESSAGE → SIMPLE ACTION → PERSONAL RESULT → OPTIONAL DEEPER DIAGRAM → TECHNICAL EVIDENCE`

### Primary visual layer

Prefer familiar visual language ordinary people already use to navigate life:

- realistic or clear illustrated objects;
- before/after scenes;
- people using a tool;
- containers, faucets, wells, pumps, test kits, animals, food, money, documents, phones, etc.;
- signs, symbols, icons, simple comparisons;
- visual cause/effect;
- color only as a supporting cue, never the sole meaning.

Example for Water:

A cloudy glass and a clear glass beside a water test kit communicates the question before the learner reads the science.

### Secondary technical visual layer

Flowcharts, block diagrams, treatment trains, system maps, technical comparison tables, schematics, and engineering diagrams remain valuable. They belong where they deepen an already-understood idea or where the learner specifically chooses technical depth.

Do not make the technical diagram the learner's first obstacle when a familiar image can establish the idea faster.

## 4. Make Them See It Before Asking Them to Study It

RRA's job is knowledge compression.

A strong learner-facing page should answer, as quickly as possible:

1. What am I looking at?
2. Why should I care?
3. What do I do?
4. What does my answer mean?
5. What is my next step?
6. Where can I go deeper if I want to?

The learner should not have to infer the purpose of a chart, tool, worksheet, or activity from a system label.

## 5. Human Naming Rule

Learner-facing tool names should describe the user's job or result in ordinary language.

Prefer:

- **Tell Us About Your Water**
- **Build My Water Backup Plan**
- **My Water Test Results**
- **My Water Treatment Plan**
- **What Happens If This Stops Working?**
- **My Water Plan**

over internal/system labels such as:

- Water Profile Planner;
- Results Interpreter;
- Treatment Job Builder;
- Failure Consequence;
- Planning Record.

Internal technical names may remain in code, schemas, file names, research records, or professional-depth layers. They should not automatically become customer-facing labels.

## 6. Interactive Product Rule

When a product is interactive, Final Product Review must show the product in context as the learner will experience it.

The owner should be able to:

- open the product at the beginning;
- move page-by-page or step-by-step;
- see visuals in the exact place they support the lesson;
- interact with questions and tools;
- see answers update personalized results;
- jump to activities/tools;
- open optional deeper technical layers;
- review mobile/print behavior where relevant.

A collection of disconnected visual files is not a complete owner-review substitute for an integrated product experience.

## 7. Personal Result / Living Record Rule

When the learner supplies answers, the product should use those answers where useful instead of asking the learner to manually recreate information elsewhere.

For example, Water answers may build **My Water Plan**:

- water source;
- intended use;
- known concern;
- important unknowns;
- testing status;
- treatment needs supported by the evidence;
- backup/storage need;
- maintenance/verification next step.

If a learner changes an earlier answer, dependent personalized results should update where technically practical.

A static template labeled `Planning Record` must not imply that the system is tracking learner answers if it is not.

## 8. Learner Signal Rule

Structured learner input and product behavior are valuable Academy intelligence.

Examples:

- water source selected;
- stated goal/concern;
- result category;
- tool started/completed;
- step where users stop;
- plain/detail/technical depth selected;
- `Show me the science` opened;
- format preference;
- opportunity branch triggered;
- next-learning choice.

These signals feed Audience + Conversion Intelligence and Opportunity Intelligence so RRA can learn what people actually need.

Example:

If most real users report city water and very few report private wells, that is evidence to reconsider the relative amount of future well-specific material. If automatic animal watering repeatedly appears, that is evidence of real interest in that branch.

## 9. Data-Minimization Rule

Product intelligence does not require unnecessary personal identity.

By default, learner-signal tracking should collect **structured learning/product signals**, not names, emails, addresses, health details, open-ended personal histories, or other identifying information.

- use a random product/session key when session grouping is useful;
- associate an account ID only when the user is signed in and the feature legitimately requires it;
- do not store IP address or browser fingerprint as Academy learner intelligence;
- do not place names/emails into signal values;
- prefer controlled categories over unrestricted free text;
- preserve parent/guardian and minor privacy boundaries;
- clearly disclose analytics/data collection when a public experience begins collecting it.

Public release and privacy copy remain separate owner gates.

## 10. Signal-to-Intelligence Loop

`LEARNER ANSWER / BEHAVIOR → STRUCTURED SIGNAL → AGGREGATED PATTERN → AUDIENCE INTELLIGENCE → OPPORTUNITY INTELLIGENCE → PRODUCT DECISION → BETTER LEARNER EXPERIENCE`

Behavior data is evidence of interest and use. It is not proof that every user wants the same thing and must not be used to justify dark patterns.

## 11. Required Product QA Questions

Before Product Review and again before Final Product Review, verify:

- Is every learner-facing term the simplest accurate term available?
- Is a harder synonym being used merely because it sounds professional?
- Can a person understand the main point without opening technical depth?
- Does the primary visual use familiar images/scenes where they communicate better than a diagram?
- Are technical diagrams available for learners who want them?
- Does each interactive tool tell the user what to do and what they will get?
- Are personalized results clearly distinguished from static examples/templates?
- Does the complete product preview show the real learning sequence in context?
- Are structured learner signals identified for useful interactive inputs/events?
- Is the tracking privacy-minimized and tied to a legitimate product-intelligence purpose?

Failure of these checks means the product is not ready for Final Product Review even if the underlying research is complete.

## 12. Water Correction — First Application

`RRA-2026-0001` is the first product to apply this standard.

The existing research, evidence, safety boundaries, diagrams, and system logic remain valuable and should be preserved.

The learner-facing experience must be revised so that:

- the integrated product is reviewable from beginning to end;
- ordinary language is the default;
- familiar image-based teaching is the primary visual layer where appropriate;
- technical diagrams become optional depth instead of the main teaching surface;
- Water Profile / Results Interpreter / Treatment Job Builder / Planning Record labels are replaced in the learner experience with human task/result language;
- learner answers build and update a clear **My Water Plan** where appropriate;
- interactive inputs/events are instrumented as learner signals;
- deeper science, engineering, standards, and evidence remain available on demand;
- existing evidence/safety boundaries are not weakened by simplification.

Simplification means removing unnecessary difficulty, not removing truth.
